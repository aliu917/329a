- We have a regular hexagon \(ABCDEF\).

- We need to find the ratio of the area of the smaller hexagon to the area of the original hexagon and express it as \(\frac{m}{n}\).

Step 1: Understand the geometric relationship.
- In a regular hexagon, the distance from the center to a midpoint of a side is half the side length.
- The lines \(AH, BI, CJ, DK, EL, FG\) are all equal and pass through the center of the hexagon.
- The smaller hexagon formed is similar to the original hexagon.

- Consider the distance from the center of the hexagon to the side of the smaller hexagon.
- This distance is \(\frac{1}{3}\) of the distance from the center to the side of the original hexagon.
- Therefore, the side length of the smaller hexagon is \(\frac{1}{2}\) of the side length of the original hexagon (since the distance from the center to a vertex of the smaller hexagon is the same as the side length of the smaller hexagon).

Step 3: Calculate the ratio of the areas.
- The area of a regular hexagon is proportional to the square of its side length.
- If the side length of the smaller hexagon is \(\frac{1}{2}\) of the side length of the original hexagon, then the area of the smaller hexagon is \(\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}\) of the area of the original hexagon.

Step 4: Express the ratio as a fraction and find \(m + n\).
- The ratio of the area of the smaller hexagon to the area of the original hexagon is \(\frac{1}{4}\).
- Therefore, \(m = 1\) and \(n = 4\).
- The sum \(m + n = 1 + 4 = 5\).

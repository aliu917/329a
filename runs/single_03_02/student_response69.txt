To determine how many elements in the set \( S = \{5^k | k \in \mathbb{Z}, 0 \le k \le 2004 \} \) begin with the digit 1, we need to analyze the leading digit of the numbers in the form \( 5^k \).

First, we express \( 5^k \) in scientific notation:
\[ 5^k = 10^{\log_{10}(5^k)} = 10^{k \log_{10} 5} \]
Let \( \log_{10} 5 \approx 0.69897 \). Then,
\[ 5^k = 10^{k \cdot 0.69897} = 10^{n + f} \]
where \( n \) is an integer and \( f \) is the fractional part of \( k \cdot 0.69897 \). This can be written as:
\[ 5^k = 10^n \cdot 10^f \]
The leading digit of \( 5^k \) is the same as the leading digit of \( 10^f \), which is a number between 1 and 10. Therefore, we need to find when \( 10^f \) has a leading digit of 1, which means \( 1 \leq 10^f < 2 \). This is equivalent to:
\[ 0 \leq f < \log_{10} 2 \]
where \( \log_{10} 2 \approx 0.30103 \).

The fractional part \( f \) of \( k \cdot 0.69897 \) is uniformly distributed over the interval \([0, 1)\) as \( k \) varies. Therefore, the proportion of \( k \) for which \( 0 \leq f < \log_{10} 2 \) is \( \log_{10} 2 \approx 0.30103 \).

However, we need to count the exact number of such \( k \) from 0 to 2004. The number of such \( k \) is:
\[ \lfloor 2005 \cdot \log_{10} 2 \rfloor = \lfloor 2005 \cdot 0.30103 \rfloor = \lfloor 603.56515 \rfloor = 603 \]

Thus, the number of elements in \( S \) that begin with the digit 1 is:
\[ \boxed{603} \]

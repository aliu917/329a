Attempt: To determine the least number of workers the company has to hire to make a profit in an 8-hour workday, we need to calculate the total costs and the total revenue generated by the workers.

First, let's calculate the daily maintenance fees:
\[ \text{Daily maintenance fees} = \$500 \]

Next, let's calculate the daily cost for each worker. Each worker is paid \$15 per hour and works 8 hours a day:
\[ \text{Daily cost per worker} = 15 \times 8 = \$120 \]

Now, let's determine the revenue generated by each worker. Each worker makes 5 widgets per hour and works 8 hours a day, so each worker makes:
\[ \text{Widgets per worker per day} = 5 \times 8 = 40 \]

Each widget is sold for \$3.10, so the revenue per worker per day is:
\[ \text{Revenue per worker per day} = 40 \times 3.10 = \$124 \]

Let \( n \) be the number of workers. The total daily cost for \( n \) workers is:
\[ \text{Total daily cost for } n \text{ workers} = 500 + 120n \]

The total daily revenue from \( n \) workers is:
\[ \text{Total daily revenue for } n \text{ workers} = 124n \]

To make a profit, the total daily revenue must be greater than the total daily cost:
\[ 124n > 500 + 120n \]

Subtract \( 120n \) from both sides of the inequality:
\[ 4n > 500 \]

Divide both sides by 4:
\[ n > 125 \]

Since \( n \) must be a whole number, the smallest integer greater than 125 is 126. Therefore, the least number of workers the company has to hire to make a profit in an 8-hour workday is:
\[ \boxed{126} \]

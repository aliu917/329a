7x &\equiv 21 \pmod{14} \\

Attempt: To solve the system of congruences, we will address each congruence one by one and then use the Chinese Remainder Theorem to find the least positive four-digit solution.

### Step 1: Solve the first congruence
\[ 7x \equiv 21 \pmod{14} \]

First, simplify the congruence:
\[ 7x \equiv 21 \pmod{14} \]
\[ 7x \equiv 7 \cdot 3 \pmod{14} \]
\[ 7x \equiv 7 \cdot 3 \pmod{14} \]
\[ 7x \equiv 7 \cdot 3 \pmod{14} \]
\[ 7(x - 3) \equiv 0 \pmod{14} \]

Since \(7\) is a factor of \(14\), we can divide both sides by \(7\):
\[ x - 3 \equiv 0 \pmod{2} \]
\[ x \equiv 3 \pmod{2} \]
\[ x \equiv 1 \pmod{2} \]

This means \(x\) is odd.

### Step 2: Solve the second congruence
\[ 2x + 13 \equiv 16 \pmod{9} \]

\[ 2x + 13 \equiv 16 \pmod{9} \]
\[ 2x + 4 \equiv 7 \pmod{9} \]  (since \(13 \equiv 4 \pmod{9}\) and \(16 \equiv 7 \pmod{9}\))
\[ 2x \equiv 3 \pmod{9} \]

\[ 2y \equiv 1 \pmod{9} \]

By testing values, we find:

So, the inverse of \(2\) modulo \(9\) is \(5\).

Multiply both sides of \(2x \equiv 3 \pmod{9}\) by \(5\):

So, the inverse of \(3\) modulo \(25\) is \(17\).

Multiply both sides of \(3x \equiv 1 \pmod{25}\) by \(17\):
\[ x \equiv 17 \pmod{25} \]

### Step 4: Combine the solutions using the Chinese Remainder Theorem

\[ 9y \equiv 1 \pmod{25} \]

#### Solve \(x \equiv 42 \pmod{225}\) and \(x \equiv 1 \pmod{2}\)

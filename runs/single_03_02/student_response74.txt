To determine the smallest integer \( n \), greater than 1, such that \( n^{-1} \pmod{1050} \) is defined, we need to find the smallest \( n > 1 \) that is coprime to 1050. This means \( \gcd(n, 1050) = 1 \).

First, we factorize 1050:
\[
1050 = 2 \times 3 \times 5^2 \times 7
\]
For \( n \) to be coprime to 1050, \( n \) must not share any prime factors with 1050. Therefore, \( n \) should not be divisible by 2, 3, 5, or 7.

We start checking the smallest integers greater than 1 to find one that is coprime to 1050:

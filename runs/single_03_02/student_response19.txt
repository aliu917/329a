To find the sum of the squares of the roots of the quadratic equation \(3x^2 + 4x - 9 = 0\), we will follow these steps:

1. **Identify the coefficients:**
   The quadratic equation is in the form \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), where \(a = 3\), \(b = 4\), and \(c = -9\).

2. **Use the sum and product of the roots:**
   For a quadratic equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), the sum of the roots (\(\alpha + \beta\)) is given by:
   \[
   \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}
   \]
   The product of the roots (\(\alpha \beta\)) is given by:
   \[
   \alpha \beta = \frac{c}{a}
   \]

3. **Calculate the sum of the roots:**
   \[
   \alpha + \beta = -\frac{4}{3}
   \]

4. **Calculate the product of the roots:**
   \[
   \alpha \beta = \frac{-9}{3} = -3
   \]

5. **Use the identity for the sum of the squares of the roots:**
   The sum of the squares of the roots can be expressed using the identity:
   \[
   \alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha \beta
   \]

6. **Substitute the values of \(\alpha + \beta\) and \(\alpha \beta\) into the identity:**
   \[
   \alpha^2 + \beta^2 = \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)^2 - 2(-3)
   \]

7. **Simplify the expression:**
   \[
   \alpha^2 + \beta^2 = \left(\frac{16}{9}\right) + 6
   \]

8. **Convert 6 to a fraction with a common denominator:**
   \[
   6 = \frac{54}{9}
   \]

9. **Add the fractions:**
   \[
   \alpha^2 + \beta^2 = \frac{16}{9} + \frac{54}{9} = \frac{70}{9}
   \]

10. **Express the final answer in the required format:**
   \[
   \boxed{\frac{70}{9}}
   \]

Thus, the sum of the squares of the roots of the quadratic equation \(3x^2 + 4x - 9 = 0\) is \(\boxed{\frac{70}{9}}\).

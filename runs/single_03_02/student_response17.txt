Given the equation \( |x-2| = p \), we need to determine \( x - p \) in terms of \( p \) under the condition \( x < 2 \).

Step 1: Understand the absolute value equation.
The absolute value equation \( |x-2| = p \) can be broken down into two cases:
1. \( x - 2 = p \)
2. \( x - 2 = -p \)

Step 2: Apply the condition \( x < 2 \).
Since \( x < 2 \), the first case \( x - 2 = p \) would imply \( x = p + 2 \), which contradicts \( x < 2 \) because \( p + 2 > 2 \) for any positive \( p \). Therefore, the first case is not valid under the given condition.

Step 3: Use the second case.
The second case \( x - 2 = -p \) implies \( x = 2 - p \). This satisfies the condition \( x < 2 \) because \( 2 - p < 2 \) for any positive \( p \).

Step 4: Substitute \( x = 2 - p \) into \( x - p \).
Now we need to find \( x - p \):
\[ x - p = (2 - p) - p = 2 - 2p \]

Thus, the final answer is:
\[
\boxed{2 - 2p}

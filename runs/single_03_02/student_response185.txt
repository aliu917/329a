To find the area of the union of the regions enclosed by the square and the circle, we need to follow these steps:

1. **Calculate the area of the square:**
   The side length of the square is 10. Therefore, the area of the square is:
   \[
   \text{Area of the square} = 10 \times 10 = 100
   \]

2. **Calculate the area of the circle:**
   The radius of the circle is 10. Therefore, the area of the circle is:
   \[
   \text{Area of the circle} = \pi \times 10^2 = 100\pi
   \]

3. **Determine the overlap between the square and the circle:**
   Since the circle is centered at one of the vertices of the square and has a radius equal to the side length of the square, the circle will overlap the square in a quarter-circle shape. The area of this quarter-circle is:
   \[
   \text{Area of the quarter-circle} = \frac{1}{4} \times 100\pi = 25\pi
   \]

4. **Calculate the area of the union of the square and the circle:**
   The area of the union is the sum of the areas of the square and the circle minus the area of the overlap (quarter-circle). Therefore, the area of the union is:
   \[
   \text{Area of the union} = \text{Area of the square} + \text{Area of the circle} - \text{Area of the quarter-circle} = 100 + 100\pi - 25\pi = 100 + 75\pi
   \]

Thus, the area of the union of the regions enclosed by the square and the circle is:
\[
\boxed{100 + 75\pi}

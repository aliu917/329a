To solve this problem, we need to calculate the energy stored in the initial configuration and the new configuration, and then find the difference between these two energies.

### Step 1: Initial Configuration
Given:
- The charges are identical.
- They form an equilateral triangle.
- The energy stored in this configuration is 15 Joules.

Let each charge be \( q \) and the side length of the equilateral triangle be \( d \).

For an equilateral triangle, there are three pairs of charges:
- Each pair of charges experiences an energy given by the formula:
  \[
  U = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r}
  \]
  where \( k \) is Coulomb's constant, \( q_1 \) and \( q_2 \) are the charges, and \( r \) is the distance between them.

Since the charges are identical and the distance between each pair is \( d \):
\[
U_{\text{pair}} = k \frac{q^2}{d}
\]

There are three such pairs in the triangle:
\[
U_{\text{total}} = 3 \times k \frac{q^2}{d}
\]

Given that the total energy is 15 Joules:
\[
3 \times k \frac{q^2}{d} = 15
\]
\[
k \frac{q^2}{d} = 5 \quad \text{(Equation 1)}
\]

### Step 2: New Configuration
Now, one of the charges is moved to the midpoint of the opposite side. Let’s denote the new positions as follows:
- \( q \) at \( A \)
- \( q \) at \( B \)
- \( q \) at \( M \) (midpoint of side \( BC \))

#### Energy Calculation for New Configuration
1. **Pair \( (A, B) \)**:
   - Distance \( AB = d \)
   \[
   U_{AB} = k \frac{q^2}{d}
   \]

2. **Pair \( (A, M) \)**:
   - Distance \( AM \) is half the side length of the triangle:
   \[
   AM = \frac{d}{2}
   \]
   \[
   U_{AM} = k \frac{q^2}{\frac{d}{2}} = 2k \frac{q^2}{d}
   \]

3. **Pair \( (B, M) \)**:
   - Distance \( BM \) is also half the side length of the triangle:
   \[
   BM = \frac{d}{2}
   \]
   \[
   U_{BM} = k \frac{q^2}{\frac{d}{2}} = 2k \frac{q^2}{d}
   \]

#### Total Energy in New Configuration
\[
U_{\text{new}} = U_{AB} + U_{AM} + U_{BM}
\]
\[
U_{\text{new}} = k \frac{q^2}{d} + 2k \frac{q^2}{d} + 2k \frac{q^2}{d}
\]
\[
U_{\text{new}} = k \frac{q^2}{d} + 4k \frac{q^2}{d}
\]
\[
U_{\text{new}} = 5k \frac{q^2}{d}
\]

Using Equation 1:
\[
U_{\text{new}} = 5 \times 5 = 25 \quad \text{Joules}
\]

### Step 3: Energy Difference
The initial energy was 15 Joules. The new energy is 25 Joules. The difference in energy is:
\[
25 - 15 = 10 \quad \text{Joules}
\]

Thus, the additional energy stored is:
\[
\boxed{10}
\]

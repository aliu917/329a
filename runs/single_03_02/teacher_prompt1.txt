You are given a reasoning attempt for answering the following question: The energy stored by any pair of positive charges is inversely proportional to the distance between them, and directly proportional to their charges. Three identical point charges start at the vertices of an equilateral triangle, and this configuration stores 15 Joules of energy. How much more energy, in Joules, would be stored if one of these charges was moved to the midpoint of the opposite side?

Attempt: Let's start by understanding the initial configuration and the energy stored in it.

1. **Initial Configuration:**
   - Three identical point charges \( q \) are at the vertices of an equilateral triangle with side length \( a \).
   - Energy stored between any two charges is given by the formula:
     \[
     U = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r}
     \]
     where \( k \) is Coulomb's constant, \( q_1 \) and \( q_2 \) are the charges, and \( r \) is the distance between them.

2. **Energy in Initial Configuration:**
   - There are three pairs of charges, each at a distance \( a \).
   - The energy stored between each pair is:
     \[
     U_{\text{pair}} = k \frac{q^2}{a}
     \]
   - Total energy \( U_{\text{initial}} \) for three pairs is:
     \[
     U_{\text{initial}} = 3 \cdot k \frac{q^2}{a}
     \]
   - Given that this total energy is 15 Joules:
     \[
     3 \cdot k \frac{q^2}{a} = 15
     \]
     Simplifying, we get:
     \[
     k \frac{q^2}{a} = 5
     \]

3. **New Configuration:**
   - One charge is moved to the midpoint of the opposite side.
   - Let the new position be such that the charge is now at the midpoint of the side opposite to it.
   - The distance from the midpoint to each of the other two charges is \( \frac{a}{2} \).
   - The energy stored between the charge at the midpoint and each of the other two charges is:
     \[
     U_{\text{new pair}} = k \frac{q^2}{\frac{a}{2}} = 2k \frac{q^2}{a}
     \]
   - The distance between the two original charges remains \( a \), so the energy stored between these two charges is:
     \[
     U_{\text{remaining pair}} = k \frac{q^2}{a}
     \]
   - Total energy \( U_{\text{new}} \) in the new configuration is:
     \[
     U_{\text{new}} = 2k \frac{q^2}{a} + k \frac{q^2}{a} = 3k \frac{q^2}{a}
     \]

4. **Comparing Energies:**
   - From the initial configuration, we know \( k \frac{q^2}{a} = 5 \).
   - Therefore, the total energy in the new configuration is:
     \[
     U_{\text{new}} = 3 \cdot 5 = 15 \text{ Joules}
     \]

5. **Difference in Energy:**
   - Since the total energy in the new configuration is the same as in the initial configuration:
     \[
     \Delta U = U_{\text{new}} - U_{\text{initial}} = 15 - 15 = 0 \text{ Joules}
     \]

Thus, the additional energy stored in the new configuration is \(\boxed{0}\).

Correct solution: Let the side length of the equilateral triangle be $d$. $15/3=5$ Joules of energy are stored when two charges are at distance $d$, so $2\cdot5=10$ Joules are stored when they are at distance $d/2$, because energy is inversely proportional to distance. This means that in the second configuration, the pair $(A,C)$ and $(B,C)$ store 10 Joules each, and since $(A,B)$ still stores 5 Joules, the final configuration stores a total of $10+10+5=25$ Joules, which is $25-15=\boxed{10}$ Joules more than the initial configuration.

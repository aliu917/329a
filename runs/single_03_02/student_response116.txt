To solve the functional equation \( f(a + b) + f(ab) = f(a) f(b) + 1 \) for all integers \( a \) and \( b \), we will follow a step-by-step approach.

### Step 1: Analyze the functional equation with specific values

First, let's substitute \( a = 0 \) and \( b = 0 \):
\[
f(0 + 0) + f(0 \cdot 0) = f(0) f(0) + 1 \implies 2f(0) = f(0)^2 + 1
\]
Rearrange this equation:
\[
f(0)^2 - 2f(0) + 1 = 0 \implies (f(0) - 1)^2 = 0 \implies f(0) = 1
\]

### Step 2: Substitute \( b = 1 \)

Next, let \( b = 1 \):
\[
f(a + 1) + f(a) = f(a) f(1) + 1
\]
Rearrange this equation:
\[
f(a + 1) = f(a) f(1) + 1 - f(a) \implies f(a + 1) = f(a)(f(1) - 1) + 1
\]

### Step 3: Determine \( f(1) \)

To find \( f(1) \), substitute \( a = 1 \) and \( b = -1 \) into the original equation:
\[
f(1 - 1) + f(1 \cdot -1) = f(1) f(-1) + 1 \implies f(0) + f(-1) = f(1) f(-1) + 1
\]
Since \( f(0) = 1 \):
\[
1 + f(-1) = f(1) f(-1) + 1 \implies f(-1) = f(1) f(-1)
\]
If \( f(-1) \neq 0 \), we can divide both sides by \( f(-1) \):
\[
1 = f(1)
\]
Thus, \( f(1) = 1 \).

### Step 4: Generalize the function

With \( f(1) = 1 \), substitute back into the recurrence relation:
\[
f(a + 1) = f(a)(1 - 1) + 1 \implies f(a + 1) = 1
\]
This implies that \( f(a) = 1 \) for all \( a \).

### Step 5: Verify the solution

Finally, verify that \( f(n) = 1 \) for all integers \( n \) satisfies the original equation:
\[
f(a + b) + f(ab) = 1 + 1 = 2
\]
\[
f(a) f(b) + 1 = 1 \cdot 1 + 1 = 2
\]
Both sides are equal, confirming that \( f(n) = 1 \) is indeed a solution.

### Conclusion

The only function that satisfies the given functional equation is \( f(n) = 1 \) for all integers \( n \). Therefore, there is exactly one such function.

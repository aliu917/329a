Given the equation \( |x-2| = p \) and the condition \( x < 2 \), we need to determine the value of \( x - p \) in terms of \( p \).

1. **Understanding the Absolute Value**:
   The absolute value equation \( |x-2| = p \) can be broken down into two cases:
   - \( x - 2 = p \)
   - \( x - 2 = -p \)

2. **Applying the Condition \( x < 2 \)**:
   - For \( x - 2 = p \), solving for \( x \) gives \( x = p + 2 \). However, this solution conflicts with the condition \( x < 2 \) because \( p + 2 > 2 \) for any \( p > 0 \).
   - For \( x - 2 = -p \), solving for \( x \) gives \( x = 2 - p \). This solution satisfies the condition \( x < 2 \) because \( 2 - p < 2 \) for any \( p > 0 \).

3. **Substituting the Valid Solution**:
   The valid solution is \( x = 2 - p \).

4. **Finding \( x - p \)**:
   Substitute \( x = 2 - p \) into the expression \( x - p \):
   \[
   x - p = (2 - p) - p = 2 - 2p
   \]

Therefore, the value of \( x - p \) in terms of \( p \) is \(\boxed{2 - 2p}\).

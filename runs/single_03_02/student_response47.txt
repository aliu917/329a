To solve the problem, we will use the Power of a Point theorem. The theorem states that for a point \(P\) outside a circle, the power of the point is the same whether we consider a tangent or a secant. Specifically, if \(PT\) is a tangent and \(PA\) and \(PB\) are segments of a secant, then \(PT^2 = PA \cdot PB\).

Given:
- \(PA = 3\)
- \(PT = AB - PA\)
- Let \(PB = x\)

First, we need to express \(AB\) in terms of \(PA\) and \(PB\):
\[AB = PA + PB - PA = PB - PA = x - 3\]

Since \(PT = AB - PA\), we substitute \(AB\) and \(PA\):
\[PT = (x - 3) - 3 = x - 6\]

Now, we apply the Power of a Point theorem:
\[PT^2 = PA \cdot PB\]
\[(x - 6)^2 = 3 \cdot x\]

Expanding and simplifying the equation:
\[x^2 - 12x + 36 = 3x\]
\[x^2 - 15x + 36 = 0\]

We solve this quadratic equation using the quadratic formula \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\), where \(a = 1\), \(b = -15\), and \(c = 36\):
\[x = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{15^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 36}}{2 \cdot 1}\]
\[x = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{225 - 144}}{2}\]
\[x = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{81}}{2}\]
\[x = \frac{15 \pm 9}{2}\]

This gives us two potential solutions:
\[x = \frac{15 + 9}{2} = 12\]
\[x = \frac{15 - 9}{2} = 3\]

Since \(PA < PB\), we discard \(x = 3\) and keep \(x = 12\).

Thus, the value of \(PB\) is \(\boxed{12}\).

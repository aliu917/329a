To solve the given system of congruences, we will address each congruence step by step and then use the Chinese Remainder Theorem to find the least positive four-digit solution.

The system of congruences is:
\[
7x \equiv 21 \pmod{14}
\]
\[
2x + 13 \equiv 16 \pmod{9}
\]
\[
-2x + 1 \equiv x \pmod{25}
\]

### Step 1: Simplify the first congruence
The first congruence is:
\[
7x \equiv 21 \pmod{14}
\]
We can simplify this by dividing both sides by 7:
\[
x \equiv 3 \pmod{2}
\]
This simplifies to:
\[
x \equiv 1 \pmod{2}
\]
So, \(x\) is odd.

### Step 2: Simplify the second congruence
The second congruence is:
\[
2x + 13 \equiv 16 \pmod{9}
\]
Subtract 13 from both sides:
\[
2x \equiv 3 \pmod{9}
\]
To solve for \(x\), we need the multiplicative inverse of 2 modulo 9. The inverse \(y\) satisfies:
\[
2y \equiv 1 \pmod{9}
\]
By testing values, we find:
\[
2 \times 5 = 10 \equiv 1 \pmod{9}
\]
Thus, the inverse is 5. Multiply both sides of \(2x \equiv 3 \pmod{9}\) by 5:
\[
x \equiv 15 \pmod{9}
\]
Simplify 15 modulo 9:
\[
x \equiv 6 \pmod{9}
\]

### Step 3: Simplify the third congruence
The third congruence is:
\[
-2x + 1 \equiv x \pmod{25}
\]
Subtract \(x\) from both sides:
\[
-3x + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{25}
\]
Subtract 1 from both sides:
\[
-3x \equiv -1 \pmod{25}
\]
Multiply both sides by \(-1\):
\[
3x \equiv 1 \pmod{25}
\]
To solve for \(x\), we need the multiplicative inverse of 3 modulo 25. The inverse \(y\) satisfies:
\[
3y \equiv 1 \pmod{25}
\]
By testing values, we find:
\[
3 \times 17 = 51 \equiv 1 \pmod{25}
\]
Thus, the inverse is 17. Multiply both sides of \(3x \equiv 1 \pmod{25}\) by 17:
\[
x \equiv 17 \pmod{25}
\]

### Step 4: Combine the congruences using the Chinese Remainder Theorem
We have the system:
\[
x \equiv 1 \pmod{2}
\]
\[
x \equiv 6 \pmod{9}
\]
\[
x \equiv 17 \pmod{25}
\]

First, solve the system:
\[
x \equiv 6 \pmod{9}
\]
\[
x \equiv 17 \pmod{25}
\]

Let \(x = 9k + 6\). Substitute into the second congruence:
\[
9k + 6 \equiv 17 \pmod{25}
\]
Subtract 6 from both sides:
\[
9k \equiv 11 \pmod{25}
\]
Find the inverse of 9 modulo 25. The inverse \(y\) satisfies:
\[
9y \equiv 1 \pmod{25}
\]
By testing values, we find:
\[
9 \times 14 = 126 \equiv 1 \pmod{25}
\]
Thus, the inverse is 14. Multiply both sides of \(9k \equiv 11 \pmod{25}\) by 14:
\[
k \equiv 154 \pmod{25}
\]
Simplify 154 modulo 25:
\[
k \equiv 4 \pmod{25}
\]
So, \(k = 25m + 4\) for some integer \(m\). Substitute back:
\[
x = 9(25m + 4) + 6 = 225m + 36 + 6 = 225m + 42
\]
Thus:
\[
x \equiv 42 \pmod{225}
\]

Now, solve the system:
\[
x \equiv 42 \pmod{225}
\]
\[
x \equiv 1 \pmod{2}
\]

Let \(x = 225n + 42\). Substitute into the second congruence:
\[
225n + 42 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}
\]
Since \(225 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}\) and \(42 \equiv 0 \pmod{2}\):
\[
n \equiv 1 \pmod{2}
\]
So, \(n = 2p + 1\) for some integer \(p\). Substitute back:
\[
x = 225(2p + 1) + 42 = 450p + 225 + 42 = 450p + 267
\]
Thus:
\[
x \equiv 267 \pmod{450}
\]

### Step 5: Find the least positive four-digit solution
We need \(x\) such that:
\[
x = 450k + 267 \geq 1000
\]
Solve for \(k\):
\[
450k + 267 \geq 1000
\]
\[
450k \geq 733
\]
\[
k \geq \frac{733}{450} \approx 1.628
\]
The smallest integer \(k\) is 2. Substitute \(k = 2\):
\[
x = 450 \times 2 + 267 = 900 + 267 = 1167
\]

Thus, the least positive four-digit solution is:
\[
\boxed{1167}
\]

The ratio of the heights of the smaller cone to the original cone is \( \frac{h}{4} \), and the ratio of the radii is also \( \frac{r}{3} \). Therefore, \( r = \frac{3h}{4} \).

### Step 1: Calculate the volume of the smaller cone \( C \)
The volume \( V_C \) of the smaller cone is given by:
\[
V_C = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3} \pi \left( \frac{3h}{4} \right)^2 h = \frac{1}{3} \pi \frac{9h^2}{16} h = \frac{3\pi h^3}{16}
\]

The volume \( V_F \) of the frustum is the volume of the original cone minus the volume of the smaller cone:
\[
V_F = \frac{1}{3} \pi (3)^2 (4) - \frac{3\pi h^3}{16} = 12\pi - \frac{3\pi h^3}{16} = \frac{192\pi - 3\pi h^3}{16} = \frac{3\pi (64 - h^3)}{16}
\]

Since the ratio of the volumes is \( k \):
\[
\frac{V_C}{V_F} = k \implies \frac{\frac{3\pi h^3}{16}}{\frac{3\pi (64 - h^3)}{16}} = k \implies \frac{h^3}{64 - h^3} = k
\]

### Step 2: Calculate the surface area of the smaller cone \( C \)
The lateral surface area \( A_L(C) \) of the smaller cone is:
\[
A_L(C) = \pi r l = \pi \frac{3h}{4} \sqrt{\left(\frac{3h}{4}\right)^2 + h^2} = \pi \frac{3h}{4} \sqrt{\frac{9h^2}{16} + h^2} = \pi \frac{3h}{4} \sqrt{\frac{25h^2}{16}} = \pi \frac{3h}{4} \cdot \frac{5h}{4} = \frac{15\pi h^2}{16}

The base area \( A_B(C) \) of the smaller cone is:
\[
A_B(C) = \pi r^2 = \pi \left( \frac{3h}{4} \right)^2 = \pi \frac{9h^2}{16}
\]

The total surface area \( A(C) \) of the smaller cone is:
\[
A(C) = A_L(C) + A_B(C) = \frac{15\pi h^2}{16} + \frac{9\pi h^2}{16} = \frac{24\pi h^2}{16} = \frac{3\pi h^2}{2}

### Step 3: Calculate the surface area of the frustum \( F \)
The lateral surface area \( A_L(F) \) of the frustum is:
\[
A_L(F) = \pi (R + r) l_F = \pi \left( 3 + \frac{3h}{4} \right) \sqrt{4^2 + \left(4 - h\right)^2} = \pi \left( 3 + \frac{3h}{4} \right) \sqrt{16 + (4 - h)^2} = \pi \left( 3 + \frac{3h}{4} \right) \sqrt{16 + 16 - 8h + h^2} = \pi \left( 3 + \frac{3h}{4} \right) \sqrt{32 - 8h + h^2}

The base area \( A_B(F) \) of the frustum is:
\[
A_B(F) = \pi R^2 = \pi (3)^2 = 9\pi

The top area \( A_T(F) \) of the frustum is:
\[
A_T(F) = \pi r^2 = \pi \left( \frac{3h}{4} \right)^2 = \pi \frac{9h^2}{16}

The total surface area \( A(F) \) of the frustum is:
\[
A(F) = A_L(F) + A_B(F) + A_T(F) = \pi \left( 3 + \frac{3h}{4} \right) \sqrt{32 - 8h + h^2} + 9\pi + \pi \frac{9h^2}{16}

Since the ratio of the surface areas is \( k \):
\[
\frac{A(C)}{A(F)} = k \implies \frac{\frac{3\pi h^2}{2}}{\pi \left( 3 + \frac{3h}{4} \right) \sqrt{32 - 8h + h^2} + 9\pi + \pi \frac{9h^2}{16}} = k
\]

Given that the ratio of the volumes and the ratio of the surface areas are both \( k \), we can equate the two expressions for \( k \):
\[
\frac{h^3}{64 - h^3} = \frac{\frac{3\pi h^2}{2}}{\pi \left( 3 + \frac{3h}{4} \right) \sqrt{32 - 8h + h^2} + 9\pi + \pi \frac{9h^2}{16}}

\[
f(0) = 3(0)^3 + (0)^2 + 4(0) + 57 = 57.
\]
Thus, \(3a^3 + a^2 + 4a + 57 \equiv 57 \pmod{a}\). This means that the GCD of \(3a^3 + a^2 + 4a + 57\) and \(a\) is the same as the GCD of \(a\) and 57. Since \(a\) is a multiple of 456, and 456 and 57 are factors of \(a\), we need to find the GCD of 456 and 57.

We use the Euclidean algorithm to find \(\gcd(456, 57)\):
\[
456 \div 57 = 8 \quad \text{with a remainder of} \quad 456 - 8 \cdot 57 = 456 - 456 = 0.
\]
Since the remainder is 0, the GCD is the last non-zero remainder, which is 57. Therefore, \(\gcd(456, 57) = 57\).

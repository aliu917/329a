To find the largest integer that always divides the expression \((10x+2)(10x+6)(5x+5)\) where \(x\) is an odd number, we will analyze the expression step by step.

First, let's rewrite the expression:
\[
(10x+2)(10x+6)(5x+5)
\]

### Step 1: Factor out common terms
Notice that:
\[
10x + 2 = 2(5x + 1)
\]
\[
10x + 6 = 2(5x + 3)
\]
\[
5x + 5 = 5(x + 1)
\]

Thus, the expression becomes:
\[
2(5x+1) \cdot 2(5x+3) \cdot 5(x+1) = 20(5x+1)(5x+3)(x+1)
\]

### Step 2: Analyze the factors
We need to determine the largest integer that always divides \(20(5x+1)(5x+3)(x+1)\) for all odd \(x\).

#### Divisibility by 20
Since \(20 = 2^2 \cdot 5\), we need to check the divisibility by \(4\) and \(5\).

- **Divisibility by 4**: 
  - \(5x + 1\) and \(5x + 3\) are two consecutive even numbers when \(x\) is odd. Among any two consecutive even numbers, one is divisible by 4. Therefore, \(4\) divides at least one of \(5x+1\) or \(5x+3\).
  - Thus, \(4\) divides \(20(5x+1)(5x+3)(x+1)\).

- **Divisibility by 5**:
  - \(5x + 1\) and \(5x + 3\) are not necessarily divisible by 5, but \(x+1\) (since \(x\) is odd, \(x+1\) is even and can be written as \(2k\)) is not a multiple of 5. However, among any three consecutive integers, one is divisible by 3, and here we have \(5x+1\), \(5x+3\), and \(x+1\). Since \(x\) is odd, \(5x+1\) or \(5x+3\) must be divisible by 5 (as \(5x \equiv -1 \pmod{5}\) or \(5x \equiv -3 \pmod{5}\)).
  - Therefore, \(5\) divides \(20(5x+1)(5x+3)(x+1)\).

### Step 3: Verify the greatest common divisor
We have established that \(20\) divides the expression. To ensure \(20\) is the largest such integer, we need to check if a larger integer always divides the expression.

- **Divisibility by 8**:
  - Among \(5x+1\) and \(5x+3\), one is divisible by 4 (as shown above), but not necessarily by 8. For instance, if \(x = 1\), then \(5x+1 = 6\) and \(5x+3 = 8\), and \(8\) divides the expression, but for \(x = 3\), \(5x+1 = 16\) and \(5x+3 = 18\), and \(8\) does not divide the expression.

- **Divisibility by 10**:
  - Similarly, while \(5\) always divides the expression, \(20\) is the highest power of 2 that always divides the expression.

Thus, the largest integer that always divides the expression \((10x+2)(10x+6)(5x+5)\) for all odd \(x\) is \(\boxed{20}\).

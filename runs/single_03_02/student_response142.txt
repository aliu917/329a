To solve the problem, we start by recalling the formula for the interior angle of a regular polygon. The interior angle of a regular \(n\)-gon is given by:

\[
\theta_n = \frac{(n-2) \cdot 180^\circ}{n}
\]

For the regular \(r\)-gon \(P_1\), the interior angle is:

\[
\theta_r = \frac{(r-2) \cdot 180^\circ}{r}
\]

For the regular \(s\)-gon \(P_2\), the interior angle is:

\[
\theta_s = \frac{(s-2) \cdot 180^\circ}{s}
\]

According to the problem, each interior angle of \(P_1\) is \(\frac{59}{58}\) times as large as each interior angle of \(P_2\). Therefore, we can write the relationship as:

\[
\theta_r = \frac{59}{58} \theta_s
\]

Substituting the expressions for \(\theta_r\) and \(\theta_s\), we get:

\[
\frac{(r-2) \cdot 180^\circ}{r} = \frac{59}{58} \cdot \frac{(s-2) \cdot 180^\circ}{s}
\]

We can cancel out the \(180^\circ\) from both sides of the equation:

\[
\frac{(r-2)}{r} = \frac{59}{58} \cdot \frac{(s-2)}{s}
\]

Next, we clear the fractions by cross-multiplying:

\[
58(r-2)s = 59(r)(s-2)
\]

Expanding both sides, we get:

\[
58rs - 116s = 59rs - 118r
\]

Rearranging terms to isolate \(s\) and \(r\):

\[
59rs - 58rs = 118r - 116s
\]

\[
rs = 118r - 116s
\]

Solving for \(s\):

\[
rs - 116s = 118r
\]

Factoring out \(s\) on the left side:

\[
s(r - 116) = 118r
\]

Solving for \(s\):

\[
s = \frac{118r}{r - 116}
\]

Since \(s\) must be an integer, \(\frac{118r}{r - 116}\) must also be an integer. This implies that \(r - 116\) must be a divisor of \(118r\). To find the largest possible value of \(s\), we need to find the smallest possible positive integer value for \(r - 116\) that is a divisor of \(118r\).

First, we find the divisors of 118. The prime factorization of 118 is:

\[
118 = 2 \times 59
\]

The divisors of 118 are: \(1, 2, 59, 118\).

We will test each divisor to find the largest possible \(s\):

1. **For \(r - 116 = 1\):**
   \[
   r = 117
   \]
   \[
   s = \frac{118 \cdot 117}{117} = 118

   \[

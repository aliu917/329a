To find the area of the trapezoid, we start by recalling the formula for the area of a trapezoid. The area \( A \) of a trapezoid is given by:

\[ A = \frac{1}{2} \times (b_1 + b_2) \times h \]

where \( b_1 \) and \( b_2 \) are the lengths of the two bases, and \( h \) is the height of the trapezoid.

Given in the problem, the height of the trapezoid is \( x \). The first base \( b_1 \) is twice the height, so we have:

\[ b_1 = 2x \]

The second base \( b_2 \) is three times the height, so we have:

\[ b_2 = 3x \]

Now, we substitute these values into the area formula:

\[ A = \frac{1}{2} \times (2x + 3x) \times x \]

Simplify the expression inside the parentheses:

\[ A = \frac{1}{2} \times 5x \times x \]

Next, multiply the terms:

\[ A = \frac{1}{2} \times 5x^2 \]

Finally, simplify the expression:

\[ A = \frac{5x^2}{2} \]

Thus, the area of the trapezoid as a common fraction in terms of the height \( x \) is:

\(\boxed{\frac{5x^2}{2}}\)

The equation \(|z - 3i| + |z - 4| = 5\) represents the set of points \(z\) in the complex plane such that the sum of the distances from \(z\) to the points \(3i\) (which is \((0,3)\)) and \(4\) (which is \((4,0)\)) is constant and equal to 5. \[
\sqrt{(4-0)^2 + (0-3)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = 5.
\]
Since the distance between the foci is equal to the length of the major axis, the ellipse degenerates into a line segment connecting the points \((0,3)\) and \((4,0)\).

\[

\]
To find the point on this line that is closest to the origin, we use the fact that the shortest distance from a point to a line is along the perpendicular from the point to the line. The slope of the line is \(-\frac{3}{4}\), so the slope of the perpendicular line is \(\frac{4}{3}\). The equation of the line passing through the origin with slope \(\frac{4}{3}\) is:
\[
y = \frac{4}{3}x.
\]
We find the intersection of the lines \(y = -\frac{3}{4}x + 3\) and \(y = \frac{4}{3}x\):
\[

\[

Therefore, the minimum value of \(|z|\) is \(\boxed{\frac{12}{5}}\).

Correct solution: By the Triangle Inequality,
\[|z - 3i| + |z - 4| = |z - 4| + |3i - z| \ge |(z - 4) + (3i - z)| = |-4 + 3i| = 5.\]But we are told that $|z - 3i| + |z - 4| = 5.$  The only way that equality can occur is if $z$ lies on the line segment connecting 4 and $3i$ in the complex plane.

To find the sum of the squares of the roots of the quadratic equation \(3x^2 + 4x - 9\), we start by identifying the roots using the quadratic formula. The quadratic formula is given by:

\[ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \]

For the quadratic equation \(3x^2 + 4x - 9\), the coefficients are \(a = 3\), \(b = 4\), and \(c = -9\). The sum and product of the roots of a quadratic equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) are given by:

\[ \text{Sum of the roots} = -\frac{b}{a} \]
\[ \text{Product of the roots} = \frac{c}{a} \]

From the given quadratic equation, the sum of the roots is:

\[ \text{Sum of the roots} = -\frac{4}{3} \]

And the product of the roots is:

\[ \text{Product of the roots} = \frac{-9}{3} = -3 \]

Let the roots be \(r_1\) and \(r_2\). We are asked to find the sum of the squares of the roots, \(r_1^2 + r_2^2\). We can use the identity:

\[ r_1^2 + r_2^2 = (r_1 + r_2)^2 - 2r_1r_2 \]

Substituting the known values:

\[ r_1^2 + r_2^2 = \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)^2 - 2(-3) \]
\[ r_1^2 + r_2^2 = \frac{16}{9} + 6 \]
\[ r_1^2 + r_2^2 = \frac{16}{9} + \frac{54}{9} \]
\[ r_1^2 + r_2^2 = \frac{70}{9} \]

Thus, the sum of the squares of the roots is:

\[
\boxed{\frac{70}{9}}

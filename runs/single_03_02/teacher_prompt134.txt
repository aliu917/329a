Attempt: To compute the double sum \(\sum_{n=2}^\infty \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{k}{2^{n+k}}\), we will first interchange the order of summation. Let's start by analyzing the range of the indices.

For a fixed \(k\), \(k\) ranges from 1 to \(n-1\), which means \(n\) ranges from \(k+1\) to \(\infty\). Therefore, we can rewrite the sum as:
\[
\sum_{n=2}^\infty \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{k}{2^{n+k}} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \sum_{n=k+1}^\infty \frac{k}{2^{n+k}}.
\]

Next, we can factor out \(k\) from the inner sum:
\[
\sum_{k=1}^\infty \sum_{n=k+1}^\infty \frac{k}{2^{n+k}} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty k \sum_{n=k+1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^{n+k}}.
\]

Now, let's focus on the inner sum \(\sum_{n=k+1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^{n+k}}\). We can rewrite the exponent \(n+k\) as \(n+k = (n-k) + k + k = (n-k) + 2k\), but it's easier to just recognize it as a geometric series:
\[
\sum_{n=k+1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^{n+k}} = \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^{m+2k}} = \frac{1}{2^{2k}} \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^m} = \frac{1}{2^{2k}} \cdot \frac{1/2}{1-1/2} = \frac{1}{2^{2k}} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{2^{2k}}.

So the original sum becomes:
\[
\sum_{k=1}^\infty k \cdot \frac{1}{2^{2k}} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{k}{4^k}.

Therefore, the value of the original sum is:
\[
\boxed{\frac{4}{9}}.
\]

Correct solution: The sum is taken over all positive integers $n$ and $k$ such that $k \le n - 1,$ or $n \ge k + 1.$  Thus, we can change the order of summation:
\begin{align*}
\sum_{n=2}^\infty \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{k}{2^{n+k}} &= \sum_{k = 1}^\infty \sum_{n = k + 1}^\infty \frac{k}{2^{n + k}} \\
&= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{k}{2^k} \sum_{n=k+1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^n} \\
&= \sum_{k = 1}^\infty \frac{k}{2^k} \left( \frac{1}{2^{k + 1}} + \frac{1}{2^{k + 2}} + \dotsb \right) \\
&= \sum_{k = 1}^\infty \frac{k}{2^k} \cdot \frac{1}{2^k} \\
&= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{k}{4^k}.
\end{align*}Let
\[S = \sum_{k = 1}^\infty \frac{k}{4^k} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{2}{4^2} + \frac{3}{4^3} + \dotsb.\]Then
\[4S = 1 + \frac{2}{4} + \frac{3}{4^2} + \frac{4}{3^3} + \dotsb.\]Subtracting these equations, we get

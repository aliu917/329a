To determine the smallest integer \( n \), greater than 1, such that \( n^{-1} \pmod{1050} \) is defined, we need to find the smallest \( n \) that is coprime to 1050. This means that \( \gcd(n, 1050) = 1 \).

First, we find the prime factorization of 1050:
\[
1050 = 2 \times 3 \times 5^2 \times 7
\]

For \( n \) to be coprime to 1050, \( n \) must not share any prime factors with 1050. Therefore, \( n \) should not be divisible by 2, 3, 5, or 7.

We will check integers greater than 1 sequentially to find the smallest one that is coprime to 1050.

1. **Check \( n = 2 \)**:
   \[
   \gcd(2, 1050) = 2 \quad (\text{not coprime})
   \]

2. **Check \( n = 3 \)**:
   \[
   \gcd(3, 1050) = 3 \quad (\text{not coprime})
   \]

3. **Check \( n = 4 \)**:
   \[
   \gcd(4, 1050) = 2 \quad (\text{not coprime})
   \]

4. **Check \( n = 5 \)**:
   \[
   \gcd(5, 1050) = 5 \quad (\text{not coprime})
   \]

5. **Check \( n = 6 \)**:
   \[
   \gcd(6, 1050) = 6 \quad (\text{not coprime})
   \]

6. **Check \( n = 7 \)**:
   \[
   \gcd(7, 1050) = 7 \quad (\text{not coprime})
   \]

7. **Check \( n = 8 \)**:
   \[
   \gcd(8, 1050) = 2 \quad (\text{not coprime})
   \]

8. **Check \( n = 9 \)**:
   \[
   \gcd(9, 1050) = 3 \quad (\text{not coprime})
   \]

9. **Check \( n = 11 \)**:
   \[
   \gcd(11, 1050) = 1 \quad (\text{coprime})
   \]

Since 11 is the smallest integer greater than 1 that is coprime to 1050, the smallest integer \( n \) such that \( n^{-1} \pmod{1050} \) is defined is:
\[
\boxed{11}

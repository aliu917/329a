To solve the problem, we need to determine the number of valid sequences of length 10 that do not contain two consecutive 1s. Let's denote the number of valid sequences of length \( n \) by \( a_n \).

We can break down the problem by considering the last digit of the sequence:
1. If the last digit is 0, the first \( n-1 \) digits can be any valid sequence of length \( n-1 \). There are \( a_{n-1} \) such sequences.
2. If the last digit is 1, the second last digit must be 0 (to avoid consecutive 1s). The first \( n-2 \) digits can be any valid sequence of length \( n-2 \). There are \( a_{n-2} \) such sequences.

Therefore, the recurrence relation is:
\[ a_n = a_{n-1} + a_{n-2} \]

To use this recurrence relation, we need initial conditions:
- For \( n = 1 \), the valid sequences are 0 and 1. So, \( a_1 = 2 \).
- For \( n = 2 \), the valid sequences are 00, 01, and 10. The sequence 11 is not valid. So, \( a_2 = 3 \).

Now, we can use the recurrence relation to find \( a_{10} \):
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_3 &= a_2 + a_1 = 3 + 2 = 5, \\
a_4 &= a_3 + a_2 = 5 + 3 = 8, \\
a_5 &= a_4 + a_3 = 8 + 5 = 13, \\
a_6 &= a_5 + a_4 = 13 + 8 = 21, \\
a_7 &= a_6 + a_5 = 21 + 13 = 34, \\
a_8 &= a_7 + a_6 = 34 + 21 = 55, \\
a_9 &= a_8 + a_7 = 55 + 34 = 89, \\
a_{10} &= a_9 + a_8 = 89 + 55 = 144.
\end{aligned}
\]

The total number of sequences of length 10 is \( 2^{10} = 1024 \). The probability that a randomly generated sequence of length 10 does not contain two consecutive 1s is:
\[ \frac{a_{10}}{2^{10}} = \frac{144}{1024} = \frac{9}{64} \]

Since 9 and 64 are relatively prime, the probability is in its simplest form. Therefore, \( m = 9 \) and \( n = 64 \), and the sum \( m + n \) is:
\[ \boxed{73} \]

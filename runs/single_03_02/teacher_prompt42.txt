You are given a reasoning attempt for answering the following question: A delicious circular pie with diameter $12\text{ cm}$ is cut into three equal-sized sector-shaped pieces. Let $l$ be the number of centimeters in the length of the longest line segment that may be drawn in one of these pieces. What is $l^2$?

Attempt: To solve the problem, we first need to understand the geometry of the sector-shaped pie piece. The pie is a circle with a diameter of 12 cm, so its radius \( r \) is:

\[
r = \frac{12}{2} = 6 \text{ cm}
\]

The pie is cut into three equal-sized sector-shaped pieces, each with a central angle of:

\[
\frac{360^\circ}{3} = 120^\circ
\]

We are asked to find the length of the longest line segment that can be drawn in one of these sector-shaped pieces. The longest line segment in a sector of a circle is the chord that connects the two endpoints of the arc of the sector, which in this case is the side of the sector opposite the central angle of 120 degrees.

To find the length of this chord, we can use the Law of Cosines in the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord. The triangle has two sides of length 6 cm (the radius of the circle) and an angle of 120 degrees between them. Let's denote the length of the chord by \( l \). According to the Law of Cosines:

\[
l^2 = 6^2 + 6^2 - 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 6 \cdot \cos(120^\circ)
\]

We know that \(\cos(120^\circ) = -\frac{1}{2}\), so we can substitute this value into the equation:

\[
l^2 = 6^2 + 6^2 - 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 6 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)
\]

Simplify the expression:

\[
l^2 = 36 + 36 + 36 = 108
\]

Thus, the value of \( l^2 \) is:

\[
\boxed{108}
\]

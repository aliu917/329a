To solve this problem, we need to consider the different ways Alpha and Beta can purchase the products and then sum these possibilities.

First, let's define the scenarios for Alpha and Beta:
- Alpha can purchase up to 3 products, with no repeats of the same flavor.
- Beta can purchase up to 3 products, with repeats allowed.

### Step 1: Calculate the number of ways Alpha can purchase products
Alpha can purchase 0, 1, 2, or 3 products. We will calculate the number of ways for each case.

#### Case 1: Alpha purchases 0 products
- There is only 1 way for this to happen.
- Number of ways: \( \binom{0}{0} = 1 \)

#### Case 2: Alpha purchases 1 product
- Alpha can choose 1 out of 5 flavors of oreos or 1 out of 3 flavors of milk.
- Number of ways: \( 5 + 3 = 8 \)

#### Case 3: Alpha purchases 2 products
- Alpha can choose 2 different products out of the 8 total products (5 oreos + 3 milk).
- Number of ways: \( \binom{8}{2} = 28 \)

#### Case 4: Alpha purchases 3 products
- Alpha can choose 3 different products out of the 8 total products.
- Number of ways: \( \binom{8}{3} = 56 \)

### Step 2: Calculate the number of ways Beta can purchase products
Beta can purchase 0, 1, 2, or 3 products, with repeats allowed.

#### Case 1: Beta purchases 0 products
- There is only 1 way for this to happen.
- Number of ways: \( \binom{8}{0} = 1 \)

#### Case 2: Beta purchases 1 product
- Beta can choose 1 out of 8 products.
- Number of ways: \( 8 \)

#### Case 3: Beta purchases 2 products
- Beta can choose 2 products with repetition allowed. This is equivalent to choosing 2 products from 8 with replacement.
- Number of ways: \( 8^2 = 64 \)

#### Case 4: Beta purchases 3 products
- Beta can choose 3 products with repetition allowed.
- Number of ways: \( 8^3 = 512 \)

### Step 3: Combine the cases for Alpha and Beta
We need to find the total number of ways they can collectively purchase 3 products. We will consider the cases where Alpha purchases \( k \) products and Beta purchases \( 3 - k \) products.

- \( k = 0 \): Beta purchases 3 products.
  - Number of ways: \( 1 \times 512 = 512 \)
- \( k = 1 \): Alpha purchases 1 product, Beta purchases 2 products.
  - Number of ways: \( 8 \times 64 = 512 \)
- \( k = 2 \): Alpha purchases 2 products, Beta purchases 1 product.
  - Number of ways: \( 28 \times 8 = 224 \)
- \( k = 3 \): Alpha purchases 3 products, Beta purchases 0 products.
  - Number of ways: \( 56 \times 1 = 56 \)

### Step 4: Sum the total number of ways
\[
512 + 512 + 224 + 56 = 1304
\]

Thus, the total number of ways Alpha and Beta could have left the store with 3 products collectively is \(\boxed{1304}\).

To find the area of the union of the regions enclosed by the square and the circle, we need to calculate the areas of the square and the circle and then subtract the area of their overlap.

1. **Calculate the area of the square:**
   The side length of the square is 10. Therefore, the area of the square is:
   \[
   10 \times 10 = 100
   \]

2. **Calculate the area of the circle:**
   The radius of the circle is 10. Therefore, the area of the circle is:
   \[
   \pi \times 10^2 = 100\pi
   \]

3. **Determine the overlap area:**
   The circle is centered at one of the vertices of the square, and its radius is equal to the side length of the square. This means that the circle will overlap with the square in a quarter-circle shape. The area of a quarter-circle with radius 10 is:
   \[
   \frac{1}{4} \times 100\pi = 25\pi
   \]

4. **Calculate the area of the union:**
   The area of the union of the square and the circle is the sum of the area of the square and the area of the circle minus the area of their overlap (the quarter-circle):
   \[
   100 + 100\pi - 25\pi = 100 + 75\pi
   \]

Therefore, the area of the union of the regions enclosed by the square and the circle is:
\[
\boxed{100 + 75\pi}

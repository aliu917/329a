To solve the expression \(\sqrt{53 + 20\sqrt{7}}\) in the form \(a + b\sqrt{c}\), we start by assuming that:

\[
\sqrt{53 + 20\sqrt{7}} = a + b\sqrt{7}
\]

Next, we square both sides of the equation to eliminate the square root:

\[
53 + 20\sqrt{7} = (a + b\sqrt{7})^2
\]

Expanding the right-hand side, we get:

\[
(a + b\sqrt{7})^2 = a^2 + 2ab\sqrt{7} + (b\sqrt{7})^2 = a^2 + 2ab\sqrt{7} + 7b^2
\]

We can separate the rational and irrational parts:

\[
a^2 + 7b^2 + 2ab\sqrt{7}
\]

For the equation to hold, the rational parts must be equal, and the coefficients of \(\sqrt{7}\) must be equal. Therefore, we have two equations:

1. \(a^2 + 7b^2 = 53\)
2. \(2ab = 20\)

From the second equation, we solve for \(ab\):

\[
2ab = 20 \implies ab = 10
\]

We now have a system of equations:

\[
a^2 + 7b^2 = 53
\]
\[
ab = 10
\]

We solve for \(a\) in terms of \(b\) from the second equation:

\[
a = \frac{10}{b}
\]

Substitute \(a = \frac{10}{b}\) into the first equation:

\[
\left(\frac{10}{b}\right)^2 + 7b^2 = 53
\]

Simplify and solve for \(b^2\):

\[
\frac{100}{b^2} + 7b^2 = 53
\]

Multiply through by \(b^2\) to clear the fraction:

\[
100 + 7b^4 = 53b^2
\]

Rearrange into a standard polynomial form:

\[
7b^4 - 53b^2 + 100 = 0
\]

Let \(x = b^2\). Then the equation becomes:

\[
7x^2 - 53x + 100 = 0
\]

We solve this quadratic equation using the quadratic formula \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\), where \(a = 7\), \(b = -53\), and \(c = 100\):

\[
x = \frac{53 \pm \sqrt{(-53)^2 - 4 \cdot 7 \cdot 100}}{2 \cdot 7}
\]

Calculate the discriminant:

\[
(-53)^2 - 4 \cdot 7 \cdot 100 = 2809 - 2800 = 9
\]

So,

\[
x = \frac{53 \pm \sqrt{9}}{14} = \frac{53 \pm 3}{14}
\]

This gives us two solutions for \(x\):

\[
x = \frac{56}{14} = 4 \quad \text{and} \quad x = \frac{50}{14} = \frac{25}{7}
\]

Since \(x = b^2\) and \(b^2\) must be an integer, we have:

\[
b^2 = 4 \implies b = 2 \text{ or } b = -2
\]

Substitute \(b = 2\) or \(b = -2\) back into \(a = \frac{10}{b}\):

If \(b = 2\):

\[
a = \frac{10}{2} = 5
\]

If \(b = -2\):

\[
a = \frac{10}{-2} = -5
\]

Thus, the solutions are \((a, b) = (5, 2)\) or \((a, b) = (-5, -2)\). Both pairs give the same form \(a + b\sqrt{7}\).

Using \((a, b) = (5, 2)\):

\[
\sqrt{53 + 20\sqrt{7}} = 5 + 2\sqrt{7}
\]

Therefore, \(a = 5\), \(b = 2\), and \(c = 7\). We find \(a + b + c\):

\[
a + b + c = 5 + 2 + 7 = 14
\]

The final answer is:

\[
\boxed{14}
\]

To solve the problem of finding the probability that Greg rolls more 1's than 6's when rolling four fair six-sided dice, we will follow these steps:

1. **Determine the total number of outcomes**: Each die has 6 faces, so for four dice, the total number of outcomes is:
   \[
   6^4 = 1296
   \]

2. **Identify the favorable outcomes**: We need to count the outcomes where the number of 1's is greater than the number of 6's. Let's denote the number of 1's by \( x \) and the number of 6's by \( y \). We are looking for cases where \( x > y \).

3. **Possible values for \( x \) and \( y \)**: Since we are rolling four dice, \( x + y \) can range from 0 to 4. We need to consider each case where \( x > y \).

4. **Calculate the number of favorable outcomes for each case**:
   - **Case \( x = 1, y = 0 \)**: We need to choose 1 position out of 4 for the 1 and the remaining 3 positions can be any of the other 4 numbers (2, 3, 4, 5).
     \[
     \binom{4}{1} \times 4^3 = 4 \times 64 = 256
     \]
   - **Case \( x = 2, y = 0 \)**: We need to choose 2 positions out of 4 for the 1's and the remaining 2 positions can be any of the other 4 numbers.
     \[
     \binom{4}{2} \times 4^2 = 6 \times 16 = 96
     \]
   - **Case \( x = 2, y = 1 \)**: We need to choose 2 positions out of 4 for the 1's and 1 position out of the remaining 2 for the 6.
     \[
     \binom{4}{2} \times \binom{2}{1} \times 4 = 6 \times 2 \times 4 = 48
     \]
   - **Case \( x = 3, y = 0 \)**: We need to choose 3 positions out of 4 for the 1's and the remaining 1 position can be any of the other 4 numbers.
     \[
     \binom{4}{3} \times 4 = 4 \times 4 = 16
     \]
   - **Case \( x = 3, y = 1 \)**: We need to choose 3 positions out of 4 for the 1's and 1 position out of the remaining 1 for the 6.
     \[
     \binom{4}{3} \times \binom{1}{1} = 4 \times 1 = 4
     \]

5. **Sum the number of favorable outcomes**:
   \[
   256 + 96 + 48 + 16 + 4 = 412
   \]

6. **Calculate the probability**:
   \[
   \text{Probability} = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of outcomes}} = \frac{412}{1296} = \frac{103}{324}
   \]

Thus, the probability that Greg rolls more 1's than 6's is \(\boxed{\frac{103}{324}}\).

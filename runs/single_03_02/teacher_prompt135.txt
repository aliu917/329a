Attempt: To compute the double sum \(\sum_{n=2}^\infty \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{k}{2^{n+k}}\), we start by changing the order of summation. Notice that the summand \(\frac{k}{2^{n+k}}\) can be rewritten as \(\frac{k}{2^n \cdot 2^k}\). The inner sum is from \(k=1\) to \(k=n-1\), so we can rewrite the double sum as follows:

\[
\sum_{n=2}^\infty \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{k}{2^{n+k}} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \sum_{n=k+1}^\infty \frac{k}{2^n \cdot 2^k} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{k}{2^k} \sum_{n=k+1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^n}

\]

Substituting this back into our expression, we get:

Correct solution: The sum is taken over all positive integers $n$ and $k$ such that $k \le n - 1,$ or $n \ge k + 1.$  Thus, we can change the order of summation:

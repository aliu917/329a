To solve the problem, we need to determine the probability that after suitable rotation, a brick of dimensions \(a_1 \times a_2 \times a_3\) can be enclosed in a box of dimensions \(b_1 \times b_2 \times b_3\). This means that after arranging the \(a_i\) and \(b_i\) in non-decreasing order, we must have \(a_1 \leq b_1\), \(a_2 \leq b_2\), and \(a_3 \leq b_3\).

First, let's consider the total number of ways to choose and arrange the numbers. The total number of ways to choose and arrange \(a_1, a_2, a_3\) and \(b_1, b_2, b_3\) is the same as the number of ways to choose 6 numbers out of 1000 and then arrange them, which is given by:
\[
\binom{1000}{6} \cdot 6! = \binom{1000}{6} \cdot 720
\]

Next, we need to count the number of favorable outcomes where \(a_1 \leq b_1\), \(a_2 \leq b_2\), and \(a_3 \leq b_3\). The favorable outcomes correspond to choosing 6 numbers and then arranging them such that the smallest three are assigned to \(a_1, a_2, a_3\) and the largest three are assigned to \(b_1, b_2, b_3\). The number of ways to choose 6 numbers from 1000 is \(\binom{1000}{6}\), and once we have chosen these 6 numbers, there is exactly one way to assign them to \(a_1, a_2, a_3\) and \(b_1, b_2, b_3\) such that the \(a_i\) are the smallest three and the \(b_i\) are the largest three. Therefore, the number of favorable outcomes is:
\[
\binom{1000}{6}
\]

The probability \(p\) is then the ratio of the number of favorable outcomes to the total number of outcomes:
\[
p = \frac{\binom{1000}{6}}{\binom{1000}{6} \cdot 720 / 6!} = \frac{\binom{1000}{6}}{\binom{1000}{6}} = \frac{1}{20}
\]

Thus, the probability \(p\) is \(\frac{1}{20}\). The sum of the numerator and the denominator is:
\[
1 + 20 = 21
\]

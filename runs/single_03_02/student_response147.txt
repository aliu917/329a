To find the smallest positive multiple of \(23\) that is \(4\) more than a multiple of \(89\), we need to solve the congruence:
\[ 23x \equiv 4 \pmod{89} \]

This can be rewritten as:
\[ 23x - 89k = 4 \]
for some integer \(k\). This is a linear Diophantine equation, and we can solve it using the Extended Euclidean Algorithm.

First, we apply the Euclidean Algorithm to find the greatest common divisor (gcd) of \(23\) and \(89\):
\[ 89 = 3 \cdot 23 + 20 \]
\[ 23 = 1 \cdot 20 + 3 \]
\[ 20 = 6 \cdot 3 + 2 \]
\[ 3 = 1 \cdot 2 + 1 \]
\[ 2 = 2 \cdot 1 + 0 \]

The gcd is \(1\), and since \(1\) divides \(4\), a solution exists. Now, we use the Extended Euclidean Algorithm to express \(1\) as a combination of \(23\) and \(89\):
\[ 1 = 3 - 1 \cdot 2 \]
\[ 2 = 20 - 6 \cdot 3 \]
\[ 1 = 3 - 1 \cdot (20 - 6 \cdot 3) = 7 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot 20 \]
\[ 3 = 23 - 1 \cdot 20 \]
\[ 1 = 7 \cdot (23 - 1 \cdot 20) - 1 \cdot 20 = 7 \cdot 23 - 8 \cdot 20 \]
\[ 20 = 89 - 3 \cdot 23 \]
\[ 1 = 7 \cdot 23 - 8 \cdot (89 - 3 \cdot 23) = 7 \cdot 23 - 8 \cdot 89 + 24 \cdot 23 = 31 \cdot 23 - 8 \cdot 89 \]

Thus, we have:
\[ 1 = 31 \cdot 23 - 8 \cdot 89 \]

Multiplying both sides by \(4\):
\[ 4 = 124 \cdot 23 - 32 \cdot 89 \]

This gives us:
\[ 124 \cdot 23 \equiv 4 \pmod{89} \]

Therefore, \(x = 124\) is a solution to the congruence \(23x \equiv 4 \pmod{89}\). Since \(124\) is not the smallest positive solution, we reduce it modulo \(89\):
\[ 124 \mod 89 = 124 - 89 = 35 \]

Thus, the smallest positive solution is \(x = 35\). The smallest positive multiple of \(23\) that is \(4\) more than a multiple of \(89\) is:
\[ 23 \cdot 35 = 805 \]

Let's denote the two numbers by \( x \) and \( y \). According to the problem, we have two pieces of information:

1. The sum of the two numbers is 6:
\[ x + y = 6 \]

2. The difference of their squares is 12:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = 12 \]

We can use the identity for the difference of squares, which is:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y) \]

Substituting the known value of \( x + y \) from the first equation into the identity, we get:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y) = 6(x - y) \]

We know from the second equation that:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = 12 \]

So we can set up the equation:
\[ 6(x - y) = 12 \]

To find \( x - y \), we divide both sides of the equation by 6:
\[ x - y = 2 \]

The positive difference of the two numbers is:
\[ \boxed{2} \]

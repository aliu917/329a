Let's denote the two numbers by \(x\) and \(y\). According to the problem, we have the following two equations:

1. The sum of the two numbers is 6:
\[x + y = 6\]

2. The difference of their squares is 12:
\[x^2 - y^2 = 12\]

We know that the difference of squares can be factored as follows:
\[x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y)\]

Substituting the first equation into the factored form of the second equation, we get:
\[12 = 6(x - y)\]

To find \(x - y\), we divide both sides of the equation by 6:
\[x - y = 2\]

Now, we have a system of linear equations:
\[x + y = 6\]
\[x - y = 2\]

We can solve this system by adding the two equations together:
\[(x + y) + (x - y) = 6 + 2\]
\[2x = 8\]
\[x = 4\]

Next, we substitute \(x = 4\) back into the first equation to find \(y\):
\[4 + y = 6\]
\[y = 2\]

The two numbers are \(x = 4\) and \(y = 2\). The positive difference between these two numbers is:
\[x - y = 4 - 2 = 2\]

Thus, the positive difference of the two numbers is \(\boxed{2}\).

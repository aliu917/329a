Question: Triangle \$ABC\$ is inscribed in circle \$\omega\$ with \$AB=5\$, \$BC=7\$, and \$AC=3\$. The bisector of angle \$A\$ meets side \$\overline{BC}\$ at \$D\$ and circle \$\omega\$ at a second point \$E\$. Let \$\gamma\$ be the circle with diameter \$\overline{DE}\$. Circles \$\omega\$ and \$\gamma\$ meet at \$E\$ and a second point \$F\$. Then \$AF^2 = \frac mn\$, where \$m\$ and \$n\$ are relatively prime positive integers. Find \$m+n\$.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Use the angle bisector theorem to find \$CD=\tfrac{21}{8}\$, \$BD=\tfrac{35}{8}\$, and use Stewart's Theorem to find \$AD=\tfrac{15}{8}\$. Use Power of Point \$D\$ to find \$DE=\tfrac{49}{8}\$, and so \$AE=8\$. Use law of cosines to find \$\angle CAD = \tfrac{\pi} {3}\$, hence \$\angle BAD = \tfrac{\pi}{3}\$ as well, and \$\triangle BCE\$ is equilateral, so \$BC=CE=BE=7\$.

In triangle \$AEF\$, let \$X\$ be the foot of the altitude from \$A\$; then \$EF=EX+XF\$, where we use signed lengths. Writing \$EX=AE \cdot \cos \angle AEF\$ and \$XF=AF \cdot \cos \angle AFE\$, we get
\begin{align}\tag{1}
EF = AE \cdot \cos \angle AEF + AF \cdot \cos \angle AFE.
\end{align}
Note \$\angle AFE = \angle ACE\$, and the Law of Cosines in  \$\triangle ACE\$ gives  \$\cos \angle ACE = -\tfrac 17\$.
Also, \$\angle AEF = \angle DEF\$, and \$\angle DFE = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\$ (\$DE\$ is a diameter), so \$\cos \angle AEF = \tfrac{EF}{DE} =  \tfrac{8}{49}\cdot EF\$.
Plugging in all our values into equation \$(1)\$, we get:
EF = \tfrac{64}{49} EF -\tfrac{1}{7} AF \quad \Longrightarrow \quad EF = \tfrac{7}{15} AF.
The Law of Cosines in  \$\triangle AEF\$, with \$EF=\tfrac 7{15}AF\$ and \$\cos\angle AFE = -\tfrac 17\$ gives
8^2 = AF^2 + \tfrac{49}{225}  AF^2 + \tfrac 2{15} AF^2 = \tfrac{225+49+30}{225}\cdot AF^2
Thus \$AF^2 = \frac{900}{19}\$. The answer is \$\boxed{919}\$.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Define the lengths of the sides of triangle \( ABC \) as \( AB = 5 \), \( BC = 7 \), and \( AC = 3 \). 

Step 1: Use the angle bisector theorem to find the segments \( CD \) and \( BD \) where \( D \) is the intersection of the angle bisector of \( A \) with \( BC \). Calculate \( CD = \frac{21}{8} \) and \( BD = \frac{35}{8} \).

Step 2: Use Stewart's Theorem to find the length of \( AD \). The result is \( AD = \frac{15}{8} \).

Step 3: Apply the Power of a Point theorem at point \( D \) to find the length \( DE \). The result is \( DE = \frac{49}{8} \).

Step 4: Calculate the length \( AE \) using the fact that \( AE + DE = AD \). Therefore, \( AE = 8 \).

Step 5: Use the law of cosines in triangle \( ACD \) to find the angle \( \angle CAD \), resulting in \( \angle CAD = \frac{\pi}{3} \).

Step 6: Since \( \angle BAD = \frac{\pi}{3} \), triangle \( BCE \) is equilateral, hence \( BC = CE = BE = 7 \).

Step 7: Consider triangle \( AEF \) and let \( X \) be the foot of the altitude from \( A \). Establish the relation \( EF = EX + XF \).

Step 8: Write \( EX = AE \cdot \cos \angle AEF \) and \( XF = AF \cdot \cos \angle AFE \). Combine these into equation (1): 
\[ EF = AE \cdot \cos \angle AEF + AF \cdot \cos \angle AFE. \]

Step 9: Note that \( \angle AFE = \angle ACE \) and use the law of cosines in triangle \( ACE \) to find \( \cos \angle ACE = -\frac{1}{7} \).

Step 10: Identify that \( \cos \angle AEF = \frac{EF}{DE} = \frac{8}{49} \cdot EF \) because \( DE \) is a diameter.

Step 11: Substitute known values into equation (1) leading to the equation:
\[ EF = \frac{64}{49} EF - \frac{1}{7} AF \quad \Longrightarrow \quad EF = \frac{7}{15} AF. \]

Step 12: Use the law of cosines in triangle \( AEF \) with \( EF = \frac{7}{15} AF \) and \( \cos \angle AFE = -\frac{1}{7} \) to set up the equation:
\[ 8^2 = AF^2 + \frac{49}{225} AF^2 + \frac{2}{15} AF^2. \]

Step 13: Simplify the equation to find \( AF^2 \). The final result is \( AF^2 = \frac{900}{19} \).

Step 14: Since \( AF^2 = \frac{m}{n} \) with \( m = 900 \) and \( n = 19 \) being relatively prime, we need to find \( m+n \).

Final answer: \( 900 + 19 = 919 \), hence the answer is \( \boxed{919} \).

Question: Sarah intended to multiply a two-digit number and a three-digit number, but she left out the multiplication sign and simply placed the two-digit number to the left of the three-digit number, thereby forming a five-digit number.  This number is exactly nine times the product Sarah should have obtained.  What is the sum of the two-digit number and the three-digit number?

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: As shown above, we have \$1000x+y=9xy\$, so \$1000/y=9-1/x\$. \$1000/y\$ must be just a little bit smaller than 9, so we find \$y=112\$, \$x=14\$, and the solution is \$\boxed{126}\$.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Denote the two-digit number as \$x\$ and the three-digit number as \$y\$.

Step 1: The five-digit number formed by placing \$x\$ to the left of \$y\$ can be expressed as \$1000x + y\$.

Step 2: According to the problem, this five-digit number is nine times the product of the two numbers, which gives the equation: \$1000x + y = 9xy\$.

Step 3: Rearranging the equation from Step 2, we have: \$1000 = 9xy - y\$, which simplifies to \$1000 = y(9x - 1)\$.

Step 4: Solving for \$y\$, we get: \$y = \frac{1000}{9x - 1}\$.

Step 5: To find suitable values for \$x\$ and \$y\$, we need \$y\$ to be a three-digit number, which means \$100 \leq y < 1000\$.

Step 6: This leads to the inequalities: \$100 \leq \frac{1000}{9x - 1} < 1000\$.

Step 7: Solving the left inequality, \$100(9x - 1) \leq 1000 \Longrightarrow 9x - 1 \leq 10 \Longrightarrow 9x \leq 11 \Longrightarrow x \leq 1.22\$. Since \$x\$ is a two-digit number, the minimum value of \$x\$ must satisfy this condition.

Step 8: For the right inequality, we solve \$\frac{1000}{9x - 1} < 1000\$, which simplifies to \$1000 < 1000(9x - 1) \Longrightarrow 1 < 9x - 1 \Longrightarrow 2 < 9x \Longrightarrow x > \frac{2}{9}\$. 

Step 9: Testing possible values of \$x\$, we can try \$x = 14\$ (a valid two-digit number) which gives \$y = \frac{1000}{9 \cdot 14 - 1} = \frac{1000}{126} \approx 7.936\$ (not valid).

Step 10: Testing \$x = 12\$, we find \$y = \frac{1000}{9 \cdot 12 - 1} = \frac{1000}{107} \approx 9.34\$ (not valid).

Step 11: Testing \$x = 14\$, we find \$y = 112\$, which is a valid three-digit number.

Step 12: Finally, we compute the sum of \$x\$ and \$y\$: \$14 + 112 = 126\$.

Final answer: \(\boxed{126}\)

Question: Define a <i>T-grid</i> to be a \$3\times3\$ matrix which satisfies the following two properties:

The <i>T-grid</i> can be considered as a tic-tac-toe board: five \$1\$'s (or X's) and four \$0\$'s (or O's).
There are only \$\dbinom{9}{5} = 126\$  ways to fill the board with five \$1\$'s and four \$0\$'s. Now we just need to subtract the number of bad grids. Bad grids are ones with more than one person winning, or where someone has won twice.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Define the T-grid as a 3x3 matrix with 5 entries as 1's and 4 entries as 0's.

Step 1: Calculate the total number of ways to fill the board with five 1's and four 0's, which is given by \(\dbinom{9}{5} = 126\).

Step 2: Identify and define "bad grids" as those that violate the T-grid conditions, specifically those with more than one winning line (three-in-a-row).

Step 3: Case 1: Analyze the scenario where each player wins once. Player 1 (1's) and Player 0 (0's) can either both win by taking rows or both win by taking columns.

Step 4: For both players winning by taking a row:
- Player 1 has 3 choices for a row.
- Player 0 has 2 choices for a different row.
- There are 3 choices for Player 0 to take a single box in the remaining row.
- The total for this scenario is \(3 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 18\).

Step 5: For both players winning by taking a column:
- Using similar reasoning, we again find \(3 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 18\) cases.

Step 6: Add the cases from Step 4 and Step 5, giving a total of \(36\) bad grids for Case 1.

Step 7: Case 2: Analyze the scenario where Player 1 wins twice. Player 0 can only fill 4 spots, so cannot win twice.

Step 8: Player 1 picks a row and a column:
- There are \(3\) ways to pick the row.
- There are \(3\) ways to pick the column.
- This results in \(3 \cdot 3 = 9\) cases.

Step 9: Player 1 picks a row/column and a diagonal:
- There are \(6\) ways to pick the row or column.
- There are \(2\) ways to pick the diagonal.
- This results in \(6 \cdot 2 = 12\) cases.

Step 10: Player 1 picks both diagonals:
- There is only \(1\) case (filling the entire grid with a diagonal).

Step 11: Add the cases from Step 8, Step 9, and Step 10 to find the total for Case 2: \(9 + 12 + 1 = 22\).

Step 12: Calculate the total number of valid T-grids by subtracting the bad grids from the total combinations: \(126 - 36 - 22 = 68\).

Final answer: \(\boxed{68}\)

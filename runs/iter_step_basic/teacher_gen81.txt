Question: The graphs of the equations
<center>\$y=k, \qquad y=\sqrt{3}x+2k, \qquad y=-\sqrt{3}x+2k,\$</center>
are drawn in the coordinate plane for \$k=-10,-9,-8,\ldots,9,10.\,\$  These 63 lines cut part of the plane into equilateral triangles of side length \$\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}.\,\$  How many such triangles are formed?

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: =
We note that the lines partition the hexagon of the six extremal lines into disjoint unit regular triangles, and forms a series of unit regular triangles along the edge of the hexagon.
<asy>
size(200);
picture pica, picb, picc;
int i;
for(i=-10;i<=10;++i){
if((i%10) == 0){draw(pica,(-20/sqrt(3)-abs((0,i))/sqrt(3),i)--(20/sqrt(3)+abs((0,i))/sqrt(3),i),black+0.7);}
else{draw(pica,(-20/sqrt(3)-abs((0,i))/sqrt(3),i)--(20/sqrt(3)+abs((0,i))/sqrt(3),i));}
}
picb = rotate(120,origin)*pica;
picc = rotate(240,origin)*pica;
add(pica);add(picb);add(picc);
</asy>
Solving the above equations for \$k=\pm 10\$, we see that the hexagon in question is regular, with side length \$\frac{20}{\sqrt{3}}\$. Then, the number of triangles within the hexagon is simply the ratio of the area of the hexagon to the area of a regular triangle. Since the ratio of the area of two similar figures is the square of the ratio of their side lengths, we see that the ratio of the area of one of the six equilateral triangles composing the regular hexagon to the area of a unit regular triangle is just \$\left(\frac{20/\sqrt{3}}{2/\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 = 100\$. Thus, the total number of unit triangles is \$6 \times 100 = 600\$.
There are \$6 \cdot 10\$ equilateral triangles formed by lines on the edges of the hexagon. Thus, our answer is \$600+60 = \boxed{660}\$.
=

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Identify the equations of the lines: \$y=k\$, \$y=\sqrt{3}x+2k\$, and \$y=-\sqrt{3}x+2k\$, where \$k=-10,-9,-8,\ldots,9,10\$.

Step 1: Note that the lines partition the hexagon formed by the six extremal lines into disjoint unit regular triangles.

Step 2: Recognize that the hexagon is regular with a side length of \$\frac{20}{\sqrt{3}}\$ for \$k=\pm 10\$.

Step 3: Calculate the area of the hexagon. The formula for the area of a regular hexagon with side length \$s\$ is given by \$\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}s^2\$. Thus, for \$s=\frac{20}{\sqrt{3}}\$, the area is \$\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\left(\frac{20}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \frac{400}{3} = 200\sqrt{3}\$.

Step 4: Calculate the area of a unit regular triangle with side length \$\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}\$:
\[
\text{Area} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}.
\]

Step 5: Find the ratio of the area of the hexagon to the area of a unit triangle:
\[
\text{Ratio} = \frac{200\sqrt{3}}{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}} = 200 \cdot 3 = 600.
\]

Step 6: Since the hexagon consists of 6 equilateral triangles, the number of unit triangles within the hexagon is \$6 \times 100 = 600\$.

Step 7: Account for the additional triangles formed along the edges of the hexagon, which adds \$60\$ triangles.

Final answer: \$600 + 60 = \boxed{660}\$.

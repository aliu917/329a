Question: Find the number of ordered pairs \$(m, n)\$ such that \$m\$ and \$n\$ are positive integers in the set \$\{1, 2, ..., 30\}\$ and the greatest common divisor of \$2^m + 1\$ and \$2^n - 1\$ is not \$1\$.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: This solution refers to the <b>Remarks</b> section.
By the Euclidean Algorithm, we have \gcd\left(2^m+1,2^m-1\right)=\gcd\left(2,2^m-1\right)=1.
We are given that \$\gcd\left(2^m+1,2^n-1\right)>1.\$ Multiplying both sides by \$\gcd\left(2^m-1,2^n-1\right)\$ gives
\begin{align*}
\gcd\left(2^m+1,2^n-1\right)\cdot\gcd\left(2^m-1,2^n-1\right)&>\gcd\left(2^m-1,2^n-1\right) \\
\gcd\left(\left(2^m+1\right)\left(2^m-1\right),2^n-1\right)&>\gcd\left(2^m-1,2^n-1\right) \hspace{12mm} &&\text{by }\textbf{Claim 1} \\
\gcd\left(2^{2m}-1,2^n-1\right)&>\gcd\left(2^m-1,2^n-1\right) \\
2^{\gcd(2m,n)}-1&>2^{\gcd(m,n)}-1 &&\text{by }\textbf{Claim 2} \\
\gcd(2m,n)&>\gcd(m,n),
\end{align*}
which implies that \$n\$ must have more factors of \$2\$ than \$m\$ does.

--------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Establish that we are looking for ordered pairs \((m, n)\) such that \(\gcd(2^m + 1, 2^n - 1) > 1\).

Step 2: Recall that by the Euclidean Algorithm, \(\gcd(2^m + 1, 2^m - 1) = 1\).

Step 3: Since we need \(\gcd(2^m + 1, 2^n - 1) > 1\), multiply both sides by \(\gcd(2^m - 1, 2^n - 1)\).

Step 4: Apply the property of gcd to obtain \(\gcd((2^m + 1)(2^m - 1), 2^n - 1) > \gcd(2^m - 1, 2^n - 1)\).

Step 5: This leads to \(\gcd(2^{2m} - 1, 2^n - 1) > \gcd(2^m - 1, 2^n - 1)\).

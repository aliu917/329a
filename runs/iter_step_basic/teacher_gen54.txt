Question: Complex numbers \$a,\$ \$b,\$ and \$c\$ are zeros of a polynomial \$P(z) = z^3 + qz + r,\$ and \$|a|^2 + |b|^2 + |c|^2 = 250.\$ The points corresponding to \$a,\$ \$b,\$ and \$c\$ in the complex plane are the vertices of a right triangle with hypotenuse \$h.\$ Find \$h^2.\$

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: By Vieta's formula, the sum of the roots is equal to 0, or \$a+b+c=0\$. Therefore, \$\frac{(a+b+c)}{3}=0\$. Because the centroid of any triangle is the average of its vertices, the centroid of this triangle is the origin. Let one leg of the right triangle be \$x\$ and the other leg be \$y\$. Without the loss of generality, let \$\overline{ac}\$ be the hypotenuse. The magnitudes of \$a\$, \$b\$, and \$c\$ are just \$\frac{2}{3}\$ of the medians because the origin, or the centroid in this case, cuts the median in a ratio of \$2:1\$. So, \$|a|^2=\frac{4}{9}\cdot((\frac{x}{2})^2+y^2)=\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{4y^2}{9}\$ because \$|a|\$ is two thirds of the median from \$a\$. Similarly, \$|c|^2=\frac{4}{9}\cdot(x^2+(\frac{y}{2})^2)=\frac{4x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{9}\$. The median from \$b\$ is just half the hypotenuse because the median of any right triangle is just half the hypotenuse. So, \$|b|^2=\frac{4}{9}\cdot\frac{x^2+y^2}{4}=\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{9}\$. Hence, \$|a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2=\frac{6x^2+6y^2}{9}=\frac{2x^2+2y^2}{3}=250\$. Therefore, \$h^2=x^2+y^2=\frac{3}{2}\cdot250=\boxed{375}\$.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Define the complex numbers \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) as the zeros of the polynomial \(P(z) = z^3 + qz + r\). According to Vieta's formulas, we have \(a+b+c=0\).

Step 1: Since the centroid of the triangle formed by the points \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) is the average of the vertices, we find that the centroid is at the origin \(\frac{a+b+c}{3}=0\).

Step 2: Let one leg of the right triangle be \(x\) and the other leg be \(y\). Assume without loss of generality that the hypotenuse is represented by the segment \(\overline{ac}\).

Step 3: The magnitudes of the complex numbers \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) relate to the medians of the triangle. The centroid divides each median in a ratio of \(2:1\).

Step 4: Calculate \(|a|^2\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\): 
\[
|a|^2 = \frac{4}{9} \cdot \left( \left( \frac{x}{2} \right)^2 + y^2 \right) = \frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{4y^2}{9}.
\]

Step 5: Calculate \(|c|^2\) similarly:
\[
|c|^2 = \frac{4}{9} \cdot \left( x^2 + \left( \frac{y}{2} \right)^2 \right) = \frac{4x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{9}.
\]

Step 6: For \(|b|^2\), since \(b\) is the median from the hypotenuse, we have:
\[
|b|^2 = \frac{4}{9} \cdot \frac{x^2 + y^2}{4} = \frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{9}.
\]

Step 7: Combine all the squares:
\[
|a|^2 + |b|^2 + |c|^2 = \left( \frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{4y^2}{9} \right) + \left( \frac{4x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{9} \right) + \left( \frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{9} \right).
\]

Step 8: Simplify the expression:
\[
|a|^2 + |b|^2 + |c|^2 = \frac{6x^2 + 6y^2}{9} = \frac{2(x^2 + y^2)}{3}.
\]

Step 9: Given that \(|a|^2 + |b|^2 + |c|^2 = 250\), we set up the equation:
\[
\frac{2(x^2 + y^2)}{3} = 250.
\]

Step 10: Solve for \(x^2 + y^2\):
\[
2(x^2 + y^2) = 750 \implies x^2 + y^2 = 375.
\]

Step 11: Since \(h^2 = x^2 + y^2\), we find:
\[
h^2 = 375.
\]

Final answer: \(\boxed{375}\)

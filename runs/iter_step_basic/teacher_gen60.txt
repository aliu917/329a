Question: In \$\triangle ABC\$ let \$I\$ be the center of the inscribed circle, and let the bisector of \$\angle ACB\$ intersect \$AB\$ at \$L\$. The line through \$C\$ and \$L\$ intersects the circumscribed circle of \$\triangle ABC\$ at the two points \$C\$ and \$D\$. If \$LI=2\$ and \$LD=3\$, then \$IC=\tfrac{m}{n}\$, where \$m\$ and \$n\$ are relatively prime positive integers. Find \$m+n\$.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Suppose we label the angles as shown below.

As \$\angle BCD\$ and \$\angle BAD\$ intercept the same arc, we know that \$\angle BAD=\gamma\$. Similarly, \$\angle ABD=\gamma\$. Also, using \$\triangle ICA\$, we find \$\angle CIA=180-\alpha-\gamma\$. Therefore, \$\angle AID=\alpha+\gamma\$. Therefore, \$\angle DAI=\angle AID=\alpha+\gamma\$, so \$\triangle AID\$ must be isosceles with \$AD=ID=5\$. Similarly, \$BD=ID=5\$. Then \$\triangle DLB \sim \triangle ALC\$, hence \$\frac{AL}{AC} = \frac{3}{5}\$. Also, \$AI\$ bisects \$\angle LAC\$, so by the Angle Bisector Theorem \$\frac{CI}{IL} =\frac{AC}{AL}= \frac{5}{3}\$. Thus \$CI = \frac{10}{3}\$, and the answer is \$\boxed{013}\$.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Denote the angles in triangle \( \triangle ABC \) as \( \alpha = \angle CAB \), \( \beta = \angle ABC \), and \( \gamma = \angle ACB \). Note that \( I \) is the incenter, \( L \) is the intersection of the angle bisector of \( \angle ACB \) with line \( AB \), and \( D \) is a point on the circumcircle.

Step 1: Recognize that \( LI = 2 \) and \( LD = 3 \).

Step 2: Since \( \angle BCD \) and \( \angle BAD \) intercept the same arc, we conclude that \( \angle BAD = \gamma \).

Step 3: By the properties of angles in triangle \( \triangle AIC \), we have \( \angle CIA = 180 - \alpha - \gamma \).

Step 4: Consequently, we find \( \angle AID = \alpha + \gamma \).

Step 5: Since \( \angle DAI = \angle AID = \alpha + \gamma \), triangle \( \triangle AID \) is isosceles, thus \( AD = ID = 5 \) (since \( LD + LI = 3 + 2 = 5 \)).

Step 6: By the same reasoning, we find that \( BD = ID = 5 \).

Step 7: Apply the similarity of triangles \( \triangle DLB \sim \triangle ALC \). This gives us the proportion \( \frac{AL}{AC} = \frac{3}{5} \).

Step 8: Using the Angle Bisector Theorem on triangle \( AIL \), we find that \( \frac{CI}{IL} = \frac{AC}{AL} = \frac{5}{3} \).

Step 9: Set \( CI = x \) and \( IL = 2 \), leading to \( \frac{x}{2} = \frac{5}{3} \).

Step 10: Solve for \( x \) to find \( CI = \frac{10}{3} \).

Step 11: Since \( IC = CI \), we have \( IC = \frac{10}{3} \).

Step 12: Determine \( m \) and \( n \) in the fraction \( \frac{m}{n} = \frac{10}{3} \) as \( m = 10 \) and \( n = 3 \).

Step 13: Verify that 10 and 3 are relatively prime.

Final answer: \( m + n = 10 + 3 = \boxed{13} \).

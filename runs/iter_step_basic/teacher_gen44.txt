Question: Find the number of rational numbers \$r\$, \$0<r<1\$, such that when \$r\$ is written as a fraction in lowest terms, the numerator and the denominator have a sum of 1000.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: If the initial manipulation is not obvious, consider the Euclidean Algorithm. Instead of using \$\frac{n}{m}\$ as the fraction to use the Euclidean Algorithm on, we can rewrite this as \$\frac{500-x}{500+x}\$ or \gcd(500+x,500-x)=\gcd((500+x)+(500-x),500-x)=\gcd(1000,500-x). Thus, we want \$\gcd(1000,500-x)=1\$. You can either proceed as Solution \$1\$, or consider that no even numbers work, limiting us to \$250\$ choices of numbers and restricting \$x\$ to be odd. If \$x\$ is odd, \$500-x\$ is odd, so the only possible common factors \$1000\$ and \$500-x\$ can share are multiples of \$5\$. Thus, we want to avoid these. There are \$50\$ odd multiples of \$5\$ less than \$500\$, so the answer is \$250-50=\boxed{200}\$.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Define the variables: Let the numerator of the fraction be \( n \) and the denominator be \( m \), where the fraction is \( \frac{n}{m} \).

Step 1: The condition for the fraction is that \( 0 < r < 1 \), which implies \( n < m \).

Step 2: We know that \( n + m = 1000 \). Therefore, we can express \( m \) in terms of \( n \): \( m = 1000 - n \).

Step 3: Substitute \( m \) into the inequality: \( n < 1000 - n \).

Step 4: Rearranging gives \( 2n < 1000 \) or \( n < 500 \).

Step 5: Since \( n \) and \( m \) must be coprime (in lowest terms), we need \( \gcd(n, m) = 1 \).

Step 6: Using the relationship \( \gcd(n, m) = \gcd(n, 1000 - n) = \gcd(n, 1000) \).

Step 7: We want \( \gcd(n, 1000) = 1 \), meaning \( n \) must be coprime with \( 1000 \).

Step 8: The prime factorization of \( 1000 = 2^3 \times 5^3 \).

Step 9: Use the principle of inclusion-exclusion to count integers \( n < 500 \) that are coprime to \( 1000 \).

Step 10: Count the multiples of \( 2 \) and \( 5 \) less than \( 500 \):
- Multiples of \( 2 \): \( \lfloor \frac{499}{2} \rfloor = 249 \).
- Multiples of \( 5 \): \( \lfloor \frac{499}{5} \rfloor = 99 \).
- Multiples of \( 10 \) (common multiples): \( \lfloor \frac{499}{10} \rfloor = 49 \).

Step 11: Use inclusion-exclusion to find the count of numbers less than \( 500 \) that are multiples of \( 2 \) or \( 5 \):
\[
249 + 99 - 49 = 299.
\]

Step 12: The total count of integers \( n \) less than \( 500 \) is \( 499 \).

Step 13: Therefore, the count of integers \( n \) that are coprime to \( 1000 \) is:
\[
499 - 299 = 200.
\]

Final answer: \(\boxed{200}\)

Question: Two noncongruent integer-sided isosceles triangles have the same perimeter and the same area. The ratio of the lengths of the bases of the two triangles is \$8: 7\$. Find the minimum possible value of their common perimeter.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Let \$s\$ be the semiperimeter of the two triangles. Also, let the base of the longer triangle be \$16x\$ and the base of the shorter triangle be \$14x\$ for some arbitrary factor \$x\$. Then, the dimensions of the two triangles must be \$s-8x,s-8x,16x\$ and \$s-7x,s-7x,14x\$. By Heron's Formula, we have
<center>
\sqrt{s(8x)(8x)(s-16x)}=\sqrt{s(7x)(7x)(s-14x)}
8\sqrt{s-16x}=7\sqrt{s-14x}
64s-1024x=49s-686x
15s=338x
</center>
Since \$15\$ and \$338\$ are coprime, to minimize, we must have \$s=338\$ and \$x=15\$. However, we want the minimum perimeter. This means that we must multiply our minimum semiperimeter by \$2\$, which gives us a final answer of \$\boxed{676}\$.

--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Let \$s\$ be the semiperimeter of the two triangles. Denote the base of the longer triangle as \$16x\$ and the base of the shorter triangle as \$14x\$ for some arbitrary factor \$x\$.

Step 1: The dimensions of the two triangles must be \$s - 8x, s - 8x, 16x\$ for the longer triangle and \$s - 7x, s - 7x, 14x\$ for the shorter triangle.

Step 8: To find the minimum perimeter, we multiply the minimum semiperimeter \$s\$ by \$2\$ to get the total perimeter.

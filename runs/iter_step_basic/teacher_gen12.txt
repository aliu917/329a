Question: Jane is 25 years old. Dick is older than Jane. In \$n\$ years, where \$n\$ is a positive integer, Dick's age and Jane's age will both be two-digit numbers and will have the property that Jane's age is obtained by interchanging the digits of Dick's age. Let \$d\$ be Dick's present age. How many ordered pairs of positive integers \$(d,n)\$ are possible?

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Start by assuming that \$n < 5\$ (essentially, Jane is in the 20s when their ages are 'reverses' of each other). Then we get the pairs (61,1),(70,2),(79,3),(88,4). Repeating this for the 30s gives (34,9),(43,10),(52,11),(61,12),(70,13),(79,14). From here, it's pretty clear that every decade we go up we get \$(d,n+11)\$ as a pair. Since both ages must always be two-digit numbers, we can show that each decade after the 30s, we get 1 fewer option. Therefore, our answer is \$4+6+5+\dots+2+1=4+21=\boxed{025}.\$

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Assume Jane's current age is 25 years old and denote Dick's current age as \$d\$. 

Step 1: Determine the condition that Dick is older than Jane, so \$d > 25\$.

Step 2: Consider the future ages of Jane and Dick in \$n\$ years, where both must be two-digit numbers.

Step 3: Set up the expression for their ages in \$n\$ years: Jane's age will be \$25 + n\$ and Dick's age will be \$d + n\$.

Step 4: Since Jane's age is obtained by interchanging the digits of Dick's age, we can express this relationship as:
\[ 25 + n = 10b + a \]
\[ d + n = 10a + b \]
where \$a\$ and \$b\$ are the digits of Dick's age.

Step 5: Reorganize the equations to eliminate \$n\$:
\[ n = 10b + a - 25 \]
\[ n = 10a + b - d \]

Step 6: Set the two expressions for \$n\$ equal to each other:
\[ 10b + a - 25 = 10a + b - d \]

Step 7: Rearranging gives:
\[ 9b - 9a + d = 25 \]
or simplified:
\[ d = 25 + 9(a - b) \]

Step 8: Since Dick must be a two-digit number, set the bounds: \$10 \leq d < 100\$.

Step 9: Substitute \$d = 25 + 9(a - b)\$ into the inequality:
\[ 10 \leq 25 + 9(a - b) < 100 \]

Step 10: Solve the inequalities:
1. From \$10 \leq 25 + 9(a - b)\$:
   \[ 9(a - b) \geq -15 \implies a - b \geq -\frac{5}{3} \rightarrow a - b \geq 0 \text{ (as they are integers)} \]

2. From \$25 + 9(a - b) < 100\$:
   \[ 9(a - b) < 75 \implies a - b < \frac{75}{9} \approx 8.33 \rightarrow a - b \leq 8 \text{ (as they are integers)} \]

Step 11: Thus, \$0 \leq a - b \leq 8\$.

Step 12: Determine the possible values of \$a\$, which must be digits (0-9).
- If \$a - b = k\$, then \$b = a - k\$. 

Step 13: For each \$k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, 8\$, evaluate the possible values of \$a\$ and \$b\$ such that both are digits.

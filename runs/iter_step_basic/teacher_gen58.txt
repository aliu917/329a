Question: In triangle \$ABC\$, \$AB=125\$, \$AC=117\$ and \$BC=120\$. The angle bisector of angle \$A\$ intersects \$ \overline{BC} \$ at point \$L\$, and the angle bisector of angle \$B\$ intersects \$ \overline{AC} \$ at point \$K\$. Let \$M\$ and \$N\$ be the feet of the perpendiculars from \$C\$ to \$ \overline{BK}\$ and \$ \overline{AL}\$, respectively. Find \$MN\$.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Extend \${CM}\$ and \${CN}\$ such that they intersect line \${AB}\$ at points \$P\$ and \$Q\$, respectively.

Since \${BM}\$ is the angle bisector of angle \$B\$ and \${CM}\$ is perpendicular to \${BM}\$, \$\triangle BCP\$ must be an isoceles triangle, so \$BP=BC=120\$, and \$M\$ is the midpoint of \${CP}\$. For the same reason, \$AQ=AC=117\$, and \$N\$ is the midpoint of \${CQ}\$.
Hence \$MN=\tfrac 12 PQ\$. Since PQ=BP+AQ-AB=120+117-125=112, so \$MN=\boxed{056}\$.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Given triangle \(ABC\) with sides \(AB=125\), \(AC=117\), and \(BC=120\), identify relevant points \(L\) and \(K\) as the intersections of the angle bisectors with segments \(BC\) and \(AC\) respectively.

Step 1: Extend lines \(CM\) and \(CN\) until they intersect line \(AB\) at points \(P\) and \(Q\), respectively.

Step 2: Since \(BM\) is the angle bisector of angle \(B\) and \(CM\) is perpendicular to \(BM\), triangle \(BCP\) is isosceles, meaning \(BP=BC=120\) and \(M\) is the midpoint of segment \(CP\).

Step 3: Similarly, since \(AQ\) is the angle bisector of angle \(A\) and \(CN\) is perpendicular to \(AQ\), triangle \(ACQ\) is isosceles, so \(AQ=AC=117\) and \(N\) is the midpoint of segment \(CQ\).

Step 4: Calculate the distance \(MN\) as half the distance \(PQ\), where \(PQ = BP + AQ - AB\).

Step 5: Substitute the values: \(PQ = 120 + 117 - 125 = 112\).

Step 6: Since \(MN = \frac{1}{2} PQ\), we find \(MN = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 112 = 56\).

Final answer: \(\boxed{056}\)

### Now, apply what you have learned:

Question: Let \$x,y,\$ and \$z\$ be real numbers satisfying the system of equations
\begin{align*}
xy + 4z &= 60 \\
yz + 4x &= 60 \\
zx + 4y &= 60.
\end{align*}
Let \$S\$ be the set of possible values of \$x.\$ Find the sum of the squares of the elements of \$S.\$

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: We first subtract the second equation from the first, noting that they both equal \$60\$.
\begin{align*}
xy+4z-yz-4x&=0 \\
4(z-x)-y(z-x)&=0 \\
(z-x)(4-y)&=0
\end{align*}
Case 1: Let \$y=4\$.
The first and third equations simplify to:
\begin{align*}
x+z&=15 \\
xz&=44
\end{align*}
from which it is apparent that \$x=4\$ and \$x=11\$ are solutions.
Case 2: Let \$x=z\$.
The first and third equations simplify to:
\begin{align*}
xy+4x&=60 \\
x^2+4y&=60
\end{align*}
We subtract the following equations, yielding:
\begin{align*}
x^2+4y-xy-4x&=0 \\
x(x-4)-y(x-4)&=0 \\
(x-4)(x-y)&=0
\end{align*}
We thus have \$x=4\$ and \$x=y\$, substituting in \$x=y=z\$ and solving yields \$x=6\$ and \$x=-10\$.
Then, we just add the squares of the solutions (make sure not to double count the \$4\$), and get 4^2+11^2+(-6)^2+10^2=16+121+36+100=\boxed{273}.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Define the real numbers as \$x\$, \$y\$, and \$z\$ satisfying the given system of equations.

Step 1: Subtract the second equation from the first equation to derive a new equation: \[xy + 4z - (yz + 4x) = 0\].

Step 2: Rearrange the new equation: \[xy - yz + 4z - 4x = 0\].

Step 3: Factor out common terms: \[(z - x)(4 - y) = 0\].

Step 4: Analyze Case 1: Assume \$y = 4\$.

Step 5: Substitute \$y = 4\$ into the first and third original equations: 
\[
x + z = 15 \\
xz = 44.
\]

Step 6: Solve the equations from Case 1. From \$x + z = 15\$, we can express \$z\$ as \$z = 15 - x\$.

Step 7: Substitute \$z\$ into \$xz = 44\$ to get: 
\[
x(15 - x) = 44.
\]

Step 8: Rearrange and form a quadratic equation: 
\[
-x^2 + 15x - 44 = 0 \rightarrow x^2 - 15x + 44 = 0.
\]

Step 9: Use the quadratic formula to find the roots: 
\[
x = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{15^2 - 4 \cdot 44}}{2} = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{225 - 176}}{2} = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{49}}{2} = \frac{15 \pm 7}{2}.
\]

Step 10: Solve for possible values of \$x\$ in Case 1: 
\[
x = 11 \quad \text{and} \quad x = 4.
\]

Step 11: Analyze Case 2: Assume \$x = z\$.

Step 12: Substitute \$x = z\$ into the first and third equations:
\[
xy + 4x = 60 \\
x^2 + 4y = 60.
\]

Step 13: Subtract the equations: 
\[
x^2 + 4y - (xy + 4x) = 0 \rightarrow x^2 - xy + 4y - 4x = 0.
\]

Step 14: Factor the new equation: 
\[
(x - 4)(x - y) = 0.
\]

Step 15: Analyze the two scenarios from Case 2: Set \$x = 4\$ and \$x = y\$.

Step 16: From \$x = 4\$, we already have this solution.

Step 17: From \$x = y\$, substitute into \$x = y = z\$ in any equation (use the first):
\[
x^2 + 4x = 60.
\]

Step 18: Rearrange and form the quadratic: 
\[
x^2 + 4x - 60 = 0.
\]

Step 19: Use the quadratic formula again: 
\[
x = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{4^2 + 4 \cdot 60}}{2} = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 240}}{2} = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{256}}{2} = \frac{-4 \pm 16}{2}.
\]

Step 20: Solve for possible values of \$x\$ in Case 2:
\[
x = 6 \quad \text{and} \quad x = -10.
\]

Step 21: Collect all distinct values of \$x\$ found: \$4\$, \$11\$, \$6\$, and \$-10\$.

Step 22: Calculate the sum of the squares of the distinct values: 
\[
4^2 + 11^2 + 6^2 + (-10)^2 = 16 + 121 + 36 + 100.
\]

Step 23: Sum the squares: 
\[
16 + 121 = 137, \quad 137 + 36 = 173, \quad 173 + 100 = 273.
\]

Final answer: \(\boxed{273}\)

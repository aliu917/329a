Question: Three runners start running simultaneously from the same point on a 500-meter circular track. They each run clockwise around the course maintaining constant speeds of 4.4, 4.8, and 5.0 meters per second. The runners stop once they are all together again somewhere on the circular course. How many seconds do the runners run?
\$ \textbf{(A)}\ 1,000\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 1,250\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 2,500\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 5,000\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 10,000 \$

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: First consider the first two runners. The faster runner will lap the slower runner exactly once, or run 500 meters farther. Let \$x\$ be the time these runners run in seconds.
4.8x-4.4x=500 \Rightarrow x=1250
Because \$4.4(1250)=5500\$ is a multiple of 500, it turns out they just meet back at the start line.
Now we must find a time that is a multiple of \$1250\$ and results in the 5.0 m/s runner to end up on the start line. Every \$1250\$ seconds, that fastest runner goes \$5.0(1250)=6250\$ meters. In \$2(1250)=2500\$ seconds, he goes \$5.0(2500)=12500\$ meters. Therefore the runners run \$\boxed{\textbf{(C)}\ 2,500}\$ seconds.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Define the speeds of the three runners: Runner 1 runs at 4.4 m/s, Runner 2 runs at 4.8 m/s, and Runner 3 runs at 5.0 m/s. Define the circular track length as 500 meters.

Step 1: Consider the first two runners (4.4 m/s and 4.8 m/s). The faster runner (4.8 m/s) will lap the slower runner (4.4 m/s) once they have run 500 meters more than the slower runner.

Step 2: Set up the equation for the time \(x\) in seconds it takes for the faster runner to lap the slower one: \(4.8x - 4.4x = 500\).

Step 3: Simplify the equation: \(0.4x = 500\).

Step 4: Solve for \(x\): \(x = \frac{500}{0.4} = 1250\) seconds. 

Step 5: Check if Runner 1 (4.4 m/s) has returned to the starting point after 1250 seconds: \(4.4 \times 1250 = 5500\) meters, which is a multiple of 500 (specifically, 11 laps around the track).

Step 6: Now check if Runner 3 (5.0 m/s) also returns to the starting point after 1250 seconds: \(5.0 \times 1250 = 6250\) meters, which is also a multiple of 500 (specifically, 12.5 laps around the track).

Step 7: Find the smallest time that is a common multiple of 1250 seconds and ensures Runner 3 also ends up at the start line. Every 1250 seconds, Runner 3 runs a distance of \(5.0 \times 1250 = 6250\) meters.

Step 8: Since 6250 meters is not an integer multiple of 500 that results in the runner being at the start line, we find the least time \(n\) such that \(5.0n\) is a multiple of 500. This can be determined by ensuring \(n\) is a multiple of 250 seconds (since \(500/5.0 = 100\) laps).

Step 9: The least common multiple of the times it takes for them to meet again is \(2 \times 1250 = 2500\) seconds (as this is the first instance when all runners meet at the start line).

Final answer: \(\boxed{2500}\) seconds.

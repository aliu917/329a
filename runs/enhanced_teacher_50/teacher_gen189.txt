Question: If the integer \$k\$ is added to each of the numbers \$36\$, \$300\$, and \$596\$, one obtains the squares of three consecutive terms of an arithmetic series. Find \$k\$.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Call the terms of the  \$a,\ a + d,\ a + 2d\$, making their squares \$a^2,\ a^2 + 2ad + d^2,\ a^2 + 4ad + 4d^2\$.
We know that \$a^2 = 36 + k\$ and \$(a + d)^2 = 300 + k\$, and subtracting these two we get \$264 = 2ad + d^2\$ (1). Similarly, using \$(a + d)^2 = 300 + k\$ and \$(a + 2d)^2 = 596 + k\$, subtraction yields \$296 = 2ad + 3d^2\$ (2).
Subtracting the first equation from the second, we get \$2d^2 = 32\$, so \$d = 4\$. Substituting backwards yields that \$a = 31\$ and \$k = \boxed{925}\$.

Since terms in an arithmetic progression have constant differences,
\sqrt{300+k}-\sqrt{36+k}=\sqrt{596+k}-\sqrt{300+k}
\implies 2\sqrt{300+k} = \sqrt{596+k}+\sqrt{36+k}
\implies 4k+1200=596+k+36+k+2\sqrt{(596+k)(36+k)}
\implies 2k+568=2\sqrt{(596+k)(36+k)}

Step 0: Denote the terms of the arithmetic series as \(a\), \(a + d\), and \(a + 2d\). The squares of these terms are \(a^2\), \((a + d)^2\), and \((a + 2d)^2\).

Step 1: Set up the equations based on the problem statement: \(a^2 = 36 + k\) and \((a + d)^2 = 300 + k\).

Step 2: Subtract the first equation from the second to find a relationship between \(a\) and \(d\):
\[
(a + d)^2 - a^2 = (300 + k) - (36 + k) \implies 264 = 2ad + d^2 \quad \text{(1)}
\]

Step 3: Now set up the third equation using \((a + d)^2 = 300 + k\) and \((a + 2d)^2 = 596 + k\):
\[
(a + 2d)^2 - (a + d)^2 = (596 + k) - (300 + k) \implies 296 = 2ad + 3d^2 \quad \text{(2)}
\]

Step 4: Subtract equation (1) from equation (2) to isolate \(d\):
\[
(296 - 264) = (2ad + 3d^2) - (2ad + d^2) \implies 32 = 2d^2
\]

Step 5: Solve for \(d\):
\[
d^2 = 16 \implies d = 4
\]

Step 6: Substitute \(d = 4\) back into equation (1) to find \(a\):
\[
264 = 2a(4) + 4^2 \implies 264 = 8a + 16 \implies 248 = 8a \implies a = 31

\[
31^2 = 36 + k \implies 961 = 36 + k \implies k = 961 - 36 = 925

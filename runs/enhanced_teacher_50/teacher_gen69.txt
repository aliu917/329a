Question: In 1991 the population of a town was a perfect square. Ten years later, after an increase of 150 people, the population was 9 more than a perfect square. Now, in 2011, with an increase of another 150 people, the population is once again a perfect square. Which of the following is closest to the percent growth of the town's population during this twenty-year period?
\$ \textbf{(A)}\ 42 \qquad\textbf{(B)}\ 47 \qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 52\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 57\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 62 \$

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Proceed through the difference of squares for \$p\$ and \$q\$:
\$141=q^2-p^2=(q-p)(q+p)\$
However, instead of testing both pairs of factors we take a more certain approach. Here \$r^2\$ is the population of the town in 2011.
\$r^2-p^2=300\$
\$(r-p)(r+p)=300\$
Test through pairs of \$r\$ and \$p\$ that makes sure \$p=22\$ or \$p=70\$.
Then go through the same routine as demonstrated above to finish this problem.
Note that this approach might take more testing if one is not familiar with finding factors.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Let the population of the town in 1991 be denoted as \( p^2 \), where \( p \) is a positive integer.

Step 1: In 2001, the population increases by 150 people, so the population is \( p^2 + 150 \).

Step 2: According to the problem, in 2001, the population is 9 more than a perfect square. So, we can express this as \( p^2 + 150 = q^2 + 9 \) for some integer \( q \).

Step 3: Rearranging gives us \( q^2 = p^2 + 141 \). This can be factored as \( q^2 - p^2 = 141 \), which can be expressed as \( (q - p)(q + p) = 141 \).

Step 4: Next, we factor 141 to find pairs \( (a, b) \) such that \( ab = 141 \). The factor pairs of 141 are \( (1, 141) \), \( (3, 47) \).

Step 5: For each factor pair \( (a, b) \):
- Set \( q - p = a \) and \( q + p = b \).
- Solve for \( q \) and \( p \) using the equations:
  - \( q = \frac{a + b}{2} \)
  - \( p = \frac{b - a}{2} \)

Step 6: Testing the factor pair \( (3, 47) \):
- \( q - p = 3 \) and \( q + p = 47 \) leads to:
  - \( q = \frac{3 + 47}{2} = 25 \)
  - \( p = \frac{47 - 3}{2} = 22 \)

Step 7: Now, we find the population in 1991: \( p^2 = 22^2 = 484 \).

Step 8: In 2011, the population increases again by 150: \( 484 + 150 + 150 = 784 \).

Step 9: Since \( 784 = 28^2 \), the population in 2011 is indeed a perfect square.

Step 10: Calculate the percent growth over the 20-year period:
- The initial population in 1991 was \( 484 \) and the final population in 2011 is \( 784 \).
- Percent growth = \(\frac{784 - 484}{484} \times 100\).

Step 11: Calculate the difference: \( 784 - 484 = 300 \).

Step 12: Calculate the percent growth: \(\frac{300}{484} \times 100 \approx 61.93\).

Step 13: Rounding gives us approximately \( 62 \).

Final answer: \(\boxed{62}\)

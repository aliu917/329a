Question: One of Euler's conjectures was disproved in the 1960s by three American mathematicians when they showed there was a positive integer such that 133^5+110^5+84^5+27^5=n^{5}. Find the value of \$n\$.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Note that \$n\$ is even, since the LHS consists of two odd and two even numbers. By Fermat's Little Theorem, we know \$n^5\equiv n\pmod{5}.\$ Hence, n\equiv3+0+4+2\equiv4\pmod{5}.
Continuing, we examine the equation modulo \$3,\$ n\equiv1-1+0+0\equiv0\pmod{3}.
Thus, \$n\$ is divisible by three and leaves a remainder of four when divided by \$5.\$ It is obvious that \$n>133,\$ so the only possibilities are \$n = 144\$ or \$n \geq 174.\$ It quickly becomes apparent that \$174\$ is much too large, so \$n\$ must be \$\boxed{144}.\$

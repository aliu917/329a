Since \$A = (u,v)\$, we can find the coordinates of the other points: \$B = (v,u)\$, \$C = (-v,u)\$, \$D = (-v,-u)\$, \$E = (v,-u)\$. If we graph those points, we notice that since the latter four points are all reflected across the x/y-axis, they form a rectangle, and \$ABE\$ is a triangle. The area of \$BCDE\$ is \$(2u)(2v) = 4uv\$ and the area of \$ABE\$ is \$\frac{1}{2}(2u)(u-v) = u^2 - uv\$. Adding these together, we get \$u^2 + 3uv = u(u+3v) = 451 = 11 \cdot 41\$. Since \$u,v\$ are positive, \$u+3v>u\$, and by matching factors we get either \$(u,v) = (1,150)\$ or \$(11,10)\$. Since \$v < u\$ the latter case is the answer, and \$u+v = \boxed{021}\$.
=

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Denote the coordinates of point A as \( A = (u,v) \).

Step 1: Find the coordinates of point B, which is the reflection of A across the line \( y = x \), giving \( B = (v,u) \).

Step 2: Find the coordinates of point C, which is the reflection of B across the y-axis, resulting in \( C = (-v,u) \).

Step 3: Find the coordinates of point D, which is the reflection of C across the x-axis, yielding \( D = (-v,-u) \).

Step 4: Find the coordinates of point E, which is the reflection of D across the y-axis, giving \( E = (v,-u) \).

Step 5: Recognize that points B, C, D, and E form a rectangle with width \( 2u \) and height \( 2v \). Therefore, the area of rectangle BCDE is \( 4uv \).

Step 6: Determine the area of triangle ABE. The base is \( 2u \) and the height is \( u - v \). The area of triangle ABE is given by \( \frac{1}{2}(2u)(u-v) = u^2 - uv \).

Step 7: Combine the areas of rectangle BCDE and triangle ABE to get the total area:
\[
\text{Total Area} = \text{Area of BCDE} + \text{Area of ABE} = 4uv + (u^2 - uv) = u^2 + 3uv.
\]

Step 8: Set the total area equal to 451:
\[
u^2 + 3uv = 451.
\]

Step 9: Rearrange the equation to factor:
\[
u(u + 3v) = 451.
\]

Step 10: Factor 451 into its prime factors: \( 451 = 11 \cdot 41 \).

Step 11: Consider the pairs of factors \( (1, 451), (11, 41), (41, 11), (451, 1) \) as possible values for \( (u, u + 3v) \).

Step 12: Analyze the pairs to find valid \( (u, v) \) such that \( u < u + 3v \) and both \( u \) and \( v \) are positive.

Step 13: For the pair \( (11, 41) \), we can set \( u = 11 \) and \( u + 3v = 41 \):
\[
3v = 41 - 11 = 30 \implies v = 10.
\]

Step 14: Confirm that \( v < u \) holds, as \( 10 < 11 \).

Step 15: Calculate \( u + v = 11 + 10 = 21 \).

Final answer: \(\boxed{21}\)

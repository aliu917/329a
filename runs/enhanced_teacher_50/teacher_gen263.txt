Question: Given that \$x\$ and \$y\$ are distinct nonzero real numbers such that \$x+\tfrac{2}{x} = y + \tfrac{2}{y}\$, what is \$xy\$?
\$ \textbf{(A)}\ \frac{1}{4}\qquad\textbf{(B)}\ \frac{1}{2}\qquad\textbf{(C)}\ 1\qquad\textbf{(D)}\ 2\qquad\textbf{(E)}\ 4\qquad \$

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: \$ x+\tfrac{2}{x}= y+\tfrac{2}{y} \$
Since \$x\not=y\$, we may assume that \$x=\frac{2}{y}\$ and/or, equivalently, \$y=\frac{2}{x}\$.
Cross multiply in either equation, giving us \$xy=2\$.
\$\boxed{\textbf{(D) }{2}}\$

Step 3: Substitute \( x = \frac{2}{y} \) into the equation to find a relationship between \( x \) and \( y \).

Step 4: Cross multiply to eliminate the fractions: \( xy = 2 \).

Step 5: Since \( x \) and \( y \) are distinct, confirm that the derived equation holds true with the condition given.

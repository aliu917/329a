Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: The area of the equilateral triangle is \$\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\$. The area of the three squares is \$3\times 1=3\$.
Since \$\angle C=360\$, \$\angle GCH=360-90-90-60=120\$.
Dropping an altitude from \$C\$ to \$GH\$ allows to create a \$30-60-90\$ triangle since \$\triangle GCH\$ is isosceles. This means that the height of \$\triangle GCH\$ is \$\dfrac{1}{2}\$ and half the length of \$GH\$ is \$\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\$. Therefore, the area of each isosceles triangle is \$\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\$. Multiplying by \$3\$ yields \$\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}\$ for all three isosceles triangles.
Therefore, the total area is \$3+\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}+\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}=3+\dfrac{4\sqrt{3}}{4}=3+\sqrt{3}\implies\boxed{\textbf{(C)}\ 3+\sqrt3}\$.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Calculate the area of the equilateral triangle \( \triangle ABC \) with side length \( 1 \), which is given by the formula \( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \).

Step 1: Calculate the area of the three squares built on the sides of the triangle. Since each square has a side length of \( 1 \), the area of each square is \( 1^2 = 1 \). Therefore, the total area of the three squares is \( 3 \times 1 = 3 \).

Step 2: Determine the angle \( \angle C \) at vertex \( C \) of the hexagon formed by the squares and the triangle. The total angle around point \( C \) is \( 360^\circ \). The angles from the two squares at \( C \) are \( 90^\circ \) each (from squares \( BCHI \) and \( CAFG \)), and the angle from the triangle is \( 60^\circ \). Therefore, \( \angle GCH = 360 - 90 - 90 - 60 = 120^\circ \).

Step 3: To find the height from point \( C \) to base \( GH \) in triangle \( GCH \), drop a perpendicular from \( C \) to line \( GH \). Since triangle \( GCH \) is isosceles with \( \angle GCH = 120^\circ \), the resulting triangle can be divided into two \( 30-60-90 \) triangles.

Step 4: In a \( 30-60-90 \) triangle, if the shorter leg (half the base of triangle \( GCH \)) is \( \frac{1}{2} \) (half the side length of the triangle), then the height (longer leg) is \( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \).

Step 5: The area of triangle \( GCH \) can be calculated as:
\[
\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times 1 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}.
\]

Step 6: Since there are three such isosceles triangles (one at each vertex of the triangle), multiply the area of one triangle by \( 3 \):
\[
\text{Total area of isosceles triangles} = 3 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}.
\]

Step 7: Add the area of the equilateral triangle and the area contribution from the squares and the isosceles triangles:
\[
\text{Total area} = 3 + \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} = 3 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} + \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} = 3 + \sqrt{3}.
\]

Final answer: \(\boxed{3 + \sqrt{3}}\)

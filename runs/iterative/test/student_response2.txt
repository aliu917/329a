Let's denote the two numbers by \( x \) and \( y \) where \( x > y \). We are given two pieces of information:
1. The sum of the two numbers is 6.
2. The difference of their squares is 12.

We can write these as equations:
\[ x + y = 6 \]
\[ x^2 - y^2 = 12 \]

We know that the difference of squares can be factored as:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y) \]

Substituting the first equation into this factorization, we get:
\[ (x + y)(x - y) = 12 \]
\[ 6(x - y) = 12 \]

To find \( x - y \), we divide both sides of the equation by 6:
\[ x - y = 2 \]

We now have a system of linear equations:
\[ x + y = 6 \]
\[ x - y = 2 \]

We can solve this system by adding the two equations together:
\[ (x + y) + (x - y) = 6 + 2 \]
\[ 2x = 8 \]
\[ x = 4 \]

Next, we substitute \( x = 4 \) back into the first equation to find \( y \):
\[ 4 + y = 6 \]
\[ y = 2 \]

The positive difference between the two numbers is:
\[ x - y = 4 - 2 = 2 \]

Thus, the positive difference of the two numbers is \(\boxed{2}\).

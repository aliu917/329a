Let's denote the two numbers by \( x \) and \( y \), where \( x > y \).

We are given two pieces of information:
1. The sum of the two numbers is 6:
   \[
   x + y = 6
   \]
2. The difference of their squares is 12:
   \[
   x^2 - y^2 = 12
   \]

First, recall the identity for the difference of squares:
\[
x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y)
\]

Using the first equation, we substitute \( x + y = 6 \) into the difference of squares identity:
\[
(x + y)(x - y) = 12 \implies 6(x - y) = 12
\]

Next, solve for \( x - y \):
\[
x - y = \frac{12}{6} = 2
\]

Thus, the positive difference of the two numbers is:
\[
\boxed{2}
\]

To ensure completeness, let's verify the solution:
1. We have \( x + y = 6 \) and \( x - y = 2 \).
2. Adding these two equations:
   \[
   (x + y) + (x - y) = 6 + 2 \implies 2x = 8 \implies x = 4
   \]
3. Subtracting the second equation from the first:
   \[
   (x + y) - (x - y) = 6 - 2 \implies 2y = 4 \implies y = 2
   \]

Therefore, the two numbers are \( x = 4 \) and \( y = 2 \). The positive difference is indeed:
\[
4 - 2 = 2
\]

The answer is confirmed to be correct: \(\boxed{2}\).

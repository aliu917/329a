Let's denote the two numbers by \(x\) and \(y\), where \(x > y\). We are given two pieces of information:
1. The sum of the two numbers is 6.
2. The difference of their squares is 12.

We can write these as the following equations:
\[ x + y = 6 \]
\[ x^2 - y^2 = 12 \]

We know from algebra that the difference of squares can be factored as:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y) \]

Substituting the given sum \(x + y = 6\) into the factored form of the difference of squares, we get:
\[ 12 = 6(x - y) \]

Now, we can solve for \(x - y\):
\[ x - y = \frac{12}{6} = 2 \]

Thus, the positive difference of the two numbers is:
\[\boxed{2}\]

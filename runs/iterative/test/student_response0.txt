Let's denote the two numbers as \( x \) and \( y \). We are given two pieces of information:
1. The sum of the two numbers is 6.
2. The difference of their squares is 12.

We can write these as equations:
\[ x + y = 6 \]
\[ x^2 - y^2 = 12 \]

First, recall the identity for the difference of squares:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y) \]

Substitute the sum \( x + y = 6 \) into the difference of squares equation:
\[ (x + y)(x - y) = 12 \]
\[ 6(x - y) = 12 \]

To find \( x - y \), divide both sides by 6:
\[ x - y = \frac{12}{6} \]
\[ x - y = 2 \]

Now we have a system of linear equations:
1. \( x + y = 6 \)
2. \( x - y = 2 \)

To solve this system, add the two equations together:
\[ (x + y) + (x - y) = 6 + 2 \]
\[ 2x = 8 \]
\[ x = 4 \]

Next, substitute \( x = 4 \) back into the first equation:
\[ 4 + y = 6 \]
\[ y = 2 \]

The two numbers are \( x = 4 \) and \( y = 2 \). The positive difference between these two numbers is:
\[ |x - y| = |4 - 2| = 2 \]

Thus, the positive difference of the two numbers is \(\boxed{2}\).

Let's denote the two numbers as \(x\) and \(y\), where \(x > y\). We are given two pieces of information:

1. The sum of the two numbers is 6:
   \[
   x + y = 6
   \]

2. The difference of their squares is 12:
   \[
   x^2 - y^2 = 12
   \]

We can use the identity for the difference of squares:
\[
x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y)
\]

Substituting the given sum into the identity, we get:
\[
(x + y)(x - y) = 12 \implies 6(x - y) = 12
\]

Solving for \(x - y\), we divide both sides by 6:
\[
x - y = 2
\]

Thus, the positive difference of the two numbers is:
\[
\boxed{2}
\]

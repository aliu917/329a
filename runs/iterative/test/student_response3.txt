Let's denote the two numbers by \(x\) and \(y\), where \(x > y\).

We are given two pieces of information:
1. The sum of the two numbers is 6.
2. The difference of their squares is 12.

We can express these pieces of information as the following equations:
1. \(x + y = 6\)
2. \(x^2 - y^2 = 12\)

We can use the identity for the difference of squares, which states \(x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y)\). Substituting the first equation into this identity, we get:
\[x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y) = 6(x - y)\]

We know from the second equation that \(x^2 - y^2 = 12\), so we can substitute this in:
\[6(x - y) = 12\]

To find \(x - y\), we divide both sides of the equation by 6:
\[x - y = \frac{12}{6} = 2\]

Thus, the positive difference of the two numbers is \(\boxed{2}\).

To verify, we can solve for \(x\) and \(y\) using the equations \(x + y = 6\) and \(x - y = 2\). Adding these two equations, we get:
\[2x = 8 \implies x = 4\]

Subtracting the second equation from the first, we get:
\[2y = 4 \implies y = 2\]

So, the two numbers are 4 and 2. Checking the conditions:
1. \(4 + 2 = 6\)
2. \(4^2 - 2^2 = 16 - 4 = 12\)

Both conditions are satisfied, confirming that the positive difference of the two numbers is indeed \(\boxed{2}\).

Let's denote the two numbers by \(x\) and \(y\). We are given two pieces of information:

1. The sum of the two numbers is 6:
   \[
   x + y = 6
   \]

2. The difference of their squares is 12:
   \[
   x^2 - y^2 = 12
   \]

We can use the identity for the difference of squares, which states that \(x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y)\). Substituting the given sum into this identity, we get:
\[
(x + y)(x - y) = 12
\]
Since \(x + y = 6\), we can substitute 6 for \(x + y\):
\[
6(x - y) = 12
\]
To find \(x - y\), we divide both sides of the equation by 6:
\[
x - y = \frac{12}{6} = 2
\]

Thus, the positive difference of the two numbers is \(\boxed{2}\).

To solve the problem, we start by labeling the vertices of the hexagon as \(A, B, C, D, E, F\) in order such that \(AB = 31\) and \(BC = CD = DE = EF = FA = 81\). The hexagon is inscribed in a circle, so it is a cyclic hexagon. We are asked to find the sum of the lengths of the three diagonals that can be drawn from \(A\), which are \(AD\), \(AE\), and \(AF\).

First, we apply Ptolemy's theorem to the cyclic quadrilaterals \(ABDE\), \(ABEF\), and \(ABCF\).

For quadrilateral \(ABDE\):
\[ AB \cdot DE + AE \cdot BD = AD \cdot BE \]
\[ 31 \cdot 81 + AE \cdot BD = AD \cdot 81 \]
\[ 2511 + AE \cdot BD = 81AD \quad \text{(1)} \]

For quadrilateral \(ABEF\):
\[ AB \cdot EF + AF \cdot BE = AE \cdot BF \]
\[ 31 \cdot 81 + AF \cdot 81 = AE \cdot 81 \]
\[ 2511 + 81AF = 81AE \]
\[ 2511 + 81AF = 81AE \]
\[ 2511 + 81AF = 81AE \quad \text{(2)} \]

For quadrilateral \(ABCF\):
\[ AB \cdot CF + AC \cdot BF = AF \cdot BC \]
\[ 31 \cdot 81 + AC \cdot 81 = AF \cdot 81 \]
\[ 2511 + 81AC = 81AF \]
\[ 2511 + 81AC = 81AF \quad \text{(3)} \]

Since \(AC\) is a chord, we can use the fact that the hexagon is symmetric and the circle is the circumcircle. By symmetry, the lengths of the diagonals \(AD\), \(AE\), and \(AF\) are equal. Let \(AD = AE = AF = x\). Then equation (1) simplifies to:
\[ 2511 + BD \cdot AE = 81x \]
\[ 2511 + BD \cdot x = 81x \]
\[ 2511 = 81x - BDx \]
\[ 2511 = x(81 - BD) \]
\[ BD = \frac{2511}{x} \]

Similarly, from equations (2) and (3), we get:
\[ AE = AF = x \]
\[ 2511 + 81x = 81x \]
\[ 2511 = 0 \]

Since \(BD = \frac{2511}{x}\), we substitute back to find \(x\):
\[ x = 81 \]

Therefore, the sum of the lengths of the three diagonals from \(A\) is:
\[ AD + AE + AF = 81 + 81 + 81 = 243 \]

The final answer is:
\[
\boxed{243}

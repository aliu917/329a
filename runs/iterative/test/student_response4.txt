Let the two numbers be \( x \) and \( y \) such that:
\[ x + y = 6 \]
\[ x^2 - y^2 = 12 \]

We start by using the algebraic identity for the difference of squares:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y) \]

Substitute \( x + y = 6 \) into the identity:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = 6(x - y) \]

We know from the problem statement that:
\[ x^2 - y^2 = 12 \]

So, we can set up the equation:
\[ 6(x - y) = 12 \]

Solve for \( x - y \):
\[ x - y = \frac{12}{6} = 2 \]

Thus, the positive difference of the two numbers is:
\[ \boxed{2} \]

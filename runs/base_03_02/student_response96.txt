\[
(10x+2)(10x+6)(5x+5)
\]
We can factor out the common factors from each term:
\[
(10x+2) = 2(5x+1)
\]
\[
(10x+6) = 2(5x+3)

\[
(5x+5) = 5(x+1)
\]
So the expression becomes:
\[
2(5x+1) \cdot 2(5x+3) \cdot 5(x+1) = 20(5x+1)(5x+3)(x+1)
\]

   Since \(x\) is odd, \(x+1\) is even. Therefore, \((x+1)\) is always divisible by 2. This means the expression is always divisible by \(20 \times 2 = 40\).

Therefore, one of \(5x+1\), \(5x+3\), or \(x+1\) must be divisible by 3. This means the expression is always divisible by \(40 \times 3 = 120\).

3. **Divisibility by 5:**
   Among any five consecutive integers, one of them is divisible by 5. Since \(x\) is odd, \(5x+1\), \(5x+3\), and \(x+1\) are not necessarily consecutive, but one of them must be divisible by 5. This means the expression is always divisible by \(120 \times 5 = 600\).

To solve the problem, we need to find the length of segment \( MC \) where \( M \) is the midpoint of the arc \( AB \) of a semicircle and \( A \) and \( B \) are the endpoints of the diameter of the semicircle. The length of \( AB \) is given as 6 cm, so the radius of the semicircle is \( 3 \) cm.

1. **Position the semicircle and square:**
   - Place the semicircle in the coordinate plane with \( A = (0, 0) \) and \( B = (6, 0) \).
   - The center of the semicircle is at \( (3, 0) \).
   - The radius of the semicircle is \( 3 \) cm.
   - Square \( ABCD \) has vertices \( A = (0, 0) \), \( B = (6, 0) \), \( C = (6, -6) \), and \( D = (0, -6) \).

2. **Determine the coordinates of \( M \):**
   - \( M \) is the midpoint of the arc \( AB \), which means \( M \) lies on the vertical line through the center of the semicircle at \( (3, 0) \) and is at a height of \( 3 \) cm above the diameter.
   - Therefore, the coordinates of \( M \) are \( (3, 3) \).

3. **Calculate the length of segment \( MC \):**
   - The coordinates of \( M \) are \( (3, 3) \).
   - The coordinates of \( C \) are \( (6, -6) \).
   - Use the distance formula to find the distance between \( M \) and \( C \):
     \[
     MC = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
     \]
     Substituting the coordinates of \( M \) and \( C \):
     \[
     MC = \sqrt{(6 - 3)^2 + (-6 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{3^2 + (-9)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 81} = \sqrt{90} = 3\sqrt{10}
     \]

Thus, the length of segment \( MC \) is \(\boxed{3\sqrt{10}}\).

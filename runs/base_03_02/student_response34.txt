\[ 5^k = 10^{k \log_{10} 5} \]

\[ 2004 \log_{10} 5 \approx 1400 \implies \log_{10} 5 \approx \frac{1400}{2004} = \frac{700}{1002} \approx 0.6980 \]

\[ 1 \leq 10^{\{k \log_{10} 5\}} < 2 \]
This is equivalent to:
\[ 0 \leq \{k \log_{10} 5\} < \log_{10} 2 \]
Since \( \log_{10} 2 \approx 0.3010 \), we need to find the proportion of \( k \) such that \( \{k \log_{10} 5\} \) falls within the interval \([0, 0.3010)\).

Because \( \log_{10} 5 \) is irrational, the sequence \( \{k \log_{10} 5\} \) is uniformly distributed over the interval \([0, 1)\). Therefore, the proportion of \( k \) for which \( \{k \log_{10} 5\} \) falls in the interval \([0, 0.3010)\) is \( 0.3010 \).

\[ 2005 \times 0.3010 = 603.505 \]
Rounding to the nearest integer, we get 604.

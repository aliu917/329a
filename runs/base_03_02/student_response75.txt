To determine the possible values of \( b \) for which the quadratic equation \( x^2 + bx + 16 \) has at least one real root, we need to analyze the discriminant of the quadratic equation. The general form of a quadratic equation is \( ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \), and its discriminant is given by \( \Delta = b^2 - 4ac \). The quadratic equation has real roots if and only if the discriminant is non-negative (\( \Delta \geq 0 \)).

For the quadratic equation \( x^2 + bx + 16 \), the coefficients are \( a = 1 \), \( b = b \), and \( c = 16 \). The discriminant is:
\[
\Delta = b^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 16 = b^2 - 64
\]
For the quadratic equation to have at least one real root, we need:
\[
b^2 - 64 \geq 0
\]
This inequality can be solved by factoring:
\[
b^2 - 64 = (b - 8)(b + 8) \geq 0
\]
To determine the intervals where this inequality holds, we find the critical points by setting the expression equal to zero:
\[
b - 8 = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad b + 8 = 0
\]
This gives us \( b = 8 \) and \( b = -8 \). These points divide the real number line into three intervals: \( (-\infty, -8) \), \( (-8, 8) \), and \( (8, \infty) \). We test a point from each interval to determine where the inequality is satisfied.

1. For \( b \in (-\infty, -8) \), choose \( b = -9 \):
\[
(-9 - 8)(-9 + 8) = (-17)(-1) = 17 \geq 0
\]
So, the inequality holds in \( (-\infty, -8) \).

2. For \( b \in (-8, 8) \), choose \( b = 0 \):
\[
(0 - 8)(0 + 8) = (-8)(8) = -64 < 0
\]
So, the inequality does not hold in \( (-8, 8) \).

3. For \( b \in (8, \infty) \), choose \( b = 9 \):
\[
(9 - 8)(9 + 8) = (1)(17) = 17 \geq 0
\]
So, the inequality holds in \( (8, \infty) \).

Since the inequality \( (b - 8)(b + 8) \geq 0 \) is satisfied at the critical points \( b = -8 \) and \( b = 8 \) as well, the solution to the inequality is:
\[
b \in (-\infty, -8] \cup [8, \infty)
\]
Therefore, the possible values of \( b \) are:
\[
\boxed{(-\infty, -8] \cup [8, \infty)}
\]

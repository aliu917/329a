To solve the problem, we need to analyze the energy stored in the system of charges before and after the movement of one charge.

### Step 1: Determine the Initial Energy Configuration
We start with three identical point charges \( q \) at the vertices of an equilateral triangle with side length \( a \).

The energy stored by any pair of charges is given by:
\[ U = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r} \]
where \( k \) is Coulomb's constant, \( q_1 \) and \( q_2 \) are the charges, and \( r \) is the distance between them.

Since the charges are identical (\( q_1 = q_2 = q \)) and the distance between each pair of charges is \( a \), the energy stored between each pair of charges is:
\[ U_{\text{pair}} = k \frac{q^2}{a} \]

There are three pairs of charges in an equilateral triangle, so the total initial energy \( U_{\text{initial}} \) is:
\[ U_{\text{initial}} = 3 \times k \frac{q^2}{a} = 15 \text{ Joules} \]

Thus,
\[ 3k \frac{q^2}{a} = 15 \]
\[ k \frac{q^2}{a} = 5 \text{ Joules} \]

### Step 2: Determine the New Energy Configuration
Now, move one charge to the midpoint of the opposite side. Let's denote the side length of the equilateral triangle as \( a \), and the new position of the moved charge is at the midpoint of the side opposite to it.

The new distances are:
- Distance between the moved charge and each of the other two charges: \( \frac{a}{2} \)
- Distance between the two remaining charges: \( a \)

The energy stored between the moved charge and each of the other two charges is:
\[ U_{\text{new, pair}} = k \frac{q^2}{\frac{a}{2}} = 2k \frac{q^2}{a} \]

There are two such pairs, so the total energy from these pairs is:
\[ 2 \times 2k \frac{q^2}{a} = 4k \frac{q^2}{a} \]

The energy stored between the two remaining charges remains the same:
\[ U_{\text{remaining pair}} = k \frac{q^2}{a} \]

Thus, the total new energy \( U_{\text{new}} \) is:
\[ U_{\text{new}} = 4k \frac{q^2}{a} + k \frac{q^2}{a} = 5k \frac{q^2}{a} \]

Using the earlier result that \( k \frac{q^2}{a} = 5 \text{ Joules} \):
\[ U_{\text{new}} = 5 \times 5 = 25 \text{ Joules} \]

### Step 3: Calculate the Increase in Energy
The increase in energy is:
\[ \Delta U = U_{\text{new}} - U_{\text{initial}} = 25 - 15 = 10 \text{ Joules} \]

Thus, the additional energy stored is:
\[ \boxed{10} \]

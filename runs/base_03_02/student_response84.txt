To solve the problem, we need to determine the side lengths of the shaded squares and then find the total shaded area. Let's go through the problem step by step.

1. **Identify the side lengths of the squares:**
   - The side length of the large square carpet is given as 9 feet.
   - We are given the ratios \(9 : \text{S} = 3\) and \(\text{S} : \text{T} = 3\).

2. **Find the side length \(\text{S}\):**
   - From the ratio \(9 : \text{S} = 3\), we can write:
     \[
     \frac{9}{\text{S}} = 3
     \]
   - Solving for \(\text{S}\):
     \[
     \text{S} = \frac{9}{3} = 3 \text{ feet}
     \]

3. **Find the side length \(\text{T}\):**
   - From the ratio \(\text{S} : \text{T} = 3\), we can write:
     \[
     \frac{\text{S}}{\text{T}} = 3
     \]
   - Solving for \(\text{T}\):
     \[
     \text{T} = \frac{\text{S}}{3} = \frac{3}{3} = 1 \text{ foot}
     \]

4. **Calculate the areas of the shaded squares:**
   - The area of one large shaded square (side length \(\text{S} = 3\)):
     \[
     \text{Area of one large square} = \text{S}^2 = 3^2 = 9 \text{ square feet}
     \]
   - The area of one small shaded square (side length \(\text{T} = 1\)):
     \[
     \text{Area of one small square} = \text{T}^2 = 1^2 = 1 \text{ square foot}
     \]

5. **Determine the total shaded area:**
   - There is 1 large shaded square and 8 small shaded squares.
   - Total area of the large shaded square:
     \[
     1 \times 9 = 9 \text{ square feet}
     \]
   - Total area of the small shaded squares:
     \[
     8 \times 1 = 8 \text{ square feet}
     \]
   - Combined total shaded area:
     \[
     9 + 8 = 17 \text{ square feet}
     \]

Thus, the total shaded area is \(\boxed{17}\) square feet.

To find the multiplicative inverse of \(201\) modulo \(299\), we need to find an integer \(x\) such that:
\[ 201x \equiv 1 \pmod{299} \]
This is equivalent to finding \(x\) such that there exists an integer \(k\) satisfying:
\[ 201x = 1 + 299k \]
or equivalently,
\[ 201x - 299k = 1 \]
This is a linear Diophantine equation, and a solution exists if and only if the greatest common divisor (gcd) of \(201\) and \(299\) is \(1\). We can use the Euclidean algorithm to find the gcd of \(201\) and \(299\).

First, we perform the Euclidean algorithm:
\[ 299 = 1 \cdot 201 + 98 \]
\[ 201 = 2 \cdot 98 + 5 \]
\[ 98 = 19 \cdot 5 + 3 \]
\[ 5 = 1 \cdot 3 + 2 \]
\[ 3 = 1 \cdot 2 + 1 \]
\[ 2 = 2 \cdot 1 + 0 \]
Since the gcd is \(1\), we can proceed to find the coefficients using the extended Euclidean algorithm.

We start from the second last equation and work backwards:
\[ 1 = 3 - 1 \cdot 2 \]
Substitute \(2 = 5 - 1 \cdot 3\):
\[ 1 = 3 - 1 \cdot (5 - 1 \cdot 3) = 2 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot 5 \]
Substitute \(3 = 98 - 19 \cdot 5\):
\[ 1 = 2 \cdot (98 - 19 \cdot 5) - 1 \cdot 5 = 2 \cdot 98 - 39 \cdot 5 \]
Substitute \(5 = 201 - 2 \cdot 98\):
\[ 1 = 2 \cdot 98 - 39 \cdot (201 - 2 \cdot 98) = 80 \cdot 98 - 39 \cdot 201 \]
Substitute \(98 = 299 - 1 \cdot 201\):
\[ 1 = 80 \cdot (299 - 1 \cdot 201) - 39 \cdot 201 = 80 \cdot 299 - 119 \cdot 201 \]
Thus, we have:
\[ -119 \cdot 201 \equiv 1 \pmod{299} \]
So, the multiplicative inverse of \(201\) modulo \(299\) is \(-119\). To express this as a positive integer between \(0\) and \(298\), we add \(299\) to \(-119\):
\[ -119 + 299 = 180 \]
Therefore, the multiplicative inverse of \(201\) modulo \(299\) is \(\boxed{180}\).

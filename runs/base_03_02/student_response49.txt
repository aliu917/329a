To determine the probability \( p \) that a brick of dimensions \( a_1 \times a_2 \times a_3 \) can be enclosed in a box of dimensions \( b_1 \times b_2 \times b_3 \) after a suitable rotation, we need to ensure that the smallest dimension of the brick is less than or equal to the smallest dimension of the box, the middle dimension of the brick is less than or equal to the middle dimension of the box, and the largest dimension of the brick is less than or equal to the largest dimension of the box. This can be expressed as \( a_1' \leq b_1' \), \( a_2' \leq b_2' \), and \( a_3' \leq b_3' \) where \( a_1', a_2', a_3' \) are the sorted values of \( a_1, a_2, a_3 \) and \( b_1', b_2', b_3' \) are the sorted values of \( b_1, b_2, b_3 \).

The probability that the sorted dimensions of the brick fit into the sorted dimensions of the box is the same as the probability that a randomly chosen permutation of \( a_1, a_2, a_3 \) is less than or equal to a randomly chosen permutation of \( b_1, b_2, b_3 \) in the sorted order. This is a classic problem in combinatorics and the answer is known to be \( \frac{1}{6} \).

To see why, consider the six possible orderings of the six numbers \( a_1, a_2, a_3, b_1, b_2, b_3 \). For any specific ordering of \( a_1, a_2, a_3 \) and \( b_1, b_2, b_3 \), there is exactly one ordering of the six numbers that satisfies the condition \( a_1' \leq b_1' \), \( a_2' \leq b_2' \), and \( a_3' \leq b_3' \). Since there are 6! = 720 possible orderings of the six numbers, and only 120 of them satisfy the condition (since there are 6/6! = 1/6 of the orderings that satisfy the condition), the probability is \( \frac{1}{6} \).

Thus, the probability \( p \) is \( \frac{1}{6} \). When written as a fraction in lowest terms, the sum of the numerator and the denominator is \( 1 + 6 = 7 \).

The final answer is \(\boxed{7}\).

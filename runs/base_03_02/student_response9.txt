To find the sum of the squares of the roots of the quadratic equation \(3x^2 + 4x - 9 = 0\), we will follow these steps:

1. **Identify the roots using Vieta's formulas:**
   For a quadratic equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), the sum and product of the roots are given by:
   \[
   \text{Sum of the roots} = -\frac{b}{a}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Product of the roots} = \frac{c}{a}
   \]
   Here, \(a = 3\), \(b = 4\), and \(c = -9\). Therefore:
   \[
   \text{Sum of the roots} = -\frac{4}{3}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Product of the roots} = \frac{-9}{3} = -3
   \]

2. **Express the sum of the squares of the roots:**
   Let the roots be \(r_1\) and \(r_2\). We need to find \(r_1^2 + r_2^2\). We can use the identity:
   \[
   r_1^2 + r_2^2 = (r_1 + r_2)^2 - 2r_1r_2
   \]

3. **Substitute the known values:**
   From Vieta's formulas, we have:
   \[
   r_1 + r_2 = -\frac{4}{3}
   \]
   \[
   r_1 r_2 = -3
   \]
   Substitute these into the identity:
   \[
   r_1^2 + r_2^2 = \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)^2 - 2(-3)
   \]

4. **Simplify the expression:**
   \[
   \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{16}{9}
   \]
   \[
   -2(-3) = 6
   \]
   \[
   r_1^2 + r_2^2 = \frac{16}{9} + 6
   \]

5. **Combine the fractions:**
   Convert 6 to a fraction with a denominator of 9:
   \[
   6 = \frac{54}{9}
   \]
   \[
   r_1^2 + r_2^2 = \frac{16}{9} + \frac{54}{9} = \frac{70}{9}
   \]

Thus, the sum of the squares of the roots is \(\boxed{\frac{70}{9}}\).

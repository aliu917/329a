To determine for how many integer values of \( n \) between 1 and 1000 inclusive the decimal representation of \(\frac{n}{1400}\) terminates, we need to understand when a fraction has a terminating decimal. A fraction in lowest terms has a terminating decimal if and only if the denominator in its simplest form has no prime factors other than 2 and 5.

First, we factorize the denominator 1400:
\[ 1400 = 2^3 \times 5^2 \times 7. \]
For \(\frac{n}{1400}\) to have a terminating decimal, the factor of 7 in the denominator must be canceled out by a factor of 7 in the numerator. Therefore, \( n \) must be a multiple of 7. We need to count the number of multiples of 7 between 1 and 1000 inclusive.

The smallest multiple of 7 is 7 and the largest is 994. The number of multiples of 7 in this range is given by:
\[ \left\lfloor \frac{1000}{7} \right\rfloor = 142. \]
So, there are 142 multiples of 7 between 1 and 1000 inclusive.

Thus, the number of integer values of \( n \) for which the decimal representation of \(\frac{n}{1400}\) terminates is \(\boxed{142}\).

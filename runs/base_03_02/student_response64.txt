To find the unit vector \(\mathbf{v}\) such that \(\mathbf{b}\) bisects the angle between \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\), we can use the angle bisector property in vector form. The vector \(\mathbf{b}\) can be expressed as a linear combination of the unit vectors in the directions of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\).

First, let's find the unit vectors in the directions of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\).

The unit vector in the direction of \(\mathbf{a}\) is:
\[
\hat{\mathbf{a}} = \frac{\mathbf{a}}{\|\mathbf{a}\|} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2 + 0^2}} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{3}{5} \\ \frac{4}{5} \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}
\]

The unit vector in the direction of \(\mathbf{b}\) is:
\[
\hat{\mathbf{b}} = \frac{\mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{b}\|} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(-1)^2 + 1^2 + (-1)^2}} \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \\ -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \end{pmatrix}
\]

Since \(\mathbf{b}\) bisects the angle between \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\), \(\mathbf{b}\) is proportional to the sum of the unit vectors in the directions of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\):
\[
\mathbf{b} = k (\hat{\mathbf{a}} + \hat{\mathbf{v}})
\]
for some scalar \(k\). We can solve for \(\hat{\mathbf{v}}\) by isolating it:
\[
\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{b}}{k} - \hat{\mathbf{a}}
\]
Since \(\mathbf{b} = k (\hat{\mathbf{a}} + \hat{\mathbf{v}})\), we can divide both sides by \(k\):
\[
\hat{\mathbf{a}} + \hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{b}}{k}
\]
Thus,
\[
\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{b}}{k} - \hat{\mathbf{a}}
\]
To find \(k\), we can use the fact that \(\hat{\mathbf{a}}\) and \(\hat{\mathbf{v}}\) are unit vectors and \(\hat{\mathbf{b}}\) is the sum of these unit vectors scaled by \(k\). Therefore, we can equate the magnitudes:
\[
\|\mathbf{b}\| = k \|\hat{\mathbf{a}} + \hat{\mathbf{v}}\|
\]
Since \(\hat{\mathbf{a}} + \hat{\mathbf{v}}\) is a vector of magnitude 1 (because \(\hat{\mathbf{a}}\) and \(\hat{\mathbf{v}}\) are unit vectors and orthogonal), we have:
\[
\|\mathbf{b}\| = k \cdot 1 \implies k = \|\mathbf{b}\| = \sqrt{3}
\]
Thus,
\[
\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{b}}{\sqrt{3}} - \hat{\mathbf{a}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} \frac{3}{5} \\ \frac{4}{5} \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} - \frac{3}{5} \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} - \frac{4}{5} \\ -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \end{pmatrix}
\]
To simplify, we find a common denominator:
\[
\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{5}{5\sqrt{3}} - \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{3}} \\ \frac{5}{5\sqrt{3}} - \frac{4\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{3}} \\ -\frac{5}{5\sqrt{3}} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{5 + 3\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{3}} \\ \frac{5 - 4\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{3}} \\ -\frac{5}{5\sqrt{3}} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{5 + 3\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{3}} \\ \frac{5 - 4\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{3}} \\ -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \end{pmatrix}
\]
Thus, the unit vector \(\mathbf{v}\) is:
\[
\mathbf{v} = \sqrt{3} \hat{\mathbf{v}} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{5 + 3\sqrt{3}}{5} \\ \frac{5 - 4\sqrt{3}}{5} \\ -\sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{5 + 3\sqrt{3}}{5} \\ \frac{5 - 4\sqrt{3}}{5} \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}
\]
The final answer is:
\[
\boxed{\begin{pmatrix} -\frac{5 + 3\sqrt{3}}{5} \\ \frac{5 - 4\sqrt{3}}{5} \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}}
\]

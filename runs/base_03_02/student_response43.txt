To solve the given system of congruences, we will address each congruence step by step and then combine the results using the Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) or other methods as necessary.

The system of congruences is:
\[
\begin{align*}
7x &\equiv 21 \pmod{14} \\
2x + 13 &\equiv 16 \pmod{9} \\
-2x + 1 &\equiv x \pmod{25}
\end{align*}
\]

### Step 1: Simplify the first congruence
\[
7x \equiv 21 \pmod{14}
\]
We can divide both sides by 7:
\[
x \equiv 3 \pmod{2}
\]
This simplifies to:
\[
x \equiv 1 \pmod{2}
\]

### Step 2: Simplify the second congruence
\[
2x + 13 \equiv 16 \pmod{9}
\]
Subtract 13 from both sides:
\[
2x \equiv 3 \pmod{9}
\]
We need the multiplicative inverse of 2 modulo 9. The inverse \( y \) satisfies:
\[
2y \equiv 1 \pmod{9}
\]
By trial, we find:
\[
2 \cdot 5 = 10 \equiv 1 \pmod{9}
\]
Thus, the inverse is 5. Multiply both sides of \( 2x \equiv 3 \pmod{9} \) by 5:
\[
x \equiv 15 \pmod{9}
\]
Simplify:
\[
x \equiv 6 \pmod{9}
\]

### Step 3: Simplify the third congruence
\[
-2x + 1 \equiv x \pmod{25}
\]
Add \( 2x \) to both sides:
\[
1 \equiv 3x \pmod{25}
\]
We need the multiplicative inverse of 3 modulo 25. The inverse \( y \) satisfies:
\[
3y \equiv 1 \pmod{25}
\]
By trial, we find:
\[
3 \cdot 17 = 51 \equiv 1 \pmod{25}
\]
Thus, the inverse is 17. Multiply both sides of \( 3x \equiv 1 \pmod{25} \) by 17:
\[
x \equiv 17 \pmod{25}
\]

### Step 4: Combine the congruences
We now have:
\[
x \equiv 1 \pmod{2}
\]
\[
x \equiv 6 \pmod{9}
\]
\[
x \equiv 17 \pmod{25}
\]

We will use the Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) to find a common solution. First, express \( x \) in terms of the second and third congruences:
\[
x = 25k + 17
\]
Substitute into the second congruence:
\[
25k + 17 \equiv 6 \pmod{9}
\]
Simplify:
\[
25 \equiv 7 \pmod{9} \quad \text{and} \quad 17 \equiv -1 \pmod{9}
\]
Thus:
\[
7k - 1 \equiv 6 \pmod{9}
\]
Add 1 to both sides:
\[
7k \equiv 7 \pmod{9}
\]
Divide by 7:
\[
k \equiv 1 \pmod{9}
\]
So:
\[
k = 9m + 1
\]
Substitute back:
\[
x = 25(9m + 1) + 17 = 225m + 42
\]
Thus:
\[
x \equiv 42 \pmod{225}
\]

Finally, we need the least positive four-digit solution:
\[
x = 225n + 42
\]
We need \( x \geq 1000 \):
\[
225n + 42 \geq 1000
\]
\[
225n \geq 958
\]
\[
n \geq \frac{958}{225} \approx 4.25
\]
So, the smallest integer \( n \) is 5:
\[
x = 225 \cdot 5 + 42 = 1125 + 42 = 1167
\]

Thus, the least positive four-digit solution is:
\[
\boxed{1167}
\]

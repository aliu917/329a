To find the area of the union of the regions enclosed by the square and the circle, we need to calculate the area of the square, the area of the circle, and then subtract the area of their overlap (if any).

1. **Calculate the area of the square:**
   The side length of the square is 10. Therefore, the area of the square is:
   \[
   \text{Area of the square} = 10 \times 10 = 100
   \]

2. **Calculate the area of the circle:**
   The radius of the circle is 10. Therefore, the area of the circle is:
   \[
   \text{Area of the circle} = \pi \times 10^2 = 100\pi
   \]

3. **Determine the overlap:**
   Since the circle is centered at one of the vertices of the square and has a radius of 10, the entire circle lies within the square. Therefore, there is no part of the circle that is outside the square, and the area of their overlap is equal to the area of the circle.

4. **Calculate the area of the union:**
   The area of the union of the square and the circle is simply the sum of the area of the square and the area of the circle, because the circle is completely within the square and there is no overlap to subtract:
   \[
   \text{Area of the union} = \text{Area of the square} + \text{Area of the circle} = 100 + 100\pi
   \]

Thus, the area of the union of the regions enclosed by the square and the circle is:
\[
\boxed{100 + 100\pi}
\]

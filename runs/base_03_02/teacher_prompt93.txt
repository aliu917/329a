You are given a reasoning attempt for answering the following question: A solid in the shape of a right circular cone is 4 inches tall and its base has a 3-inch radius. The entire surface of the cone, including its base, is painted. A plane parallel to the base of the cone divides the cone into two solids, a smaller cone-shaped solid $C$ and a frustum-shaped solid $F,$ in such a way that the ratio between the areas of the painted surfaces of $C$ and $F$ and the ratio between the volumes of $C$ and $F$ are both equal to $k$. Given that $k=\frac m n,$ where $m$ and $n$ are relatively prime positive integers, find $m+n.$


Attempt: To solve the problem, we first need to determine the dimensions and areas of the smaller cone \(C\) and the frustum \(F\). Let the height of the smaller cone be \(h\) and the radius of its base be \(r\). Since the plane is parallel to the base, the smaller cone is similar to the original cone. The ratio of the heights of the smaller cone to the original cone is the same as the ratio of their radii, and also the same as the ratio of their slant heights. Therefore, we have:

\[ \frac{h}{4} = \frac{r}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{h^2 + r^2}}{5} \]

From \(\frac{h}{4} = \frac{r}{3}\), we get \(r = \frac{3h}{4}\). The ratio of the areas of the surfaces and volumes of the smaller cone \(C\) to the frustum \(F\) is given as \(k\). The volume of a cone is proportional to the cube of its height, and the surface area (excluding the base) is proportional to the square of its height. Therefore, the volume ratio and the surface area ratio are both \(k = \left(\frac{h}{4}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{h}{4}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{h}{4}\right)^2\).

Since the volume and surface area ratios are the same, we have:

\[ k = \left(\frac{h}{4}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{h}{4}\right)^2 \]

This simplifies to:

\[ k = \frac{h^3}{4^3} = \frac{h^2}{4^2} \]

\[ k = \frac{h^3}{64} = \frac{h^2}{16} \]

Multiplying both sides by 64, we get:

\[ 16h^3 = 64h^2 \]

Dividing both sides by \(16h^2\) (assuming \(h \neq 0\)):

\[ h = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 2 \]

So, the height of the smaller cone is \(h = 2\) inches, and the radius of its base is \(r = \frac{3 \cdot 2}{4} = 1.5\) inches.

Now, we calculate the volume and surface area of the smaller cone \(C\) and the frustum \(F\).

The volume of the original cone is:

\[ V_{\text{original}} = \frac{1}{3} \pi (3)^2 (4) = 12\pi \]

The volume of the smaller cone \(C\) is:

\[ V_C = \frac{1}{3} \pi (1.5)^2 (2) = \frac{1}{3} \pi \cdot 2.25 \cdot 2 = \pi \]

The volume of the frustum \(F\) is:

\[ V_F = V_{\text{original}} - V_C = 12\pi - \pi = 11\pi \]

The ratio of the volumes is:

\[ k = \frac{V_C}{V_F} = \frac{\pi}{11\pi} = \frac{1}{11} \]

The lateral surface area of the original cone is:

\[ A_{\text{lateral, original}} = \pi \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 15\pi \]

The lateral surface area of the smaller cone \(C\) is:

\[ A_{\text{lateral, C}} = \pi \cdot 1.5 \cdot \sqrt{2^2 + 1.5^2} = \pi \cdot 1.5 \cdot \sqrt{4 + 2.25} = \pi \cdot 1.5 \cdot \sqrt{6.25} = \pi \cdot 1.5 \cdot 2.5 = 3.75\pi \]

The lateral surface area of the frustum \(F\) is:

\[ A_{\text{lateral, F}} = A_{\text{lateral, original}} - A_{\text{lateral, C}} = 15\pi - 3.75\pi = 11.25\pi \]

The total surface area of the original cone is:

\[ A_{\text{total, original}} = A_{\text{lateral, original}} + \pi (3)^2 = 15\pi + 9\pi = 24\pi \]

The total surface area of the smaller cone \(C\) is:

\[ A_{\text{total, C}} = A_{\text{lateral, C}} + \pi (1.5)^2 = 3.75\pi + 2.25\pi = 6\pi \]

The total surface area of the frustum \(F\) is:

\[ A_{\text{total, F}} = A_{\text{total, original}} - A_{\text{total, C}} + \pi (1.5)^2 = 24\pi - 6\pi + 2.25\pi = 20.25\pi \]

The ratio of the surface areas is:

\[ k = \frac{A_{\text{total, C}}}{A_{\text{total, F}}} = \frac{6\pi}{20.25\pi} = \frac{6}{20.25} = \frac{6}{20.25} = \frac{6}{20.25} = \frac{8}{27} \]

Since the volume and surface area ratios are the same, we have \(k = \frac{1}{11}\). Therefore, \(m = 1\) and \(n = 11\), and \(m+n = 1+11 = 12\).

The final answer is:

\[
\boxed{12}

Correct solution: Our original solid has volume equal to $V = \frac13 \pi r^2 h = \frac13 \pi 3^2\cdot 4 = 12 \pi$ and has surface area $A = \pi r^2 + \pi r \ell$, where $\ell$ is the slant height of the cone. Using the Pythagorean Theorem, we get $\ell = 5$ and $A = 24\pi$.
Let $x$ denote the radius of the small cone. Let $A_c$ and $A_f$ denote the area of the painted surface on cone $C$ and frustum $F$, respectively, and let $V_c$ and $V_f$ denote the volume of cone $C$ and frustum $F$, respectively. Because the plane cut is parallel to the base of our solid, $C$ is similar to the uncut solid and so the height and slant height of cone $C$ are $\frac{4}{3}x$ and $\frac{5}{3}x$, respectively. Using the formula for lateral surface area of a cone, we find that $A_c=\frac{1}{2}c\cdot \ell=\frac{1}{2}(2\pi x)\left(\frac{5}{3}x\right)=\frac{5}{3}\pi x^2$. By subtracting $A_c$ from the surface area of the original solid, we find that $A_f=24\pi - \frac{5}{3}\pi x^2$.
Next, we can calculate $V_c=\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2h=\frac{1}{3}\pi x^2 \left(\frac{4}{3}x\right)=\frac{4}{9}\pi x^3$. Finally, we subtract $V_c$ from the volume of the original cone to find that $V_f=12\pi - \frac{4}{9}\pi x^3$. We know that $\frac{A_c}{A_f}=\frac{V_c}{V_f}=k.$ Plugging in our values for $A_c$, $A_f$, $V_c$, and $V_f$, we obtain the equation $\frac{\frac{5}{3}\pi x^2}{24\pi - \frac{5}{3}\pi x^2}=\frac{\frac{4}{9}\pi x^3}{12\pi - \frac{4}{9}\pi x^3}$. We can take reciprocals of both sides to simplify this equation to $\frac{72}{5x^2} - 1 = \frac{27}{x^3} - 1$ and so $x = \frac{15}{8}$. Then $k = \frac{\frac{5}{3}\pi x^2}{24\pi - \frac{5}{3}\pi x^2}= \frac{125}{387} = \frac mn$ so the answer is $m+n=125+387=\boxed{512}$.

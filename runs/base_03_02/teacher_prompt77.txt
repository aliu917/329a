You are given a reasoning attempt for answering the following question: An oreo shop sells $5$ different flavors of oreos and $3$ different flavors of milk. Alpha and Beta decide to purhcase some oreos. Since Alpha is picky, he will not order more than 1 of the same flavor. To be just as weird, Beta will only order oreos, but she will be willing to have repeats of flavors. How many ways could they have left the store with 3 products collectively? (A possible purchase is Alpha purchases 1 box of uh-oh oreos and 1 gallon of whole milk while Beta purchases 1 bag of strawberry milkshake oreos).


Attempt: To solve the problem, we need to consider the possible ways Alpha and Beta can collectively purchase 3 products. We will break this down into cases based on the number of products each of them buys.

### Case 1: Alpha buys 0 products and Beta buys 3 products
- Beta can buy 3 oreos with repeats. The number of ways to choose 3 oreos from 5 flavors with repetition allowed is given by the "stars and bars" theorem, which is \(\binom{3+5-1}{3} = \binom{7}{3} = 35\).

### Case 2: Alpha buys 1 product and Beta buys 2 products
- Alpha can buy 1 product in 8 ways (5 flavors of oreos + 3 flavors of milk).
- Beta can buy 2 oreos with repeats. The number of ways to choose 2 oreos from 5 flavors with repetition allowed is \(\binom{2+5-1}{2} = \binom{6}{2} = 15\).
- Therefore, the number of ways for this case is \(8 \times 15 = 120\).

### Case 3: Alpha buys 2 products and Beta buys 1 product
- Alpha can buy 2 products in \(\binom{8}{2} = \binom{8}{2} = 28\) ways (choosing 2 out of 8 products without repetition).
- Beta can buy 1 oreo in 5 ways.
- Therefore, the number of ways for this case is \(28 \times 5 = 140\).

### Case 4: Alpha buys 3 products and Beta buys 0 products
- Alpha can buy 3 products in \(\binom{8}{3} = 56\) ways (choosing 3 out of 8 products without repetition).

### Summing up all the cases
The total number of ways they could have left the store with 3 products collectively is the sum of the number of ways for each case:
\[ 35 + 120 + 140 + 56 = 351 \]

Thus, the final answer is:
\[
\boxed{351}
\]

Correct solution: We use casework:
Alpha purchases $3$ items, Beta $0$. Then there are ${8\choose 3} = 56$ ways for Alpha to pick $3$ different items.
Alpha purchases $2$ items, Beta $1$. Then there are ${8\choose 2} = 28$ ways for Alpha to pick $2$ different items and there are $5$ ways for Beta to choose her oreo, with a total of $28 \cdot 5 = 140$.
Alpha purchases $1$ items, Beta $2$. Then there are $8$ ways for Alpha to pick $1$ different items. There are ${5\choose 2} = 10$ ways for Beta to choose two distinct oreos, and $5$ ways for Beta to choose two oreos of the same flavor. This totals to $8 \cdot (10+5) = 120$.
Alpha purchases $0$ items, Beta $3$. There are ${5\choose 3} = 10$ ways for Beta to choose three distinct oreos. For Beta to choose two oreos of the same flavor and another oreo, there are $5$ choices for the first and $4$ choices for the second, with a total of $20$ choices. There are $5$ ways to choose three of the same flavored oreo. This totals to $10 + 20 + 5 = 35$.
The total is $56 + 140 + 120 + 35 = \boxed{351}$.

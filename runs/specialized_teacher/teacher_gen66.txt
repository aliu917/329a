CORRECT SOLUTION STEPS:
Step 0: Define point \(X\) as the intersection of \(\overline{CP}\) and \(\overline{AB}\).
Step 1: Since \(\overline{PQ} \parallel \overline{CA}\) and \(\overline{PR} \parallel \overline{CB}\), we know that \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR\). Thus, the ratio of their areas is given by \(\frac{[\Delta PQR]}{[\Delta ABC]} = \left(\frac{PX}{CX}\right)^2\).
Step 2: Assign weights to points \(C\), \(A\), and \(B\) based on the segments \(CE\), \(EA\), \(CD\), and \(DB\). Let \(W_C = 15\).
Step 3: Calculate the weight of point \(A\): 
\[
W_A = \left(\frac{CE}{AE}\right) W_C = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 15 = 5.
\]
Step 4: Calculate the weight of point \(B\): 
\[
W_B = \left(\frac{CD}{BD}\right) W_C = \frac{2}{5} \cdot 15 = 6.
\]
Step 5: Determine the weight of point \(X\) (intersection of lines) by adding the weights of \(A\) and \(B\):
\[
W_X = W_A + W_B = 5 + 6 = 11.
\]
Step 6: Calculate the weight of point \(P\) (intersection point):
\[
W_P = W_C + W_X = 15 + 11 = 26.
\]
Step 7: Find the ratio of the segments:
\[
\frac{PX}{CX} = \frac{W_C}{W_P} = \frac{15}{26}.
\]
Step 8: Substitute the segment ratio into the area ratio:
\[
\frac{[\Delta PQR]}{[\Delta ABC]} = \left(\frac{15}{26}\right)^2 = \frac{225}{676}.
\]
Step 9: Identify \(m\) and \(n\) in the fraction \(\frac{m}{n}\), where \(m = 225\) and \(n = 676\).
Step 10: Find \(m + n = 225 + 676 = 901\).

Final answer: \(\boxed{901}\)

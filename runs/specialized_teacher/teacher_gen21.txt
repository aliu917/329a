Question: Call a three-term strictly increasing arithmetic sequence of integers special if the sum of the squares of the three terms equals the product of the middle term and the square of the common difference. Find the sum of the third terms of all special sequences.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Let the terms be \$a-b\$, \$a\$, and \$a+b\$. Then we want \$(a-b)^2+a^2+(a+b)^2=ab^2\$, or \$3a^2+2b^2=ab^2\$. Rearranging, we get \$b^2=\frac{3a^2}{a-2}\$. Simplifying further, \$b^2=3a+6+\frac{12}{a-2}\$. Looking at this second equation, since the right side must be an integer, \$a-2\$ must equal \$\pm1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12\$. Looking at the first equation, we see \$a>2\$ since \$b^2\$ is positive. This means we must test \$a=3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 14\$. After testing these, we see that only \$a=5\$ and \$a=14\$ work which give \$b=5\$ and \$b=7\$ respectively. Thus the answer is \$10+21=\boxed{031}\$.
Note: If you don't understand the simplification of \$b^2=\frac{3a^2}{a-2}\$, you can actually just use synthetic division and arrive at the same place

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Let the terms of the arithmetic sequence be represented as \$a - b\$, \$a\$, and \$a + b\$ where \$a\$ is the middle term and \$b\$ is the common difference.

Step 1: Write the equation for the sum of the squares of the three terms: \$(a-b)^2 + a^2 + (a+b)^2 = ab^2\$.

Step 2: Expand the left-hand side: \$(a-b)^2 + a^2 + (a+b)^2 = (a^2 - 2ab + b^2) + a^2 + (a^2 + 2ab + b^2) = 3a^2 + 2b^2\$.

Step 3: Set the equation from step 1 equal to the expanded left-hand side: \$3a^2 + 2b^2 = ab^2\$.

Step 4: Rearrange this equation to isolate \$b^2\$: \$b^2 = \frac{3a^2}{a - 2}\$.

Step 5: Simplify the expression for \$b^2\$ further: \$b^2 = 3a + 6 + \frac{12}{a - 2}\$.

Step 6: Analyze the condition for \$b^2\$ to be an integer: since the right side must be an integer, \$a - 2\$ must equal \$\pm 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12\$.

Step 7: Determine the possible values of \$a\$ by solving for \$a - 2 > 0\$, which means \$a > 2\$ and thus test \$a = 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 14\$.

Step 8: Test each value of \$a\$ to see which yield integer values for \$b\$:
- For \$a = 3\$: \$b^2 = \frac{3 \times 3^2}{3 - 2} = 27\$, \$b = \sqrt{27}\$ (not an integer)
- For \$a = 4\$: \$b^2 = \frac{3 \times 4^2}{4 - 2} = 24\$, \$b = \sqrt{24}\$ (not an integer)
- For \$a = 5\$: \$b^2 = \frac{3 \times 5^2}{5 - 2} = 25\$, \$b = 5\$ (integer)
- For \$a = 6\$: \$b^2 = \frac{3 \times 6^2}{6 - 2} = 27\$, \$b = \sqrt{27}\$ (not an integer)
- For \$a = 8\$: \$b^2 = \frac{3 \times 8^2}{8 - 2} = 48\$, \$b = \sqrt{48}\$ (not an integer)
- For \$a = 14\$: \$b^2 = \frac{3 \times 14^2}{14 - 2} = 84\$, \$b = 7\$ (integer)

Step 9: Collect the third terms from the valid special sequences: For \$a = 5, b = 5\$, the third term is \$a + b = 10\$. For \$a = 14, b = 7\$, the third term is \$a + b = 21\$.

Step 10: Add the third terms of all valid special sequences: \$10 + 21 = 31\$.

Final answer: \(\boxed{031}\)

### Now, apply what you have learned:

Question: Let \$x,y,\$ and \$z\$ be real numbers satisfying the system of equations
\begin{align*}
xy + 4z &= 60 \\
yz + 4x &= 60 \\
zx + 4y &= 60.
\end{align*}
Let \$S\$ be the set of possible values of \$x.\$ Find the sum of the squares of the elements of \$S.\$

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: We first subtract the second equation from the first, noting that they both equal \$60\$.
\begin{align*}
xy+4z-yz-4x&=0 \\
4(z-x)-y(z-x)&=0 \\
(z-x)(4-y)&=0
\end{align*}
Case 1: Let \$y=4\$.
The first and third equations simplify to:
\begin{align*}
x+z&=15 \\
xz&=44
\end{align*}
from which it is apparent that \$x=4\$ and \$x=11\$ are solutions.
Case 2: Let \$x=z\$.
The first and third equations simplify to:
\begin{align*}
xy+4x&=60 \\
x^2+4y&=60
\end{align*}
We subtract the following equations, yielding:
\begin{align*}
x^2+4y-xy-4x&=0 \\
x(x-4)-y(x-4)&=0 \\
(x-4)(x-y)&=0
\end{align*}
We thus have \$x=4\$ and \$x=y\$, substituting in \$x=y=z\$ and solving yields \$x=6\$ and \$x=-10\$.
Then, we just add the squares of the solutions (make sure not to double count the \$4\$), and get 4^2+11^2+(-6)^2+10^2=16+121+36+100=\boxed{273}.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Define the variables \(x\), \(y\), and \(z\) as real numbers satisfying the given system of equations.

Step 1: Subtract the second equation from the first:
\[
xy + 4z - (yz + 4x) = 0
\]

Step 2: Rearrange the equation:
\[
xy + 4z - yz - 4x = 0 \implies 4(z - x) - y(z - x) = 0
\]

Step 3: Factor the equation:
\[
(z - x)(4 - y) = 0
\]

Step 4: Case 1: Assume \(y = 4\). Substitute \(y = 4\) into the first and third equations:
\[
xy + 4z = 60 \implies 4x + 4z = 60 \implies x + z = 15
\]
\[
zx + 4y = 60 \implies zx + 16 = 60 \implies zx = 44
\]

Step 5: From the equations \(x + z = 15\) and \(xz = 44\), express \(z\) in terms of \(x\) and substitute:
\[
z = 15 - x \implies x(15 - x) = 44
\]

Step 6: Expand and rearrange to form a quadratic equation:
\[
15x - x^2 = 44 \implies x^2 - 15x + 44 = 0
\]

Step 7: Solve the quadratic equation using the quadratic formula:
\[
x = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{15^2 - 4 \cdot 44}}{2} = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{225 - 176}}{2} = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{49}}{2}
\]
\[
x = \frac{15 \pm 7}{2} = \{11, 4\}
\]

Step 8: Case 2: Assume \(x = z\). Substitute \(x = z\) into the first and third equations:
\[
xy + 4x = 60 \quad \text{and} \quad x^2 + 4y = 60
\]

Step 9: Rearranging gives:
\[
y = \frac{60 - 4x}{x} \quad \text{and} \quad 4y = 60 - x^2
\]

Step 10: Substitute for \(y\) from the first equation into the second:
\[
4\left(\frac{60 - 4x}{x}\right) = 60 - x^2
\]

Step 11: Multiply through by \(x\) to eliminate the fraction:
\[
240 - 16x = 60x - x^3
\]

Step 12: Rearrange to form a cubic equation:
\[
x^3 - 76x + 240 = 0
\]

Step 13: Check for rational roots using the Rational Root Theorem. Testing \(x = 6\):
\[
6^3 - 76 \cdot 6 + 240 = 216 - 456 + 240 = 0
\]
Thus, \(x = 6\) is a root.

Step 14: Factor the cubic polynomial:
\[
(x - 6)(x^2 + 6x - 40) = 0
\]

Step 15: Solve the quadratic \(x^2 + 6x - 40 = 0\) using the quadratic formula:
\[
x = \frac{-6 \pm \sqrt{6^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot (-40)}}{2 \cdot 1} = \frac{-6 \pm \sqrt{36 + 160}}{2} = \frac{-6 \pm \sqrt{196}}{2}
\]
\[
x = \frac{-6 \pm 14}{2} \implies x = 4 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -10
\]

Step 16: Gather all unique solutions for \(x\): \(4\), \(11\), \(6\), and \(-10\).

Step 17: Calculate the sum of the squares of the unique solutions:
\[
4^2 + 11^2 + 6^2 + (-10)^2 = 16 + 121 + 36 + 100 = 273
\]

Final answer: \(\boxed{273}\)

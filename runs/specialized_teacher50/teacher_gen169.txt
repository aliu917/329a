Question: What is the product of the  s of the  \$x^2 + 18x + 30 = 2 \sqrt{x^2 + 18x + 45}\$?

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: If we were to expand by squaring, we would get a  , which isn't always the easiest thing to deal with.

Now we can square; solving for \$y\$, we get \$y=10\$ or \$y=-6\$. The second root is extraneous since \$2\sqrt{y+15}\$ is always non-negative (and moreover, plugging in \$y=-6\$, we get \$-6=6\$, which is obviously false). Hence we have \$y=10\$ as the only solution for \$y\$. Substituting \$x^2+18x+30\$ back in for \$y\$,
<center>\$x^2+18x+30=10 \Longrightarrow x^2+18x+20=0.\$</center> Both of the roots of this equation are real, since its discriminant is \$18^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 20 = 244\$, which is positive. Thus by , the product of the real roots is simply \$\boxed{020}\$.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Substitute \$y\$ for \$x^2 + 18x + 30\$ to simplify the equation, resulting in \$y = 2\sqrt{y + 15}\$.

Step 1: Square both sides of the equation to eliminate the square root: \$y^2 = 4(y + 15)\$.

Step 2: Expand the right side: \$y^2 = 4y + 60\$.

Step 3: Rearrange the equation to standard form: \$y^2 - 4y - 60 = 0\$.

Step 6: Find the roots: \$y = \frac{4 \pm 16}{2}\$, giving \$y = 10\$ or \$y = -6\$.

Step 7: Identify that \$y = -6\$ is extraneous since it doesn't satisfy the original equation.

Step 8: Substitute \$y = 10\$ back into the equation: \$x^2 + 18x + 30 = 10\$.

Step 9: Rearrange to find the roots: \$x^2 + 18x + 20 = 0\$.

Step 10: Calculate the discriminant for \$x^2 + 18x + 20 = 0\$: \$18^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 20 = 244\$.

Step 11: Verify that the discriminant is positive, confirming two real roots.

Step 12: Calculate the product of the roots using Vieta's formulas, which states that for the equation \$ax^2 + bx + c = 0\$, the product of the roots is \$\frac{c}{a}\$: thus, the product is \$\frac{20}{1} = 20\$.

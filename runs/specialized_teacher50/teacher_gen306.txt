Question: Triangle \$ABC\$ has positive integer side lengths with \$AB=AC\$. Let \$I\$ be the intersection of the bisectors of \$\angle B\$ and \$\angle C\$. Suppose \$BI=8\$. Find the smallest possible perimeter of \$\triangle ABC\$.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Let \$D\$ be the midpoint of \$\overline{BC}\$. Then by SAS Congruence, \$\triangle ABD \cong \triangle ACD\$, so \$\angle ADB = \angle ADC = 90^o\$.
Now let \$BD=y\$, \$AB=x\$, and \$\angle IBD = \dfrac{\angle ABD}{2} = \theta\$.
Then \$\mathrm{cos}{(\theta)} = \dfrac{y}{8}\$
and \$\mathrm{cos}{(2\theta)} = \dfrac{y}{x} = 2\mathrm{cos^2}{(\theta)} - 1 = \dfrac{y^2-32}{32}\$.
Cross-multiplying yields \$32y = x(y^2-32)\$.
Since \$x,y>0\$, \$y^2-32\$ must be positive, so \$y > 5.5\$.
Additionally, since \$\triangle IBD\$ has hypotenuse \$\overline{IB}\$ of length \$8\$, \$BD=y < 8\$.
Therefore, given that \$BC=2y\$ is an integer, the only possible values for \$y\$ are \$6\$, \$6.5\$, \$7\$, and \$7.5\$.
However, only one of these values, \$y=6\$, yields an integral value for \$AB=x\$, so we conclude that \$y=6\$ and \$x=\dfrac{32(6)}{(6)^2-32}=48\$.
Thus the perimeter of \$\triangle ABC\$ must be \$2(x+y) = \boxed{108}\$.
==Solution 2 (No Trig)==
Let \$AB=x\$ and the foot of the altitude from \$A\$ to \$BC\$ be point \$E\$ and \$BE=y\$. Since ABC is isosceles, \$I\$ is on \$AE\$. By Pythagorean Theorem, \$AE=\sqrt{x^2-y^2}\$. Let \$IE=a\$ and \$IA=b\$. By Angle Bisector theorem, \$\frac{y}{a}=\frac{x}{b}\$. Also, \$a+b=\sqrt{x^2-y^2}\$. Solving for \$a\$, we get \$a=\frac{\sqrt{x^2-y^2}}{1+\frac{x}{y}}\$. Then, using Pythagorean Theorem on \$\triangle BEI\$ we have \$y^2+\left(\frac{\sqrt{x^2-y^2}}{1+\frac{x}{y}}\right)^2=8^2=64\$. Simplifying, we have \$y^2+y^2\frac{x^2-y^2}{(x+y)^2}=64\$. Factoring out the \$y^2\$, we have \$y^2\left(1+\frac{x^2-y^2}{(x+y)^2}\right)=64\$. Adding 1 to the fraction and simplifying, we have \$\frac{y^2x(x+y)}{(x+y)^2}=32\$. Crossing out the \$x+y\$, and solving for \$x\$ yields \$32y = x(y^2-32)\$. Then, we continue as Solution 1 does.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Let \( D \) be the midpoint of \( \overline{BC} \). Denote \( BD = y \) and \( AB = AC = x \). 

Step 1: By SAS Congruence, triangle \( ABD \) is congruent to triangle \( ACD \), so \( \angle ADB = \angle ADC = 90^\circ \).

Step 2: The angle \( \angle IBD \) is half of \( \angle ABD \), denoted as \( \theta \).

Step 3: Using the cosine of \( \theta \), we have \( \cos(\theta) = \frac{y}{8} \).

Step 4: Using the double angle formula for cosine, \( \cos(2\theta) = \frac{y}{x} = 2\cos^2(\theta) - 1 = \frac{y^2 - 32}{32} \).

Step 5: Cross-multiplying gives us the equation \( 32y = x(y^2 - 32) \).

Step 6: Since \( x, y > 0 \), it follows that \( y^2 - 32 \) must be positive, leading to \( y > 5.5 \).

Step 7: Given that triangle \( IBD \) has hypotenuse \( \overline{IB} \) of length \( 8 \), we also know \( BD = y < 8 \).

Step 8: Since \( BC = 2y \) must be an integer, the possible integer values for \( y \) are \( 6 \), \( 7 \), as \( 6.5 \) and \( 7.5 \) are not integers.

Step 9: Testing \( y = 6 \), we substitute into the equation \( 32y = x(y^2 - 32) \) to find \( x \):
\[
x = \frac{32(6)}{(6)^2 - 32} = \frac{192}{36 - 32} = \frac{192}{4} = 48.
\]

Step 10: Calculate the perimeter of triangle \( ABC \):
\[
\text{Perimeter} = AB + AC + BC = x + x + 2y = 2x + 2y = 2(48) + 2(6) = 96 + 12 = 108.
\]

Final answer: \( \boxed{108} \)

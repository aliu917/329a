Find \$m + n\$.

CORRECT SOLUTION STEPS:
Step 0: Let \$X\$ be the intersection of \$\overline{CP}\$ and \$\overline{AB}\$.
Step 1: Since \$\overline{PQ} \parallel \overline{CA}\$ and \$\overline{PR} \parallel \overline{CB}\$, we have that \$\angle CAB = \angle PQR\$ and \$\angle CBA = \angle PRQ\$.
Step 2: This implies that triangle \$ABC\$ is similar to triangle \$PQR\$, denoted as \$\Delta ABC \sim \Delta PQR\$.
Step 3: From the similarity of triangles, we can express the ratio of their areas: \$\frac{[\Delta PQR]}{[\Delta ABC]} = \left(\frac{PX}{CX}\right)^2\$.
Step 4: Assign a weight to point \$C\$ as \$W_C = 15\$.
Step 5: Calculate the weight of point \$A\$ using the ratio of segments on \$\overline{AC}\$: \$W_A = \left(\frac{CE}{AE}\right)W_C = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 15 = 5\$.
Step 6: Calculate the weight of point \$B\$ using the ratio of segments on \$\overline{BD}\$: \$W_B = \left(\frac{CD}{BD}\right)W_C = \frac{2}{5} \cdot 15 = 6\$.
Step 7: Find the weight at point \$X\$, which is the sum of weights at points \$A\$ and \$B\$: \$W_X = W_A + W_B = 5 + 6 = 11\$.
Step 8: Calculate the weight at point \$P\$, which includes the weights at \$C\$ and \$X\$: \$W_P = W_C + W_X = 15 + 11 = 26\$.
Step 9: Now, compute the ratio \$\frac{PX}{CX}\$ using the weights: \$\frac{PX}{CX} = \frac{W_C}{W_P} = \frac{15}{26}\$.
Step 10: Substitute this ratio into the area ratio formula: \$\frac{[\Delta PQR]}{[\Delta ABC]} = \left( \frac{15}{26} \right)^2 = \frac{225}{676}\$.
Step 11: Identify \$m = 225\$ and \$n = 676\$, which are relatively prime positive integers.
Step 12: Calculate \$m + n = 225 + 676 = 901\$.

Final answer: \(\boxed{901}\)

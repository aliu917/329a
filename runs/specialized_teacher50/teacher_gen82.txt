Question: Let \$x,y,\$ and \$z\$ be real numbers satisfying the system of equations
\begin{align*}
xy + 4z &= 60 \\
yz + 4x &= 60 \\
zx + 4y &= 60.
\end{align*}
Let \$S\$ be the set of possible values of \$x.\$ Find the sum of the squares of the elements of \$S.\$

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: We first subtract the second equation from the first, noting that they both equal \$60\$.
\begin{align*}
xy+4z-yz-4x&=0 \\
4(z-x)-y(z-x)&=0 \\
(z-x)(4-y)&=0
\end{align*}
Case 1: Let \$y=4\$.
The first and third equations simplify to:
\begin{align*}
x+z&=15 \\
xz&=44
\end{align*}
from which it is apparent that \$x=4\$ and \$x=11\$ are solutions.
Case 2: Let \$x=z\$.
The first and third equations simplify to:
\begin{align*}
xy+4x&=60 \\
x^2+4y&=60
\end{align*}
We subtract the following equations, yielding:
\begin{align*}
x^2+4y-xy-4x&=0 \\
x(x-4)-y(x-4)&=0 \\
(x-4)(x-y)&=0
\end{align*}
We thus have \$x=4\$ and \$x=y\$, substituting in \$x=y=z\$ and solving yields \$x=6\$ and \$x=-10\$.
Then, we just add the squares of the solutions (make sure not to double count the \$4\$), and get 4^2+11^2+(-6)^2+10^2=16+121+36+100=\boxed{273}.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Let \(x, y, z\) be real numbers satisfying the system of equations:  
\[
\begin{align*}
xy + 4z &= 60 \\
yz + 4x &= 60 \\
zx + 4y &= 60.
\end{align*}
\]

Step 1: Subtract the second equation from the first:  
\[
xy + 4z - (yz + 4x) = 0.
\]

Step 2: Rearranging gives:  
\[
xy - yz + 4z - 4x = 0 \implies 4(z - x) - y(z - x) = 0.
\]

Step 3: Factor the equation:  
\[
(z - x)(4 - y) = 0.
\]

Step 4: Case 1: Assume \(y = 4\). Substitute into the first and third equations:  
\[
\begin{align*}
x + z &= 15, \\
xz &= 44.
\end{align*}
\]

Step 5: Solve the system from Case 1: From \(z = 15 - x\), substitute into \(xz = 44\):  
\[
x(15 - x) = 44 \implies 15x - x^2 = 44 \implies x^2 - 15x + 44 = 0.
\]

Step 6: Solve the quadratic equation:  
\[
x = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{(15)^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 44}}{2 \cdot 1} = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{225 - 176}}{2} = \frac{15 \pm 7}{2}.
\]

Step 7: Calculate the roots:  
\[
x = \frac{22}{2} = 11 \quad \text{and} \quad x = \frac{8}{2} = 4.
\]

Step 8: Case 2: Assume \(x = z\). Substitute into the first and third equations:  
\[
\begin{align*}
xy + 4x &= 60, \\
x^2 + 4y &= 60.
\end{align*}
\]

Step 9: Subtract the equations in Case 2:  
\[
x^2 + 4y - (xy + 4x) = 0 \implies x^2 + 4y - xy - 4x = 0 \implies x(x - 4) - y(x - 4) = 0.
\]

Step 10: Factor the equation:  
\[
(x - 4)(x - y) = 0.
\]

Step 11: Solve for \(x\): Either \(x = 4\) or \(x = y\). If \(x = 4\), we already have this solution. 

Step 12: If \(x = y\), substitute into \(x = y = z\):  
\[
x^2 + 4x = 60 \implies x^2 + 4x - 60 = 0.
\]

Step 13: Solve the quadratic equation from this case:  
\[
x = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{(4)^2 + 4 \cdot 60}}{2} = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 240}}{2} = \frac{-4 \pm 16}{2}.
\]

Step 14: Calculate the roots:  
\[
x = \frac{12}{2} = 6 \quad \text{and} \quad x = \frac{-20}{2} = -10.
\]

Step 15: Collect all unique solutions:  
The possible values of \(x\) are \(4, 11, 6, -10\).

Step 16: Calculate the sum of the squares of the unique solutions:  
\[
4^2 + 11^2 + 6^2 + (-10)^2 = 16 + 121 + 36 + 100 = 273.
\]

Final answer: \(\boxed{273}\)

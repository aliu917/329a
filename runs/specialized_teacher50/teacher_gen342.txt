Alfred and Bonnie play a game in which they take turns tossing a fair coin. The winner of a game is the first person to obtain a head. Alfred and Bonnie play this game several times with the stipulation that the loser of a game goes first in the next game. Suppose that Alfred goes first in the first game, and that the probability that he wins the sixth game is \$m/n\,\$, where \$m\,\$ and \$n\,\$ are relatively prime positive integers. What are the last three digits of \$m+n\,\$?

CORRECT SOLUTION STEPS:
Step 0: Define the probability of Alfred winning when he starts a game as \$P(S)\$ and when Bonnie starts as \$P(NS)\$.
Step 1: Establish that the probability \$P(S) = \frac{2}{3}\$ (Alfred wins when starting) and \$P(NS) = \frac{1}{3}\$ (Bonnie wins when starting).
Step 2: List the games from Game 1 to Game 7, noting that Game 1 is a starter (S) and Game 7 is a non-starter (NS).
Step 3: Recognize that between the 7 games, there are 6 opportunities to switch between S and NS.
Step 4: Determine that an odd number of switches is required to go from S to NS, which can be 1, 3, or 5.
Step 5: Calculate the probability for 1 switch: There are \${6 \choose 1} = 6\$ ways to switch, so \$P = 6\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^5\left(\frac{2}{3}\right) = \frac{12}{729}\$.
Step 6: Calculate the probability for 3 switches: There are \${6 \choose 3} = 20\$ ways to switch, so \$P = 20\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^3\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3 = \frac{160}{729}\$.
Step 7: Calculate the probability for 5 switches: There are \${6 \choose 5} = 6\$ ways to switch, so \$P = 6\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^5 = \frac{192}{729}\$.
Step 8: Sum the probabilities from Steps 5, 6, and 7 to find the total probability of Alfred winning the sixth game: 
\[
\frac{12 + 160 + 192}{729} = \frac{364}{729}.
\]
Step 9: Since \$m = 364\$ and \$n = 729\$, compute \$m+n = 364 + 729 = 1093\$.
Step 10: Determine the last three digits of \$m+n\$, which is \$093\$.

Final answer: \(\boxed{093}\)

Question: Find the number of rational numbers \$r\$, \$0<r<1\$, such that when \$r\$ is written as a fraction in lowest terms, the numerator and the denominator have a sum of 1000.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: If the initial manipulation is not obvious, consider the Euclidean Algorithm. Instead of using \$\frac{n}{m}\$ as the fraction to use the Euclidean Algorithm on, we can rewrite this as \$\frac{500-x}{500+x}\$ or \gcd(500+x,500-x)=\gcd((500+x)+(500-x),500-x)=\gcd(1000,500-x). Thus, we want \$\gcd(1000,500-x)=1\$. You can either proceed as Solution \$1\$, or consider that no even numbers work, limiting us to \$250\$ choices of numbers and restricting \$x\$ to be odd. If \$x\$ is odd, \$500-x\$ is odd, so the only possible common factors \$1000\$ and \$500-x\$ can share are multiples of \$5\$. Thus, we want to avoid these. There are \$50\$ odd multiples of \$5\$ less than \$500\$, so the answer is \$250-50=\boxed{200}\$.

Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Define the variables, let \( n \) be the numerator and \( m \) be the denominator of the fraction \( \frac{n}{m} \) such that \( n + m = 1000 \).

Step 1: Rewrite the fraction in terms of \( n \) as \( \frac{n}{m} = \frac{n}{1000 - n} \).

Step 2: Recognize that for the fraction \( \frac{n}{m} \) to be in lowest terms, \( \gcd(n, m) = 1 \) must hold.

Step 3: Use the property of the gcd: \( \gcd(n, m) = \gcd(n, 1000 - n) \).

Step 4: Simplify the gcd condition: \( \gcd(n, 1000 - n) = \gcd(n, 1000) \). Thus, we need \( \gcd(n, 1000) = 1 \).

Step 5: Determine the range for \( n \): Since \( 0 < r < 1 \), we have \( 0 < n < 1000 \).

Step 6: Identify the values of \( n \) that are coprime to \( 1000 \). The prime factorization of \( 1000 \) is \( 2^3 \cdot 5^3 \).

Step 7: Use Euler's Totient Function \( \phi(1000) \) to count the integers less than \( 1000 \) that are coprime to \( 1000 \). 

Step 8: Calculate \( \phi(1000) = 1000 \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right)\left(1 - \frac{1}{5}\right) = 1000 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{5} = 400 \).

Step 9: Since \( n \) must be less than \( 500 \) (to ensure \( r < 1 \)), and \( n \) is symmetric around \( 500 \) (i.e., if \( n \) is coprime to \( 1000 \), then \( 1000 - n \) is also coprime), half of the coprime integers up to \( 1000 \) will be in the range \( 0 < n < 500 \).

Step 10: Therefore, the total number of suitable \( n \) values is \( \frac{\phi(1000)}{2} = \frac{400}{2} = 200 \).

Final answer: \(\boxed{200}\)

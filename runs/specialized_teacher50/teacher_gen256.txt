QUESTION:
Let \$\mathcal{S}\$ be the set of all perfect squares whose rightmost three digits in base \$10\$ are \$256\$. Let \$\mathcal{T}\$ be the set of all numbers of the form \$\frac{x-256}{1000}\$, where \$x\$ is in \$\mathcal{S}\$. In other words, \$\mathcal{T}\$ is the set of numbers that result when the last three digits of each number in \$\mathcal{S}\$ are truncated. Find the remainder when the tenth smallest element of \$\mathcal{T}\$ is divided by \$1000\$.

CORRECT SOLUTION STEPS:
Step 0: Define the set of perfect squares as \(\mathcal{S}\) such that the rightmost three digits are 256, and define the set \(\mathcal{T}\) where each element is of the form \(\frac{x-256}{1000}\) with \(x\) in \(\mathcal{S}\).
Step 1: For a perfect square \(s^2\) to have the last three digits as 256, we need \(s^2 - 256 = 1000n\) for some integer \(n\). This can be rewritten as \((s + 16)(s - 16) = 1000n\).
Step 2: To ensure \((s + 16)(s - 16)\) is a multiple of 1000, at least one of \(s + 16\) or \(s - 16\) must be divisible by 5. Since \(s + 16 \not\equiv s - 16 \pmod{5}\), one term must contain all the factors of 5, specifically must be a multiple of 125.
Step 3: Both \(s + 16\) and \(s - 16\) must contain at least two factors of 2 to ensure that their product is divisible by 8. Therefore, either \(s + 16\) or \(s - 16\) must be divisible by 500.
Step 4: This leads us to the conclusion that \(s\) can be expressed as \(s = 500n \pm 16\) for integers \(n\).
Step 5: Count the values of \(s\) starting from \(n = 0\) to find the tenth value. The values are \(16, 484, 984, 1484, 1984, 2484\) (which corresponds to \(n = 0\) to \(n = 5\)).
Step 6: The tenth \(s\) is obtained when \(n = 5\), yielding \(s = 500 \cdot 5 - 16 = 2484\).
Step 7: Now compute the corresponding element in \(\mathcal{T}\): 
\[
\frac{2484^2 - 256}{1000}.
\]
Step 8: Calculate \(2484^2\):
\[
2484^2 = 6176256.
\]
Step 9: Now compute:
\[
6176256 - 256 = 6176000.
\]
Step 10: Finally, divide by 1000 to find the element in \(\mathcal{T}\):
\[
\frac{6176000}{1000} = 6176.
\]
Step 11: Find the remainder when 6176 is divided by 1000:
\[
6176 \mod 1000 = 176.
\]

Final answer: \(\boxed{176}\)

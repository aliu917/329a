QUESTION:
A disk with radius \$1\$ is externally tangent to a disk with radius \$5\$. Let \$A\$ be the point where the disks are tangent, \$C\$ be the center of the smaller disk, and \$E\$ be the center of the larger disk. While the larger disk remains fixed, the smaller disk is allowed to roll along the outside of the larger disk until the smaller disk has turned through an angle of \$360^\circ\$. That is, if the center of the smaller disk has moved to the point \$D\$, and the point on the smaller disk that began at \$A\$ has now moved to point \$B\$, then \$\overline{AC}\$ is parallel to \$\overline{BD}\$. Then \$\sin^2(\angle BEA)=\tfrac{m}{n}\$, where \$m\$ and \$n\$ are relatively prime positive integers. Find \$m+n\$.

CORRECT SOLUTION STEPS:
Step 0: Let \(A\) be the point where the disks are tangent, \(C\) be the center of the smaller disk, and \(E\) be the center of the larger disk. The radius of the smaller disk is \(1\) and the radius of the larger disk is \(5\).
Step 1: Let \(F\) be the new tangency point of the two disks after the smaller disk rolls. The smaller disk rolls along the minor arc \( \overarc{AF} \) on the larger disk.
Step 2: Denote \(\alpha = \angle AEF\) in radians. The arc length of \( \overarc{AF} \) that the larger disk covers is \(5\alpha\).
Step 3: Since the smaller disk rolls without slipping, all points on the smaller disk come into contact with the larger disk, meaning \( \overarc{BF} = \overarc{AF} = 5\alpha\).
Step 4: Since \(\overline{AC} \parallel \overline{BD}\), it follows that \(\angle BDF \cong \angle FEA\), so the angles for minor arcs \( \overarc{BF} \) and \( \overarc{AF} \) are equal, indicating minor arc \( \overarc{BF} \) has an angle of \(\alpha\).
Step 5: The length of minor arc \( \overarc{BF} \) corresponding to the smaller disk, which has a radius of \(1\), is \(\alpha\). Therefore, the total rolling distance gives us the equation \(5\alpha + \alpha =\) circumference of the smaller disk.
Step 6: The circumference of the smaller disk is \(2\pi\). Thus, we have the equation \(6\alpha = 2\pi\), leading to \(\alpha = \frac{\pi}{3}\).
Step 7: To find \(\sin^2(\angle BEA)\), we construct triangle \(BDE\). Drop a perpendicular from \(D\) to line \(\overline{EA}\) at point \(X\).
Step 8: In triangle \(BDE\), we know \(DE = 6\) and since \(EX\) is half of the radius of the larger disk, \(EX = 3\). The height \(DX = 3\sqrt{3}\) is calculated from the geometry of the triangles.
Step 9: Similarly, drop a perpendicular from \(B\) to line \(\overline{EA}\) at point \(Y\). Since \( \overline{BD} \parallel \overline{EA} \), we have \(XY = BD = 1\) and \(BY = DX = 3\sqrt{3}\).
Step 10: We find \(EY = EX - XY = 3 - 1 = 2\).
Step 11: Use the Pythagorean theorem in triangle \(BEY\) to find \(BE\): \(BE = \sqrt{(EY^2 + BY^2)} = \sqrt{(2^2 + (3\sqrt{3})^2)} = \sqrt{4 + 27} = \sqrt{31}\).
Step 12: Calculate \(\sin(\angle BEA) = \frac{\sqrt{27}}{\sqrt{31}}\) and thus \(\sin^2(\angle BEA) = \frac{27}{31}\).
Step 13: Since \(m = 27\) and \(n = 31\) are relatively prime, we find \(m+n = 27 + 31\).

Final answer: \( \boxed{58} \)

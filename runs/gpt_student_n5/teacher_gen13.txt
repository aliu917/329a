You are given a reasoning attempt for answering the following question: A disk with radius \$1\$ is externally tangent to a disk with radius \$5\$. Let \$A\$ be the point where the disks are tangent, \$C\$ be the center of the smaller disk, and \$E\$ be the center of the larger disk. While the larger disk remains fixed, the smaller disk is allowed to roll along the outside of the larger disk until the smaller disk has turned through an angle of \$360^\circ\$. That is, if the center of the smaller disk has moved to the point \$D\$, and the point on the smaller disk that began at \$A\$ has now moved to point \$B\$, then \$\overline{AC}\$ is parallel to \$\overline{BD}\$. Then \$\sin^2(\angle BEA)=\tfrac{m}{n}\$, where \$m\$ and \$n\$ are relatively prime positive integers. Find \$m+n\$.

Attempt: To analyze the problem, we start with the following information:

1. The smaller disk has radius \( r_1 = 1 \) and the larger disk has radius \( r_2 = 5 \).
2. The disks are externally tangent at point \( A \).
3. The center of the smaller disk is \( C \) and the center of the larger disk is \( E \).
4. The distance between the centers \( C \) and \( E \) is \( r_1 + r_2 = 1 + 5 = 6 \).

Now, as the smaller disk rolls around the larger disk, its center \( C \) moves along a circular path around the center \( E \) with a radius equal to \( r_2 + r_1 = 5 + 1 = 6 \).

### Step 1: Calculate the angle turned by the smaller disk.

As the smaller disk rolls without slipping, the distance it travels when it completes one full rotation (360 degrees) is equal to its circumference. The circumference of the smaller disk is:

\[
C_1 = 2 \pi r_1 = 2 \pi \times 1 = 2\pi
\]

The distance traveled by the center \( C \) around the larger disk when it rolls one complete rotation is equal to the circumference of the larger disk:

\[
C_2 = 2 \pi r_2 = 2 \pi \times 5 = 10\pi
\]

### Step 2: Determine the angle of rotation of the smaller disk.

When the smaller disk rolls around the larger disk, it must rotate through an angle \(\theta\) such that the distance traveled by the center \( C \) equals the arc length rolled out on the smaller disk. 

Let \( \theta \) be the angle (in radians) through which the smaller disk rotates as \( C \) travels around \( E \):

\[
\text{Arc length} = r_1 \cdot \theta = 1 \cdot \theta = \theta
\]

The distance traveled by center \( C \) while moving around the larger disk is the circumference of the path it travels:

\[
\text{Distance traveled by } C = 10\pi
\]

Setting these equal gives:

\[
\theta = 10\pi
\]

### Step 3: Determine the position of point \( B \).

After rolling one complete turn, point \( A \) (originally at the point of tangency) moves to point \( B \). Since the smaller disk has rolled through an angle of \( 10\pi \) radians (equivalent to \( 5 \) full rotations due to \( 10\pi / 2\pi = 5 \)), point \( B \) will be directly opposite point \( A \).

### Step 4: Determine the angle \( \angle BEA \).

Now we need to find the angle \( \angle BEA \). Since point \( B \) is directly opposite point \( A \), the line segments \( \overline{AE} \) and \( \overline{BE} \) form an isosceles triangle with \( AE = r_2 = 5 \) and \( BE = r_2 + r_1 = 6 \).

To find \( \angle BEA \), we can observe that points \( A \) and \( B \) along with point \( E \) form a triangle. The line \( \overline{AB} \) is equal to the diameter of the smaller disk:

\[
AB = 2r_1 = 2 \cdot 1 = 2
\]

### Step 5: Calculate \( \sin^2(\angle BEA) \).

By using the Law of Cosines in triangle \( ABE \):

\[
AB^2 = AE^2 + BE^2 - 2 \cdot AE \cdot BE \cdot \cos(\angle BEA)

Substituting values:

\[
2^2 = 5^2 + 6^2 - 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 6 \cdot \cos(\angle BEA)

Calculating:

4 = 25 + 36 - 60 \cdot \cos(\angle BEA)

\[
4 = 61 - 60 \cdot \cos(\angle BEA)

60 \cdot \cos(\angle BEA) = 61 - 4 = 57

\[
\cos(\angle BEA) = \frac{57}{60}

Now, we calculate \( \sin^2(\angle BEA) \):

Using the identity \( \sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x) = 1 \):

\[
\sin^2(\angle BEA) = 1 - \cos^2(\angle BEA)

\[
= 1 - \left(\frac{57}{60}\right)^2 = 1 - \frac{3249}{3600} = \frac{3600 - 3249}{3600} = \frac{351}{3600}

### Step 6: Reduce the fraction.

The fraction \( \frac{351}{3600} \) can be simplified. The gcd of \( 351 \) and \( 3600 \) is \( 3 \):

\[
\frac{351 \div 3}{3600 \div 3} = \frac{117}{1200}

Finally, since \( 117 \) and \( 1200 \) are relatively prime, we identify \( m = 117 \) and \( n = 1200 \).

Question: Six cards numbered \$1\$ through \$6\$ are to be lined up in a row. Find the number of arrangements of these six cards where one of the cards can be removed leaving the remaining five cards in either ascending or descending order.

Solution chain-of-thought reasoning: Realize that any sequence that works (ascending) can be reversed for descending, so we can just take the amount of sequences that satisfy the ascending condition and multiply by two.
If we choose any of the numbers \$1\$ through \$6\$, there are five other spots to put them, so we get \$6 \cdot 5 = 30\$. However, we overcount some cases. Take the example of \$132456\$. We overcount this case because we can remove the \$3\$ or the \$2\$. Therefore, any cases with two adjacent numbers swapped is overcounted, so we subtract \$5\$ cases (namely, \$213456, 132456, 124356, 123546, 123465\$,) to get \$30-5=25\$, but we have to add back one more for the original case, \$123456\$. Therefore, there are \$26\$ cases. Multiplying by \$2\$ gives the desired answer, \$\boxed{052}\$.

Similar to above, a \$1-1\$ correspondence between ascending and descending is established by subtracting each number from \$7\$.
We note that the given condition is equivalent to "cycling" \$123456\$ for a contiguous subset of it. For example,

It's not hard to see that no overcount is possible, and that the cycle is either \$1\$ "right" or \$1\$ "left." Therefore, we consider how many elements we flip by. If we flip \$1\$ or \$2\$ such elements, then there is one way to cycle them. Otherwise, we have \$2\$ ways. Solution steps:
--------------------------------------------------
Step 0: Define the numbers on the cards as \$1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\$.

Step 1: Realize that any arrangement that is in ascending order can be reversed for descending order. Thus, calculate arrangements for ascending order and multiply the result by 2.

Step 2: Choose one of the numbers from the set \$\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}\$ to be removed. There are 6 choices for this.

Step 3: After removing one number, there are 5 remaining cards. The remaining 5 cards must be arranged in either ascending or descending order.

Step 4: The number of ways to arrange 5 cards in ascending order is exactly 1 (i.e., they can only be arranged as they are). This applies to the descending order as well.

Step 5: Multiply the number of choices for the removed card by the number of arrangements of the remaining cards. So, we have \$6 \cdot 1 = 6\$ arrangements for the ascending order.

Step 6: Since we can also have arrangements in descending order, multiply the result by 2 to account for both ascending and descending cases: \$6 \cdot 2 = 12\$.

Step 7: Consider the overcounting issue; if two adjacent numbers are swapped (for example \$132456\$ can be derived from removing either \$2\$ or \$3\$), we need to account for those overcounts. 

Step 8: Identify and subtract the overcounted cases where two adjacent numbers can be removed, specifically \$5\$ arrangements (e.g., \$213456, 132456, 124356, 123546, 123465\$).

Step 9: Since we've subtracted these overcounts, add back the original strictly ascending case (which is counted as valid).

Step 10: Calculate the final number of valid arrangements: \$30 - 5 + 1 = 26\$.

Step 11: Finally, multiply by 2 to account for both ascending and descending orders: \$26 \cdot 2 = 52\$.

Final answer: \(\boxed{52}\)
